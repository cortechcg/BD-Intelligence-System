## Introduction, Background & Conceptual Framework

# SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND

## 5.1 Context and Strategic Importance

FIPS Africa implements an enterprise-led extension model that promotes the adoption of frugal agricultural technologies among smallholder farming households by working through VBAs, agrodealers, youth agri-entrepreneurs and service providers across the value chains it promotes, expanding farmers' access to quality inputs and advisory services while creating livelihood opportunities for VBAs and youth. The durability of this model now rests on whether the entrepreneurs at its centre can run profitable, expanding, financeable businesses. Preliminary observations point to capacity gaps among VBAs, youth and women entrepreneurs in business planning, financial management, record keeping, cash flow management, costing and pricing, inventory control, marketing, customer acquisition, governance, digital business management, negotiation and credit readiness, gaps that constrain enterprise profitability, limit business expansion and reduce access to formal financing, affecting sustainability at end of project life. FIPS Africa trainers and enterprise coaches similarly require strengthened competencies in business adult learning methodologies, participatory facilitation, enterprise coaching, mentorship, business advisory services, enterprise diagnostics and performance monitoring.

## 5.2 Purpose and Objectives

The overall objective is to strengthen the technical, business, coaching and financial readiness capacities of VBAs, agrodealers and youth entrepreneurs while mentoring FIPS Africa trainers, who will then support their businesses to grow and scale. The eight specific objectives run from a comprehensive Capacity Needs Assessment among target entrepreneurs and trainers, through competency-based curricula, Training of Trainers using adult business learning methodologies, structured coaching and mentorship delivered through FIPS trainers, and assessment of enterprise progress against baseline capacity levels, to preparing entrepreneurs for engagement with financial institutions and investors, facilitating financial linkage meetings and forums with banks, SACCOs, microfinance institutions and other financiers, and providing post-training mentorship and follow-up support. The assignment runs for 12 months from contract signing, structured as a Pilot Phase of approximately 6 months followed by a Scale-Up Phase, with MEL integrated throughout and a close-out period for final reporting and dissemination.

## 5.3 Our Interpretation of the Assignment

Cortech reads this assignment as a cascade capacity-strengthening engagement, not a direct delivery contract: Specific Objective 4 places coaching and mentorship of enterprises in the hands of FIPS trainers, so our role is to equip, coach and quality-assure those trainers, including their ability to conduct enterprise visits, undertake business diagnostics and develop individualized Business Improvement Plans, rather than to coach hundreds of enterprises ourselves. An implication the documents leave unstated is that the Pilot Phase carries a tight critical path: the CNA baseline must be completed early enough to anchor the results measurement framework established at inception and to shape curricula that FIPS Africa must jointly validate before the ToT can run, and every one of those steps passes through a FIPS Africa review-and-approval gate with agreed turnaround times that must be planned for, not absorbed as slippage. A second implication is that the MEL scope rewards restraint: the tender is explicit that existing FIPS Africa MEL systems, tools and processes are to be reviewed and upgraded jointly, with new tools developed only where gaps are identified, so a from-scratch MEL architecture would contradict the co-creation and institutional-ownership requirement. Finally, because FIPS Africa mobilises stakeholders and covers participant, venue, reproduction and consultancy logistics costs, our implementation schedule is deliberately built around FIPS Africa's mobilisation calendar and contains no duplication of those responsibilities.

## 5.4 Key Evaluation Questions

The tender frames its questions through the specific objectives and the key performance indicators the MEL framework must track, and our design answers them in the client's own terms. The central questions are: what proportion of participating entrepreneurs achieve bankable business plans, proper financial records, credit-ready status, successful access to financing and linkages to financial institutions; how have enterprise-level indicators of sales, profitability, savings, business formalization and other agreed indicators of business growth and resilience moved against the CNA baseline; and to what extent have FIPS Africa trainers, field officers and enterprise coaches attained the business facilitation, coaching, mentorship and advisory competencies measured through trainer competency assessments. The final evaluation will compare baseline and endline results, assess the effectiveness and outcomes of the intervention, document lessons learned and provide practical recommendations for sustainability, institutionalization and scale-up.

## 5.6 Understanding of Success

Success is not the delivery of a training programme; it is a measurable shift in the financing readiness and business performance of VBAs, agrodealers and youth and women entrepreneurs, verified through the baseline-to-endline comparison and the agreed KPIs on bankable business plans, financial records, credit-ready status and access to financing. # SECTION 2: CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK

The tender does not prescribe a single analytical framework, but its logic is unmistakably that of a capacity-development results chain, and Cortech will formalise it as such. Our anchor is a theory of change co-developed with FIPS Africa at inception and expressed through the co-created results measurement framework: strengthened trainer competencies (through ToT and structured coaching) lead to improved enterprise practices (through trainer-led enterprise visits, business diagnostics and individualized Business Improvement Plans), which lead to improved enterprise performance (sales, profitability, savings, business formalization) and financing readiness (bankable business plans, proper financial records, credit-ready status), which in turn produce successful access to financing and durable linkages to banks, SACCOs, MFIs, government enterprise funds and impact investors. Each link in this chain corresponds to input, output, outcome and impact indicators with baselines drawn from the CNA, exactly as the MEL scope requires.

Three complementary lenses operationalise this chain. First, a competency-based capacity development lens: the CNA will define observable competency standards for both entrepreneurs and FIPS Africa trainers across business management, financial literacy, marketing, operations, leadership and governance, digital business skills, climate-smart business practices and financing readiness, so that every curriculum module, pre- and post-training assessment and trainer competency assessment measures against the same standards. Second, an adult learning lens grounded in the ILO Start and Improve Your Business (SIYB) methodology and participatory facilitation, appropriate for youth and low-literacy audiences, ensuring the Facilitator's Manual and toolkits are field-ready rather than classroom artefacts. Third, a cascade fidelity lens: because outcomes are delivered through FIPS trainers rather than directly by the consultant, the framework explicitly measures transfer at each tier, from ToT competency assessments, to coaching and mentorship reports on trainer performance, to enterprise performance scorecards, so that weak links in the cascade are visible and correctable during periodic reflection and learning sessions.

This framework fits the assignment because it converts the tender's eight specific objectives into one testable results logic, respects the co-creation and institutional-ownership principle by building on FIPS Africa's existing results measurement systems, and gives the final evaluation a clean baseline-to-endline comparison against which effectiveness, lessons learned and recommendations for sustainability, institutionalization and scale-up can be credibly stated.

## Technical Methodology

PROPOSED APPROACH AND METHODOLOGY

1. Overall Approach

Cortech Consulting Group proposes a cascade, co-creation and evidence-driven approach built around the architecture FIPS Africa has already established: an enterprise-led extension model in which VBAs, agrodealers and youth agri-entrepreneurs deliver frugal agricultural technologies to smallholder farming households, supported by FIPS Africa trainers, field officers and enterprise coaches. Our design responds directly to the eight specific objectives and to the capacity gaps FIPS Africa has already observed in business planning, financial management, record keeping, cash flow management, costing and pricing, inventory control, marketing, customer acquisition, governance, digital business management, negotiation and credit readiness. Critically, we honour the cascade logic of Specific Objective 4: structured coaching and mentorship reaches enterprises through FIPS trainers, whom we train, coach and mentor, rather than through direct consultant delivery at scale. This is what makes the results sustainable at end of project life.

Three design principles anchor the methodology. First, the Capacity Needs Assessment (CNA) baseline drives everything downstream: curriculum content, trainer competency benchmarks, the results measurement framework indicators and the final baseline-versus-endline evaluation. Second, co-creation and institutional ownership govern the MEL workstream: we will jointly review and upgrade FIPS Africa's existing MEL systems, tools and processes, developing new tools only where gaps are identified, exactly as the RFP requires. Third, the assignment does not end at training delivery. The 12-month arc runs from the Pilot Phase (approximately 6 months) through the Scale-Up Phase to financial linkages with banks, SACCOs, MFIs, government enterprise funds and impact investors, followed by post-financing mentorship and a close-out period for final reporting and dissemination. Cortech brings directly comparable evidence: the Agritech landscape study for Heifer International Kenya under the AYuTe Africa Challenge (2025) analysing Kenya's youth agri-enterprise ecosystem, curriculum development and participatory training for AECF's IIW-SS investees in South Sudan's agribusiness sector (2024), and multi-method research and learning for the Danish Refugee Council's Pathway to Prosperity Programme in Turkana West (2025).

2. Phase-by-Phase Breakdown

Phase 1: Inception (Month 1). The team lead, an investment readiness / SME finance specialist, convenes stakeholder consultations with FIPS Africa programme leadership, trainers, field officers and enterprise coaches. The team conducts a desk review of programme documents, trainer records and enterprise records that FIPS Africa will facilitate access to. In the same window, our business development services / M&E specialist co-facilitates a joint working session with FIPS Africa MEL staff to map existing results measurement systems and agree the results chain. Output: an Inception Report containing the refined methodology, detailed 12-month work plan, stakeholder engagement strategy, data collection plan, risk management framework and deliverables and reporting schedule, submitted for FIPS Africa approval within agreed turnaround times before fieldwork begins.

Phase 2: Pilot Phase fieldwork and delivery (Months 2 to 6). The CNA is fielded in Month 2 using KoboToolbox-scripted enterprise diagnostic surveys with VBAs, agrodealers and youth and women entrepreneurs, complemented by key informant interviews and focus group discussions, and a separate trainer competency self-assessment and observation instrument for FIPS Africa trainers, field officers and enterprise coaches. Months 3 to 4 cover the development and joint validation of the competency-based learning resources: Training Curriculum, Facilitator's Manual, Coaching Toolkit, Mentorship Toolkit, Financing Readiness Toolkit and Monitoring & Evaluation Toolkit, all designed for youth and low-literacy audiences using ILO Start and Improve Your Business (SIYB) principles led by our certified SIYB trainer (or equivalent). Months 4 to 5 deliver the Training of Trainers (ToT) programme with pre- and post-training assessments and individual competency assessments. Months 5 to 6 launch trainer-led enterprise visits, business diagnostics and the first individualized Business Improvement Plans (BIPs), with our agribusiness and value chain development expert shadowing trainers in the field.

Phase 3: Scale-Up Phase (Months 7 to 11). Trainers extend coaching and mentorship across the full participating enterprise portfolio, supported by our structured coaching supervision. In parallel, the team applies the Enterprise Financing Readiness Assessment Template, provides hands-on support to strengthen financial statements, cash-flow forecasts, budgets, business records and statutory and regulatory compliance, and supports preparation of bankable business plans, investment plans and pitch decks. Credit readiness sessions with mock loan interviews and investment pitch simulations run in Months 8 to 9, followed by financial literacy and investor/bank engagement forums in Months 9 to 10 and direct financial linkages with post-financing mentorship in Months 10 to 11.

Phase 4: Analysis, endline and close-out (Months 11 to 12). The endline re-administers the CNA instruments and enterprise performance scorecards; SPSS is used for baseline-endline quantitative comparison and NVivo for coding qualitative coaching, forum and reflection-session data. The final evaluation, handover package and dissemination event close the assignment.

4. Data Quality Assurance

Quality is procedural, not asserted. All instruments are scripted in KoboToolbox with skip logic, range checks and mandatory fields; enumerators and trainers receive two days of tool-specific training with piloting before deployment. The M&E specialist runs daily data review during CNA and endline fieldwork, flagging outliers and incomplete records for same-day callback. A minimum of ten percent of enterprise diagnostics is back-checked through supervisor re-visits or telephone verification. Qualitative transcripts are double-coded in NVivo with an agreed codebook, and findings are triangulated across survey data, trainer records, enterprise records and forum feedback. All deliverables pass internal peer review by a director not involved in drafting before submission to FIPS Africa's review and approval gates.

Cortech operates under established child safeguarding and PSEA policies, and all field staff sign the code of conduct before deployment. Coaching, forums and financing linkages are facilitated to avoid conflicts of interest, with the Signed Conflict of Interest declaration submitted as required and no team member holding any interest in participating financial institutions. Data collected belongs to FIPS Africa and is transferred in full as part of the Deliverable 11 handover package.

## Sampling Strategy & Data Analysis Plan

SECTION 1: DATA COLLECTION APPROACH AND SELECTION LOGIC

This assignment does not require a probability sample. The Capacity Needs Assessment (CNA), the trainer competency assessments and the baseline-to-endline comparison are census-based and purposive exercises rather than population estimates: all FIPS Africa trainers, field officers and enterprise coaches participating in the ToT will be assessed individually, and all enterprises enrolled in the coaching and financing readiness pathway will complete the Enterprise Financing Readiness Assessment Template and enterprise performance scorecards, so no sampling error applies to these instruments. For the qualitative component, KII and FGD participants will be selected purposively in consultation with FIPS Africa during inception, using beneficiary and enterprise records that FIPS Africa will make available. Selection strata will cover entrepreneur category (VBAs, agrodealers, youth agri-entrepreneurs), gender with deliberate inclusion of women entrepreneurs, value chain, enterprise maturity and location, alongside KIIs with FIPS Africa trainers, enterprise coaches, and representatives of banks, SACCOs, MFIs, government enterprise funds and impact investors. FGDs will be gender-segregated where appropriate, tools will be adapted for low-literacy audiences, and the selection criteria, participant lists and any deviations will be documented in the Inception Report and agreed with FIPS Africa before fieldwork begins.

SECTION 2: DATA ANALYSIS PLAN

Quantitative data from the CNA questionnaire, pre- and post-training assessments, trainer competency assessment forms, enterprise performance scorecards and financing readiness assessments will be collected digitally on KoboToolbox with GPS tagging and metadata capture, then cleaned, coded and exported to SPSS, with Excel used to support data organization. Analysis will proceed in two layers. Descriptive statistics (frequencies, means, cross-tabulations) will profile competency gaps by entrepreneur category, gender, value chain and location at CNA baseline, establishing the baseline values that feed directly into the co-created results measurement framework. Inferential statistics will then test change over the assignment period: paired t-tests will compare pre- and post-training scores for each ToT competency domain, chi-square tests will assess shifts in categorical KPIs such as the proportion of entrepreneurs with bankable business plans, proper financial records and credit-ready status, and correlation analysis will explore relationships between coaching intensity (number of trainer enterprise visits and BIP milestones completed) and enterprise-level outcomes including sales, profitability, savings and business formalization. The same syntax files will be re-run at endline so the final evaluation compares baseline and endline results on identical definitions.

Qualitative data from KIIs, FGDs, coaching and mentorship reports and reflection and learning session records will be transcribed, translated where necessary and coded in NVivo using a hybrid framework: a deductive codebook structured around the CNA domains (business planning, financial management, record keeping, cash flow management, costing and pricing, inventory control, marketing, digital business skills, governance and credit readiness) plus trainer facilitation and coaching competencies, with inductive codes added for emergent themes such as barriers to engaging SACCOs and MFIs.

Triangulation will be built into the analysis rather than appended to it. Convergence matrices will place each quantitative finding alongside the corresponding qualitative evidence, so that, for example, a scorecard-measured rise in record keeping is tested against what trainers report from business diagnostics and what entrepreneurs describe in FGDs. Divergent findings will be flagged and interrogated in the periodic reflection and learning sessions with FIPS Africa, ensuring interpretation is jointly owned and that adaptations to the Pilot Phase approach are evidence-based before the Scale-Up Phase.

## Quality Assurance & Ethical Considerations

SECTION 1. QUALITY ASSURANCE FRAMEWORK

Quality assurance for this assignment is built around the approval gates FIPS Africa has defined: an approved Inception Report, learning materials validated and finalised jointly with FIPS Africa, a co-created results measurement framework, and review and approval of key deliverables within agreed turnaround times. Cortech, an ISO certified firm, applies a three-tier review to every deliverable: preparation by the responsible specialist, internal peer review by a second senior team member not involved in drafting, and sign-off by the Team Lead before submission to FIPS Africa.

During the Inception phase, the refined methodology, 12-month work plan, data collection plan and risk management framework are peer reviewed internally and then tabled with FIPS Africa staff for joint review, so the Inception Report enters the approval process already aligned with FIPS Africa's existing MEL systems, tools and processes. For the Capacity Needs Assessment, data quality is controlled at source: CNA instruments are scripted in KoboToolbox with skip logic, range constraints and mandatory fields; enumerators are trained and pilot-tested before deployment; supervisors run daily data checks for completeness, duplication and outliers; and quantitative data are cleaned and analysed in SPSS with qualitative material coded in NVivo, giving the CNA report and the baseline that feeds the results measurement framework a documented audit trail.

In the Pilot Phase, the Training Curriculum, Facilitator's Manual, Coaching Toolkit, Mentorship Toolkit and Financing Readiness Toolkit pass internal peer review and are then validated in a joint workshop with FIPS Africa before any ToT delivery. ToT quality is verified through pre- and post-training assessments and trainer competency assessments, with results reviewed against CNA benchmarks. During the Scale-Up Phase, coaching reports, Business Improvement Plans, enterprise performance scorecards and financing readiness assessments are spot-checked monthly by the MEL Specialist.

Client feedback loops run throughout: periodic reflection and learning sessions with FIPS Africa review emerging findings, interrogate challenges and agree adaptations, and all FIPS Africa review comments on deliverables are logged in a comments matrix showing how each was addressed before resubmission.

SECTION 2. ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS AND SAFEGUARDING

This assignment involves collecting personal, business and financial information from VBAs, agrodealers, youth agri-entrepreneurs and women entrepreneurs, including low-literacy audiences within smallholder farming households, as well as competency data on FIPS Africa trainers, field officers and enterprise coaches. Cortech will conduct all activities in line with its ISO certified quality management system, its child safeguarding policy and its PSEA compliant policy framework, and with FIPS Africa's own codes of conduct and safeguarding requirements.

Participation in the CNA, pre- and post-training assessments, coaching visits, financing readiness diagnostics and the final baseline-to-endline evaluation will be voluntary. Respondents will receive a clear explanation of purpose, duration, confidentiality provisions and intended use of data before consenting. Given the presence of low-literacy audiences, consent will be obtained in written or verbal form as contextually appropriate, administered in Swahili or local languages where needed. Trainer competency assessments will be handled confidentially, with individual results shared only for coaching and development purposes as agreed with FIPS Africa.

Data protection is treated with particular care because the assignment handles sensitive enterprise information, including financial statements, cash-flow forecasts, business records, credit readiness data and financing documentation packages. All data will be collected on encrypted KoboToolbox platforms, stored on access-controlled servers, anonymised in analysis and reporting, and handed over to FIPS Africa at close-out in line with the handover package, after which Cortech copies will be securely deleted on an agreed schedule. All team members will sign confidentiality agreements and Cortech's Code of Conduct before fieldwork, and the firm will submit the Signed Conflict of Interest declaration the tender requires with the financial proposal.

The tender documents do not require formal ethical clearance; Cortech will nevertheless comply with applicable Kenyan research and data protection requirements, including statutory permits where relevant. Do-no-harm principles will govern financing linkage activities in particular: mock loan interviews, investment pitch simulations and investor/bank forums will be structured so entrepreneurs are never pressured into borrowing, and post-financing mentorship will monitor for over-indebtedness risks.

## Risk Management

## Risk Management and Mitigation Plan

Cortech Consulting Group recognizes that a 12-month, two-phase assignment built on a cascade model, where enterprise coaching is delivered through FIPS Africa trainers rather than directly by the consultant, carries operational, methodological and stakeholder risks that require proactive planning. The register below prioritizes the risks most material to this assignment's geography, respondent groups and sequencing.

| Risk Category | Description | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Field access and mobilisation | VBAs, agrodealers and youth agri-entrepreneurs are dispersed across rural Kenyan operating areas; seasonal farm labour peaks (planting, harvest) may depress turnout for the CNA and ToT sessions. | Medium | High | Schedule fieldwork and forums around the agricultural calendar during inception; coordinate mobilisation timelines with FIPS Africa, which is responsible for convening participants; cluster enterprise visits by locality to reduce travel burden. |
| Data quality | Self-reported financial data from entrepreneurs with weak record keeping and low-literacy respondents may distort the CNA baseline and enterprise performance scorecards. | High | High | Triangulate self-reports against FIPS Africa enterprise records; deploy KoboToolbox instruments with built-in validation and Swahili administration; use pictorial and simplified diagnostic tools tested during piloting; daily data review by the M&E specialist. |
| Approval gates and sequencing | The Inception Report requires approval and learning materials must be validated jointly with FIPS Africa before ToT; slow turnaround could compress the Pilot Phase. | Medium | High | Agree deliverable review turnaround times at inception; submit materials in staged batches rather than a single package; hold structured validation workshops with fixed sign-off dates in the month-by-month work plan. |
| Cascade model integrity | Turnover or limited availability of FIPS Africa trainers, field officers and enterprise coaches would weaken delivery of BIPs and enterprise visits during the Scale-Up Phase. | Medium | High | Train a broader trainer cohort than the minimum required; document all coaching protocols in the Coaching Toolkit and Mentorship Toolkit for rapid onboarding of replacements; track trainer competency assessments to identify reinforcement needs early. |
| Financial institution engagement | Banks, SACCOs, MFIs and impact investors may show limited appetite at linkage forums, undermining credit-ready status and access-to-financing KPIs. | Medium | High | Engage financiers bilaterally before forums to pre-match products to enterprise profiles; ensure enterprises arrive with complete financing documentation packages and rehearsed pitches from mock loan interviews; include government enterprise funds to widen options. |
| Participant attrition | Entrepreneurs may drop out between the CNA baseline and endline, weakening the baseline-endline comparison in the final evaluation. | Medium | Medium | Maintain a participant tracking database from inception; over-sample at baseline; use trainer enterprise visits as retention touchpoints; document attrition reasons for the learning sessions. |
| MEL ownership | New MEL tools imposed on FIPS Africa staff risk duplication of existing systems and abandonment after handover. | Medium | Medium | Undertake a joint review of existing FIPS Africa MEL tools before designing anything new; upgrade rather than replace where feasible; deliver practical MEL coaching to staff throughout, not only at close-out. |
| Team availability | Loss of a key specialist mid-assignment, particularly the team lead, would disrupt continuity across both phases. | Low | High | Assign named alternates within the Cortech network with equivalent qualifications; maintain shared documentation so any deliverable can be picked up without loss of institutional memory; stagger specialist inputs across the work plan. |

Emerging risks, corrective actions and any material schedule implications will be tabled with the FIPS Africa focal point through agreed communication channels, and formally interrogated during the periodic reflection and learning sessions so that mitigation decisions are made jointly and recorded against the results measurement framework.

Risk ownership will be assigned to named team members: the team lead holds contextual and stakeholder risks, the M&E specialist holds data quality and evaluation design risks, and the SIYB-certified trainer holds ToT and cascade delivery risks. Because FIPS Africa carries responsibility for participant mobilisation, venues and consultancy logistics, the register will explicitly track interface risks at that division of labour, with escalation triggers agreed at inception, ensuring that neither party's dependencies become silent points of failure during the 12-month implementation period.
