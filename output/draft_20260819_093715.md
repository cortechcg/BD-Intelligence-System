## Introduction And Framework

# 5. INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND

## 5.1 Context and Strategic Importance

The ILO's PROSPECTS Partnership Programme, funded by the Government of the Netherlands, represents a multi-country effort to strengthen forced displacement responses through decent work, employment, and inclusive labour market systems. In Kenya, PROSPECTS interventions in Garissa and Turkana Counties have targeted refugee and host community populations with livelihoods, skills development, and enterprise support services in some of the most economically constrained and climate-stressed environments in the country. A baseline tracer study at this stage establishes the empirical foundation against which future outcome and impact claims regarding employment, income, and self-reliance will be measured. Without a methodologically sound baseline capturing pre-intervention conditions across both refugee and host community strata, the programme risks an evidentiary gap that undermines its ability to demonstrate contribution to decent work outcomes to the donor and tripartite constituents.

## 5.2 Purpose and Objectives

The assignment's core purpose is to establish a rigorous baseline of labour market status, livelihood conditions, and social inclusion indicators among PROSPECTS beneficiaries in Garissa and Turkana Counties, tracked through a combination of quantitative surveys and qualitative Focus Group Discussions. Specific objectives include generating a statistically valid dataset of at least 616 completed beneficiary surveys, capturing narrative depth through 10-12 FGDs disaggregated by relevant demographic and status categories, and producing a clean, well-documented dataset structured to support a comparable endline tracer study. A secondary objective is to test and refine the tracking and attrition management protocols that will govern beneficiary follow-up throughout the programme's remaining lifecycle.

## 5.3 Our Interpretation of the Assignment

Cortech interprets this assignment as fundamentally a beneficiary-tracking exercise operating under conditions of population mobility and communication constraint typical of displacement-affected counties, rather than a conventional cross-sectional survey. This distinction shapes methodological priorities toward locating, verifying, and re-establishing contact with a defined beneficiary list drawn from PROSPECTS programme records, rather than fresh sample construction. We further interpret the ToR's emphasis on electronic data collection and real-time monitoring as a requirement for continuous quality assurance throughout fieldwork, not merely a data entry preference, and have structured our approach accordingly.

## 5.4 Key Evaluation Questions

The baseline is organized around questions concerning beneficiaries' current employment and livelihood status, access to financial services, engagement with skills development or entrepreneurship support, and perceived social cohesion between refugee and host community members prior to full programme exposure. Additional questions probe disaggregation by gender, disability status, and displacement status to establish subgroup baselines necessary for equity-sensitive endline comparison. These questions directly operationalize the PROSPECTS results framework's employment, self-reliance, and social cohesion outcome areas.

## 5.5 Deliverables

Cortech will produce five deliverables aligned to the ToR schedule: an Inception Report (18 September 2026) detailing methodology, translated instruments, sampling frame, and fieldwork plan; a Fieldwork Report (7 October 2026) summarizing data collection performance and quality; and, by 16 October 2026, the Raw and Cleaned Quantitative Dataset, Qualitative Transcripts, and consolidated Study Datasets for Baseline. Each deliverable will be submitted in the format and completeness standard specified by ILO PROSPECTS, with cleaning logs and metadata accompanying all datasets.

## 5.6 Understanding of Success

Success on this assignment is defined not solely by achieving the minimum survey count but by the integrity of the beneficiary tracking mechanism established for future endline comparability, the completeness and usability of the qualitative record, and the timeliness of reporting that allows the PROSPECTS team to act on data quality findings before endline design begins. Cortech understands that a baseline with weak tracking protocols or undocumented attrition decisions would compromise the credibility of the entire tracer study design, and treats protocol rigor as the primary success criterion.

## Methodology

# PROPOSED APPROACH AND METHODOLOGY

## 1. Overall Approach

Cortech Consulting Group proposes a mixed-methods, longitudinal tracer design to establish baseline conditions for PROSPECTS beneficiaries in Garissa and Turkana counties. The design combines a structured quantitative survey (minimum 616 valid responses) with qualitative Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) to capture both the measurable indicators of livelihoods, employment, and self-reliance, and the contextual and behavioral dimensions — social cohesion, financial access barriers, protection concerns — that quantitative instruments alone cannot surface. This dual-track structure allows the study to serve as a genuine baseline against which subsequent tracer rounds can measure change with statistical and narrative rigor.

The rationale for this design rests on the specific challenges of tracer studies with refugee and host-community populations: high population mobility, phone-number turnover, language diversity, and sensitivity around displacement and livelihood status. A single-method approach risks either statistical noise (from a purely quantitative survey unable to explain attrition or contextual anomalies) or insufficient generalizability (from qualitative work alone). Triangulating survey data, FGD transcripts, and secondary programme records allows Cortech to cross-validate findings, identify outliers, and produce a dataset that ILO PROSPECTS and partner agencies can trust for downstream comparison.

Sampling will use the cleaned PROSPECTS beneficiary database as the sampling frame, applying stratified random sampling by county, beneficiary category (refugee/host community), and intervention type to ensure proportional representation. A replacement protocol (oversampling by 15–20%) will be built into the sample design to manage anticipated attrition from phone unreachability or relocation, a known risk in Garissa and Turkana contexts.

## 2. Phase-by-Phase Breakdown

**Phase 1 — Inception (Weeks 1–2):** Desk review of PROSPECTS programme documents, prior baseline/endline tools, and beneficiary databases; refinement of sampling frame; translation and adaptation of survey and FGD instruments into Swahili and relevant local languages; instrument piloting with a small non-sampled group in each county; enumerator and FGD facilitator recruitment and training (including PSEA and child safeguarding briefing). Output: Inception Report with finalized methodology, tools, and fieldwork plan.

**Phase 2 — Field Data Collection (Weeks 3–6):** Deployment of trained enumerators using KoboToolbox on tablets/smartphones for both phone-based and in-person interviews, depending on beneficiary reachability. Parallel scheduling of 10–12 FGDs, purposively sampled to capture variation by gender, age, livelihood category, and location. Key informant interviews (KIIs) with programme staff, local authorities, and financial service providers will supplement the FGDs to contextualize labour market and social cohesion findings. Daily supervisor back-checks (minimum 10% of completed surveys) and real-time KoboToolbox dashboard monitoring will track completion rates, response quality, and attrition patterns throughout fieldwork.

**Phase 3 — Data Processing and Analysis (Weeks 6–8):** Quantitative data exported from KoboToolbox, cleaned, and coded for analysis in SPSS, generating descriptive and inferential statistics disaggregated by sex, age, location, and beneficiary category. FGD and KII audio recordings will be transcribed verbatim into English and coded thematically in NVivo, using a codebook aligned to PROSPECTS outcome areas (employment, skills, financial inclusion, social cohesion). Where geographic patterns are relevant — e.g., proximity to markets or service points — QGIS will be used to map beneficiary distribution against infrastructure and labour market access points.

**Phase 4 — Reporting (Weeks 8–10):** Findings triangulated across survey, FGD/KII, and spatial data streams to produce the Fieldwork Report, cleaned datasets, and full qualitative transcripts, structured against ILO PROSPECTS baseline indicators.

## SECTION 1: SAMPLING STRATEGY

**Sample Size and Confidence Parameters**

The tracer study will employ a **minimum target of 616 completed surveys**, derived from the PROSPECTS beneficiary database across Garissa and Turkana Counties. This sample size reflects a 95% confidence level and 4% margin of error for the primary beneficiary population, assuming a 15% anticipated attrition rate due to migration, contact loss, and non-response in refugee and pastoral contexts.

The sampling framework stratifies beneficiaries by:
- **County** (Garissa / Turkana / Nairobi)
- **Cohort** (programme entry year; essential for tracer validity)
- **Primary outcome category** (employment status, enterprise activity, skills certification)
- **Gender** (to ensure adequate female representation, minimum 40%)
- **Displacement status** (refugee / host community / IDP, where applicable)

**Sampling Methodology**

Quantitative data collection employs **stratified random sampling** from the cleaned beneficiary database provided by ILO PROSPECTS. Within each stratum, systematic random sampling ensures representativeness while reducing selection bias. This approach is feasible given the finite, known population frame and allows comparison of outcomes across programme cohorts.

For **Focus Group Discussions (10–12 groups)**, we apply **purposive sampling** to recruit participants representing diverse outcomes: individuals successfully employed; those operating micro-enterprises; participants who exited the programme without employment gains; and host community members engaged in the programme. FGD composition prioritizes geographic, gender, and outcome diversity rather than statistical representativeness.

**Attrition and Replacement Protocol**

We anticipate 15–20% contact failure (phone disconnection, relocation, deceased). The protocol includes:
- **First attempt:** Electronic survey via Kobo (SMS invitation + phone interview)
- **Second attempt:** Field visit (3–5 days post-initial contact)
- **Third attempt:** Proxy respondent (household member or peer referral)
- **Replacement:** Random selection from reserve sample (10% buffer maintained)

All attrition is documented with reasons (unreachable, deceased, refused, language barrier) to enable bias assessment in analysis. Back-checks (5% of completed surveys) validate data integrity and enumerator performance.

## SECTION 2: DATA ANALYSIS PLAN

**Quantitative Analysis Framework**

Quantitative data collected via Kobo Toolbox undergoes iterative cleaning in Excel, followed by analysis in **SPSS v.27**. The analysis employs a **mixed descriptive-inferential approach**:

*Descriptive analysis* generates frequency distributions and cross-tabulations for primary outcomes (employment status, income, enterprise activity, skill certifications) disaggregated by county, cohort, gender, and displacement status. Measures of central tendency and dispersion profile beneficiary livelihoods trajectories.

*Inferential analysis* applies chi-square tests (categorical outcome comparisons across cohorts/gender) and t-tests (income and asset differences) to assess statistical significance. Logistic regression models programme participation against employment outcomes, controlling for demographic and baseline socioeconomic variables. Attrition weighting adjusts for systematic non-response bias (e.g., if programme exiters are underrepresented in phone surveys).

**Qualitative Analysis Framework**

FGD transcripts (English translations, source language originals retained) are coded in **NVivo 14** using a hybrid deductive-inductive approach. The deductive codebook reflects PROSPECTS' theory of change (skills → employment → self-reliance → social cohesion); inductive codes capture emergent themes (barriers to employment, informal economy dynamics, community acceptance of refugees, gender norms affecting women's participation).

Thematic analysis identifies patterns within outcome categories (successful employment, enterprise failure, host community friction) and across geographies. Memo-writing during coding preserves analytical interpretation. A secondary analysis examines programme experience variance by gender and displacement status, with attention to minority perspectives.

# SECTION 1: QUALITY ASSURANCE FRAMEWORK

Cortech will implement a multi-layered quality assurance system to ensure data accuracy, consistency, and usability throughout the tracer study. This approach integrates real-time monitoring, structured peer review, and staged deliverable validation.

**Real-Time Data Quality Monitoring**

All quantitative data collected via Kobo Toolbox will be monitored daily through automated constraint checks and skip logic validation. The Data Management Specialist will generate real-time dashboards tracking response completion rates, range validation flags, and consistency anomalies. Any enumerator exceeding 5% error rates triggers immediate retraining or field support. For qualitative data, FGD facilitators will submit transcripts within 48 hours of session completion to allow the Lead Consultant to verify content quality and flag gaps requiring follow-up interviews.

**Fieldwork Supervision and Back-Checks**

The Field Research/M&E Specialist will conduct unannounced back-checks on 15–20% of completed surveys (minimum 90 respondents). These back-checks verify respondent identification, consent documentation, and consistency on key variables. Discrepancies exceeding ±10% variance on sensitive items trigger case review and potential re-interview. Weekly field supervision meetings will address enumerator performance, contextual challenges, and protocol adherence.

**Staged Deliverable Review**

Each deliverable undergoes three validation stages: (1) internal Cortech review by non-executing team members; (2) client technical review through the ILO PROSPECTS team; (3) incorporation of feedback with documented rationale. Draft inception reports, fieldwork summaries, and datasets will circulate for comment 5 days before final submission, allowing sufficient revision time.

**Data Verification and Cleaning Protocols**

The Data Management Specialist will execute standardized cleaning procedures: flagging missing values, identifying outliers, cross-checking survey logic, and validating administrative data against beneficiary registries. All cleaning steps are documented in audit logs accessible to the ILO. Cleaned datasets are reconciled against raw data to confirm no information loss. A Data Quality Summary Report accompanying final datasets will detail all transformations applied.

**Stakeholder Feedback Loops**

Monthly brief calls with the ILO will surface emerging data quality concerns or methodological adjustments. A mid-point data review meeting (by 7 October) will allow the ILO to validate initial findings before full analysis begins, ensuring alignment on analytical direction and interpretation approach.

This assignment operates within Cortech's certified institutional safeguarding framework, aligned with ILO standards and donor compliance requirements for research involving vulnerable populations in displacement contexts.

**Institutional Compliance**

Cortech holds ISO certification and operates under mandatory Child Safeguarding and PSEA (Prevention of Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) policies. All core team members complete annual safeguarding training and sign ethics commitments prior to assignment inception. These policies are adapted to Kenya operations and available for ILO review. **Informed Consent and Autonomy**

All participants receive oral and written consent information in Swahili explaining study purpose, data use, risks, confidentiality protections, and the right to refuse or withdraw. Consent is documented through electronic signature within Kobo or witnessed verbal affirmation for phone-based interviews. Survey instruments use numeric identifiers only; no names appear in datasets. Beneficiary registries linking identifiers to personal data are held separately under encryption and restricted to the Lead Consultant and Data Management Specialist. All electronic devices (tablets, laptops) employ password protection and data encryption. Completed questionnaires and FGD transcripts are stored on secure password-protected servers with automatic backup. Data will be retained for the ILO's agreed period and securely deleted thereafter.

**Protection of Vulnerable Groups**

Refugees, women of reproductive age, and persons with disabilities will receive particular attention during consent and data collection. Female enumerators will be prioritized for surveying women survivors of trauma. Interview settings will ensure privacy and safety,

Data Management Specialist leads data cleaning, validation, and preparation of final deliverables. Activities include: (i) removal of duplicate entries and inconsistent responses; (ii) re-contact with enumerators/beneficiaries where data is unclear; (iii) generation of clean quantitative datasets in Excel format; (iv) transcription of FGD recordings into English-language transcripts in MS Word; (v) production of the Fieldwork Report documenting data collection processes, accuracy, and data quality comments; and (vi) submission of all raw and cleaned datasets, transcripts, and reports by October 16.
