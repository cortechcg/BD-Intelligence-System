## Our Understanding of the Assignment

## Our Understanding of the Assignment

Cortech Consulting Group understands that the Impact-Based Forecasting System for Climate Risks in Somalia under the CDRF-ADRiFi Project is intended to address a practical decision-making gap: the need to translate available forecasts and existing forecast products into clear, locally manageable information that links anticipated climate hazards to likely impacts, warning levels, triggers and early action. The assignment is not limited to improving the presentation of forecasts. It is concerned with the full forecast impact action framework through which institutions and users can interpret forecast information, identify the implications of drought and flood, and act through agreed decision workflows before impacts escalate.

The required service is therefore the design and demonstration of a practical, scalable and locally manageable national Impact Based Forecasting framework and prototype analytical package for priority climate-related disaster risks in Somalia. The framework must be grounded in the systems, information and institutional arrangements already available in Somalia. It must review existing forecasts, early-warning systems, datasets, institutional mandates and decision workflows, rather than assume that a new data architecture, forecasting service or national operational platform will be created through this assignment. The intended result is a transparent and usable bridge between hazard information, anticipated impacts, institutional responsibilities and early-action guidance.

The Somalia Disaster Management Agency, referred to in the procurement reference as SoDMA, requires a national perspective that remains operationally realistic within the assignment period. The primary duty station is Mogadishu, while demonstration and testing will take place in no more than two agreed test areas, subject to feasibility and security. This recognises both the need to establish a framework that can be applied across Somalia and the practical constraints affecting travel, access and field-based engagement. The prototype analytical package must consequently be capable of demonstrating how the national Impact Based Forecasting framework can be used in agreed test areas without presenting those areas as a substitute for national institutional ownership or national applicability.

The assignment focuses specifically on drought and flood. These hazards require hazard-to-impact matrices that do more than identify the presence or severity of a hazard. They must enable users to interpret what forecast conditions may mean for people, services, livelihoods, access, infrastructure or other priority consequences identified through the assignment’s priority use cases. The tender documents require the development of thresholds, warning levels, trigger logic and early-action guidance for drought and flood. These elements must be coherent: thresholds should support defined warning levels; warning levels should communicate the significance of forecast conditions; trigger logic should make transparent the conditions under which action is considered or initiated; and early-action guidance should clarify the practical actions and decision-support needed in response.

Cortech understands that the required outputs are deliberately operational. The assignment is expected to produce and test prototype maps, bulletins, alerts, risk matrices and decision-support templates using available data and existing forecast products. These products are not standalone communications materials. They are intended to be practical instruments through which forecast information is translated into impact information and actionable decisions. Their usefulness will depend on their relationship to the identified priority use cases, relevant institutional mandates and existing decision workflows. A prototype bulletin, alert or risk matrix must therefore be understandable and usable by the institutions and users engaged in the forecast impact action framework, rather than being designed solely as a technical analytical product.

The assignment also recognises that sustained use of Impact Based Forecasting depends on institutional and procedural arrangements, not only on analytical products. Standard operating procedures and data-governance arrangements are named requirements because the framework will need clarity on how available data and existing forecast products are accessed, interpreted, shared, validated and used within decision workflows. The standard operating procedures are expected to support consistent operational use of the prototype analytical package, while data-governance arrangements will provide an agreed basis for managing the information that informs forecasts, impact interpretation and decision-support products. User training is correspondingly a core output, enabling relevant users to understand and apply the framework, prototype maps, bulletins, alerts, risk matrices and decision-support templates.

Success for the CDRF-ADRiFi Project will be a framework that is technically transparent while remaining practical for Somalia’s institutional context. It will demonstrate a credible forecast-to-impact-to-action chain for drought and flood, identify priority use cases, and provide tools that users can apply with available data and existing forecast products. It will also establish the basis for continued operationalisation through standard operating procedures, data-governance arrangements, user training and a phased and costed operational roadmap. The roadmap is expected to distinguish the demonstrated prototype analytical package from longer-term operational needs and provide an orderly basis for scaling the Impact Based Forecasting framework beyond the agreed test areas.

The tender documents are equally clear about the assignment’s boundaries. The work will not establish a fully automated national real-time forecasting platform and will not procure permanent ICT or hydrometeorological hardware. The consultant’s role is to design, demonstrate, test and support adoption of a framework and prototype analytical package that make better use of what is already available. This distinction is material to the assignment’s feasibility within six months and to the expectation that the resulting system will be locally manageable.

Cortech recognises that the required service combines climate-resilience, early-warning systems, decision-support systems, data analysis, documentation and capacity building within Somalia’s operating environment. Our Somalia presence, including Mogadishu, and our experience in community early warning and disaster risk reduction, anticipatory action for drought and floods, climate adaptation, GIS/QGIS-based spatial analysis, institutional assessment and user-oriented training are relevant to this requirement. ## Proposed Technical Approach — Summary

## Proposed Technical Approach, A Summary

Cortech Consulting Group will apply a practical, utilization-focused and evidence-driven approach to support SoDMA in designing and demonstrating a national Impact Based Forecasting framework and prototype analytical package for priority climate-related disaster risks in Somalia. The approach is structured around the full forecast-impact-action chain: understanding the available forecast information and institutional context; translating hazard information into anticipated impacts; defining thresholds, warning levels and trigger logic; and supporting decision-makers with clear early-action guidance and usable decision-support products.

The assignment will be delivered from Mogadishu over the six-month period, with limited travel to no more than two agreed test areas where feasible and secure. Cortech’s Somalia operational presence, Somali and English language capability, experience in climate resilience, disaster risk reduction, anticipatory action, GIS/QGIS, data analysis and institutional capacity strengthening provide a relevant foundation for this assignment. The approach will not assume the establishment of a fully automated national real-time forecasting platform or the procurement of permanent ICT or hydrometeorological hardware. Instead, it will work systematically with available data and existing forecast products.

### 1. Inception, mobilisation and institutional alignment

The assignment will commence with an inception process led from Mogadishu to establish a shared understanding of the required national Impact Based Forecasting framework, priority climate-related disaster risks in Somalia, decision users and operational constraints. Cortech will engage SoDMA and relevant users of existing forecasts, early-warning systems and decision-support products to clarify institutional mandates, decision workflows, information needs and the practical conditions under which warnings lead, or do not lead, to early action.

This stage will produce a structured review framework covering existing forecasts, early-warning systems, datasets, institutional mandates and decision workflows. It will also establish transparent criteria for selecting priority use cases for drought and flood, and for identifying no more than two agreed test areas for demonstration and testing. Selection of agreed test areas will be based on SoDMA direction, availability of relevant data and forecast products, and feasibility and security considerations.

### 2. Forecast-impact-action analysis for drought and flood

Cortech will use a participatory systems-analysis method to connect hazard information with likely impacts, accountable decision points and feasible actions. This will combine document review, technical consultations, institutional mapping and analysis of available data through GIS/QGIS and appropriate quantitative and qualitative analytical tools.

For drought and flood, the team will work with SoDMA and relevant stakeholders to develop hazard-to-impact matrices that clearly set out the relationship between forecast conditions, exposed people, places, services and livelihood systems, anticipated impacts, warning levels and potential early actions. The matrices will be designed as transparent operational tools rather than static technical products. They will identify the information required at each stage of escalation and the decisions that must follow.

The forecast impact action framework will define priority use cases and establish a coherent structure for thresholds, warning levels, trigger logic and early-action guidance. The process will test whether proposed thresholds are understandable, based on available information, linked to institutional mandates and realistic within existing decision workflows. Where data limitations, institutional overlaps or workflow gaps affect operational use, these will be documented and addressed through standard operating procedures and data-governance arrangements.

### 3. Prototype analytical package and demonstration

Building on the analysis, Cortech will develop and test a prototype analytical package that enables users to interpret climate risk information consistently and act upon it. The package will include prototype maps, bulletins, alerts, risk matrices and decision-support templates, developed using available data and existing forecast products.

Demonstration and testing in the agreed test areas will focus on usability, clarity and operational relevance. Rather than treating testing as a technical validation exercise alone, Cortech will use structured user feedback to assess whether the prototype products communicate anticipated drought and flood impacts, support timely interpretation of warning levels and trigger logic, and provide actionable early-action guidance. Findings from the demonstration will be used to refine the national framework and ensure that the resulting products are practical, scalable and locally manageable.

### 4. Quality assurance, capacity strengthening and operationalisation

Quality assurance will be embedded throughout delivery. Cortech will maintain documented assumptions, source references, version control and internal technical review for all hazard-to-impact matrices, thresholds, warning levels, prototype products and operational guidance. Draft products will be reviewed with SoDMA and relevant users to ensure that technical content is translated into decision-ready formats and remains aligned with institutional mandates and decision workflows.

User training will be integrated with prototype development and testing. Training will focus on practical interpretation and use of the forecast impact action framework, prototype maps, bulletins, alerts, risk matrices and decision-support templates. It will also strengthen understanding of standard operating procedures and data-governance arrangements needed to sustain use of the system after the assignment.

The final phase will consolidate the national Impact Based Forecasting framework, prototype analytical package, standard operating procedures, data-governance arrangements and a phased and costed operational roadmap. The roadmap will set out sequenced actions for strengthening operational use beyond the assignment, while remaining grounded in available data, existing forecast products and the institutional realities identified during implementation.

### 5. Proposed team and mobilisation capacity

Cortech will deploy a multidisciplinary team combining Somalia-based coordination, stakeholder engagement, GIS/QGIS and data-analysis capability, capacity building and documentation expertise. These strengths are directly relevant to reviewing datasets and decision workflows, developing prototype decision-support products, facilitating user engagement and preparing practical standard operating procedures and data-governance arrangements.

Specialist expertise in hydrometeorological forecasting, impact-based forecasting, remote sensing, hazard modelling, threshold design and trigger logic is central to the technical integrity of this assignment. Specific specialist personnel and their verified qualifications will be confirmed if Cortech is invited to submit a full proposal. Cortech can mobilise from Mogadishu and coordinate limited travel to agreed test areas in accordance with feasibility and security requirements.
