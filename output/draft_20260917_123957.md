## Research design and methods

Research design and methods

1. Overall Approach

Cortech Consulting Group proposes a mixed-methods, criteria-driven evaluation design built directly on the outcome, output and reported-reach data GCERF has provided for Grant 1 (peacebuilding infrastructures, conflict resolution training, livelihoods for vulnerable individuals, and local government reconciliation capacity in Southwest State: Baidoa and Hudur) and Grant 2 (women and youth leadership, community reconciliation, trauma recovery, P/CVE media outreach, and livelihoods/TVET in Hirshabelle: Jowhar, Adan Yabaal and Matabaan). The design treats each grant as a distinct evaluand with its own theory of change, outcome structure and beneficiary population, while applying one common analytical framework, the OECD-DAC criteria (Relevance, Coherence, Effectiveness, Efficiency, Impact/Emerging Impact, Sustainability) plus Data Integrity and Beneficiary Verification, so that findings remain comparable for GCERF's portfolio-level decision-making.

The design rationale responds directly to two features of this assignment. First, GCERF's own research questions are outcome-specific and evidentiary: they ask whether the right people were reached through a verifiable process, whether reported results are plausible, and whether beneficiary counting was documented in a way that supports the numbers claimed. This requires an evaluation matrix that traces each criterion to the specific outcome tables in the RFP rather than a generic OECD-DAC template. Second, the assignment combines a standard evaluation with an independent beneficiary verification exercise covering approximately 10% of reported beneficiaries for each grant, drawn from Grant 1's reported total of 1,088 beneficiaries across Outcomes A, B and C and Grant 2's reported total of 4,180 beneficiaries across Outcomes A, B and C. Cortech's approach therefore runs two parallel, cross-validating data streams: a standard OECD-DAC evaluation stream (document review, key informant interviews, focus group discussions) and a beneficiary verification stream (structured phone and in-person verification calls against sampled beneficiary lists), triangulated in a single analytical framework rather than reported as separate exercises.

Cortech's data collection will use KoboToolbox for structured verification calls, household-level surveys and stakeholder interview instruments, enabling GPS-tagged, offline-capable collection appropriate to Somalia's access constraints across Baidoa, Hudur, Jowhar, Adan Yabaal and Matabaan. Quantitative datasets from KoboToolbox will be cleaned and analysed in SPSS, including cross-tabulation of verified versus reported beneficiary counts by stratum. Qualitative data from key informant interviews and focus group discussions will be coded in NVivo using a codebook structured around the eight evaluation criteria and each grant's outcome areas. Where geographic distribution of reach requires spatial cross-checking against reported implementation sites, Python/QGIS will support mapping of verified beneficiary locations against reported programme sites in Southwest State and Hirshabelle.

2. Phase-by-phase breakdown

Phase 1, Inception (1 to 10 November 2026): following contract award on 1 November 2026, the Evaluation Team Lead will review the grant-specific documentation GCERF makes available post-award (theories of change, results frameworks, targets, beneficiary lists, endline reports, final workplans, Annex 1 programme descriptions and geographic area details) and finalise the evaluation matrix, sampling frame and data collection tools against this fuller dataset. Tools will not be pre-piloted before award; piloting will occur within this ten-day inception window once actual beneficiary lists are available, consistent with the compressed award-to-inception timeline GCERF has set. The Inception Report, due 10 November 2026, will consolidate the methodology, sampling strategy, workplan, evaluation matrix, final report outline, ethical safeguards and data collection tools for GCERF's review.

Phase 2, Field Work (10 November to 1 December 2026): the Data Collection Coordinator/Field Manager will recruit and train enumerators, supervise daily data collection in Baidoa, Hudur, Jowhar, Adan Yabaal and Matabaan, and manage logistics against the security and access constraints specific to each district. Raw data collection is finalised by 1 December 2026 as mandated.

Phase 3, Analysis (1 to 9 December 2026): quantitative and qualitative analysis proceeds in parallel with the final days of fieldwork rather than sequentially after it, so that the aide memoire can be delivered within the seven days GCERF allows. The Data Analyst/Quantitative Specialist runs SPSS cleaning and cross-tabulation as datasets arrive from the field; the Qualitative Analyst/Research Specialist begins NVivo coding of interview transcripts as they are completed and translated.

Phase 4, Reporting (9 December 2026 to 13 January 2027): findings are consolidated into the draft evaluation report (23 December 2026), refined into the final report in English (8 January 2027) alongside cleaned raw datasets, qualitative summaries, data dictionaries/codebooks and analysis syntax files, and presented to GCERF in two separate online presentations, one for Grant 1 and one for Grant 2, on 13 January 2027.

3. Method per deliverable

Inception Report and Data Collection Tools (10 November 2026): the Evaluation Team Lead produces a grant-specific evaluation matrix mapping each OECD-DAC criterion and Data Integrity to the RFP's stated research questions, data sources, instruments, sampling approach and limitations for Grant 1 and Grant 2 separately. One data collection tool per population group is designed in KoboToolbox, covering peace committee members, elders and local leaders, youth bloggers and vulnerable individuals for Grant 1, and women and youth leaders, community member participants, trauma-recovery participants and media outreach audiences for Grant 2, translated into Somali and, for Southwest State, Af-Maay, with forward and back-translation checked by bilingual team members before piloting.

Aide Memoire (9 December 2026): drawing on preliminary SPSS output on verified-versus-reported beneficiary rates and early NVivo themes, the team produces a 2 to 3 page memo flagging initial findings, data quality concerns and assumptions underlying the verification exercise, submitted within one week of fieldwork completion.

Draft and Final Evaluation Reports (23 December 2026 and 8 January 2027): findings are structured against Relevance, Coherence, Effectiveness, Efficiency, Impact/Emerging Impact, Sustainability, Beneficiary Verification and Data Integrity, with each criterion populated by triangulated quantitative and qualitative evidence specific to Grant 1's Southwest State outcomes and Grant 2's Hirshabelle outcomes, following the report outline agreed at inception.

Raw Datasets and Supporting Materials (8 January 2027): cleaned KoboToolbox exports, SPSS syntax files, NVivo coding outputs, data dictionaries and codebooks are delivered in formats GCERF can independently audit.

Online Presentations (13 January 2027): two separate, grant-specific presentations are prepared and delivered, one addressing Grant 1 findings for Southwest State stakeholders and one addressing Grant 2 findings for Hirshabelle stakeholders.

4. Data quality assurance

Beneficiary verification sampling will be stratified by grant, geographic location, sex/age category where safe and appropriate, beneficiary category (peace committee members, elders, youth bloggers and vulnerable individuals for Grant 1; women and youth leaders and community participants for Grant 2) and type of support received, targeting approximately 109 verified cases for Grant 1 and 418 for Grant 2, with the target adjusted where access or security constraints in specific districts require reweighting while preserving stratum representativeness. Verification modality will be assigned by location and sensitivity: phone-based verification with privacy protocols for income-sensitive livelihoods outcomes (Grant 1 Outcome B vulnerable individuals; Grant 2 Outcome C entrepreneurship and TVET beneficiaries) and for districts with restricted physical access, and in-person interviews where security conditions permit. Field supervisors will conduct daily review of incoming KoboToolbox submissions to catch missing values, logic errors and outlier responses while fieldwork is still underway, rather than at the end of the collection period. Enumerators will be trained on standardised administration protocols to reduce interviewer bias, and a random subsample of completed interviews will be back-checked by the Field Manager. Transcription and translation of qualitative material will be cross-checked by a second Somali-speaking team member before NVivo coding to control for translation drift. Data integrity findings will additionally examine how each grant's original beneficiary counting was conducted and documented, comparing programme monitoring records against verified counts to identify the likely cause and effect of any discrepancies.

5. Ethical considerations integration

All data collection tools and protocols will embed informed consent procedures appropriate to low-literacy respondents, verbal consent scripts read aloud in Somali or Af-Maay before any interview or phone verification call, and confidentiality safeguards including anonymised identifiers in place of national ID or full name linkage during phone-based verification, given the risk of clan-based targeting in Somalia's operating environment. Referral pathways will be established for respondents disclosing distress, particularly for trauma-recovery participants under Grant 2 Outcome B, to avoid re-traumatisation during interviews, and interview guides for former AS members and IDPs under Grant 1 Outcome B will be reviewed for do-no-harm risk before fieldwork begins. Secure data storage will use password-protected, access-controlled KoboToolbox servers with encrypted transfer of any personally identifying information, and raw data will be stripped of direct identifiers before analysis files are shared with GCERF. These safeguards will be detailed in full in the Risk Mitigation Plan section of this proposal, alongside security and access risk mitigation specific to Baidoa, Hudur, Jowhar, Adan Yabaal and Matabaan.

## Assumptions (optional)

Assumptions (optional)

The technical approach and workplan set out in this proposal are built on the information provided in RFP-2026-14, including the Grant 1 and Grant 2 outcome, output and reported reach tables, the six OECD-DAC criteria and Data Integrity criterion, and the fixed deliverable dates from contract award through the online presentations. Cortech Consulting Group notes that the RFP itself states that grant theories of change, results frameworks, targets, beneficiary lists, endline reports, final workplans, Annex 1 programme descriptions and detailed geographical area information will be made available to the selected consultant only after contract award and prior to inception. The following assumptions have therefore been applied to keep the proposed approach credible and decidable at proposal stage, while allowing the evaluation matrix, sampling frame and data collection tools to be finalised and agreed with GCERF during the inception period ending 10 November 2026.

1. Timely provision of grant documentation at contract award. It is assumed that GCERF and the two grantees implementing Grant 1 (Southwest State: Baidoa, Hudur) and Grant 2 (Hirshabelle: Jowhar, Adan Yabaal, Matabaan) will make the grant theories of change, results frameworks, disaggregated beneficiary lists, prior monitoring reports, final workplans and Annex 1 programme descriptions available within the first week following the 1 November 2026 award, so that the evaluation matrix and stratified sampling frame can be finalised and reflected in the inception report due 10 November 2026.

2. Beneficiary lists sufficiently disaggregated to support stratification. It is assumed that the beneficiary lists provided post-award will allow stratification by grant, geographic location (Baidoa, Hudur, Jowhar, Adan Yabaal, Matabaan), sex/age where safe and appropriate, beneficiary category (peace committee members, elders and local leaders, youth bloggers, vulnerable individuals, local government officials for Grant 1; women and youth leaders, community member participants, trauma-recovery participants, media outreach audience for Grant 2) and type of support received. Where lists arrive incomplete or without full disaggregation, this will be flagged as a Data Integrity finding rather than treated as a reason to depart from the ~10% verification target.

3. Flexibility in the "approximately 10%" verification target. It is assumed that GCERF's use of "approximately 10%" allows the assessment team to adjust final sample sizes per stratum, within the ranges indicated by the reported reach totals for Grant 1 (1,088) and Grant 2 (4,180), where security, access or non-response conditions in specific districts require a modest upward or downward adjustment, provided representativeness across grant, location, sex/age and beneficiary category is maintained and any deviation is documented and justified in the inception report and final report.

4. Verification modality determined jointly with GCERF and grantees at inception. It is assumed that the choice between phone-based, in-person or hybrid verification for each location and beneficiary category will be confirmed during inception in consultation with GCERF and the two grantees, based on the most current security assessment for Baidoa, Hudur, Jowhar, Adan Yabaal and Matabaan, rather than fixed unilaterally in this proposal. Phone-based or hybrid verification is assumed as the default for beneficiary categories where in-person contact raises heightened protection concerns, including former AS members and vulnerable individuals under Grant 1 Outcome B and the 80 women and youth livelihoods beneficiaries under Grant 2 Outcome C.

5. Grantee cooperation in identifying and safely reaching sensitive beneficiary categories. It is assumed that the two grantees will facilitate introductions, contact validation and, where necessary, safe scheduling for beneficiary categories requiring particular sensitivity, including former AS members, IDPs, minority clans, and trauma-recovery participants, and will confirm any additional do-no-harm or referral protocols already in place for these groups under their own safeguarding policies.

6. Tools finalised and piloted during the inception window, not pre-piloted. Consistent with the ten-day gap between contract award (1 November 2026) and the inception report deadline (10 November 2026), it is assumed that data collection tools will be developed, translated, back-translated and piloted with a small number of respondents from each population group during this window, and that GCERF will accept a final, tested version of the tools as part of the inception report rather than expecting pre-piloted instruments at proposal stage.

8. Fieldwork completion by 1 December 2026 is achievable subject to access. It is assumed that access to Baidoa, Hudur, Jowhar, Adan Yabaal and Matabaan will permit data collection to be finalised by 1 December 2026 as scheduled, and that any district-specific access restriction identified during inception will be addressed through the phone/hybrid verification modality described above rather than requiring an extension to the fieldwork window.
