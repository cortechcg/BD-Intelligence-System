## Executive Summary

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Christian Aid, with support from the Charles Stewart Mott Foundation, has spent three years (January 2024 to December 2026) implementing the Catalyzing Multilateral Development Banks Financing for Energy access in Africa project across Kenya and Malawi, working through national partners Kenya Climate Change Working Group (KCCWG) and Civil Society Network on Climate Change (CISONECC). The project's core challenge is documented but underexamined: despite growing evidence that decentralised renewable energy (DRE) systems can deliver universal energy access while supporting just transition imperatives, African civil society coordination with the Africa Development Bank and other Multilateral Development Banks (MDBs) remains fragmented, evidence of policy influence is difficult to isolate, and the sustained engagement mechanisms required to shift AfDB financing priorities away from fossil fuel dependency are not yet institutionalized. The project attempted to address this through strategic policy research, multi-stakeholder dialogues, capacity building, and direct engagement in AfDB annual meetings and Country Strategy Paper processes, coupled with evidence generation on renewable energy investment. Yet three years on, the extent to which these activities achieved measurable shifts in AfDB policy, strategy, financing priorities and public narratives—and which coordination and knowledge products will endure beyond project closure—remains unassessed. This end-of-project evaluation will generate the independent evidence required to demonstrate what was accomplished, why it succeeded or fell short, and what lessons should guide Christian Aid and its wider civil society partners in future advocacy engagement with the AfDB and other MDBs.

Cortech proposes a rigorous mixed-methods evaluation anchored explicitly in outcome harvesting and contribution analysis—methodologies designed specifically to address the attribution challenge inherent in policy influence work, where causality cannot be established through simple before-after comparison. Rather than constructing a generic logframe-compliance narrative, the evaluation will use contribution analysis to reconstruct the causal pathways through which specific project activities (convenings, dialogues, research outputs, AfDB engagement, the learning platform) are believed to have contributed to observed changes in AfDB and MDB policy, strategy, stakeholder positions and public narratives on DRE. The evaluation matrix, to be developed in the inception report, will disaggregate each OECD-DAC evaluation criterion (effectiveness, relevance, coherence and partnership, sustainability and learning, efficiency, impact and contribution, and cross-cutting gender equality and social inclusion) into discrete sub-questions, indicators and data sources. Cortech will conduct a comprehensive document review of the project logframe and Theory of Change, annual technical and donor reports, research and policy briefs, meeting reports, partner reports, media outputs, and relevant AfDB policy frameworks (Mission 300 Country Compacts and Country Strategy Papers) to establish baselines and track evidence of policy movement. Primary data collection will centre on purposively sampled key informant interviews and focus group discussions with stakeholders disaggregated by country (Kenya and Malawi), stakeholder type (CSO, faith actor, AfDB official, private sector, media, government), and gender, ensuring the evaluation captures representative and diverse perspectives on project influence and sustainability. Qualitative data will be analysed using NVivo, with contribution narratives mapped against project assumptions to test which causal pathways held and which did not. This approach moves beyond accountability-only reporting to generate actionable learning on what modalities of civil society engagement with the AfDB actually move policy, how coalition-building sustainability can be strengthened, and where gender equality and social inclusion were embedded versus overlooked in project design and results.

Cortech will mobilize a senior evaluation team with demonstrable expertise in both advocacy evaluation and multilateral development bank contexts. The lead evaluation consultant will bring a minimum of 10 years' experience conducting end-term evaluations across Africa for major multilateral agencies, with specific track record in civil society advocacy and policy influence assessment. This consultant's prior work includes end-of-project evaluations for civil society and governance programmes (IGAD Land Governance Programme, 2026), institutional capacity assessments for development agencies (Federal Ministry of Health Capacity Assessment, Somalia, World Bank, 2025), and participatory learning frameworks for continental policy processes (Free Movement of Persons Framework for African Union and IOM, 2024). The team will be supported by a qualitative analyst with expertise in outcome harvesting and contribution analysis methods, and a data management specialist who will ensure all primary data collection adheres to OECD-DAC and UNEG evaluation ethics norms, including informed consent protocols, respondent confidentiality, secure data storage, and anonymisation procedures—critical safeguards given that respondents will include AfDB officials, government representatives and media actors. Cortech's Kenya-based operational hub and established relationships with civil society networks provide rapid mobilization capacity and contextual knowledge of both national energy policy landscapes and CSO engagement dynamics. For Malawi data collection, Cortech will deploy a research partner with confirmed field presence and established relationships with CISONECC and national stakeholders to ensure equivalent data quality and stakeholder access across both countries.

The evaluation will be delivered to a compressed eight-week schedule (September–October 2026) through rigorous sequencing of concurrent activities and clearly defined quality gates. Weeks 1–2 will cover contracting and initial briefing, with comprehensive document orientation and stakeholder mapping completed in parallel. Week 3 will produce the inception report, detailing the full evaluation matrix, detailed workplan, data collection tools, and methodology, submitted for Christian Aid's review and approval before fieldwork begins. Weeks 4–5 will execute primary data collection across Kenya and Malawi simultaneously, with weekly data quality checks and early thematic analysis to identify emerging findings. Week 5–6 will complete analysis and produce a draft evaluation report presenting preliminary findings, conclusions and recommendations against all seven OECD-DAC criteria plus cross-cutting GESI analysis, substantiated with direct evidence from interviews, focus groups and document review. Week 7 will convene a validation session with Christian Aid and selected implementing partners to present emerging findings, gather stakeholder feedback on interpretation and recommendations, and incorporate substantive changes into the final account. Week 8 will finalize the comprehensive evaluation report reflecting validation input, together with an executive summary and a short learning brief tailored for wider civil society actors seeking to strengthen future advocacy on energy access, public finance, fossil fuel phase-out and locally led renewable energy solutions. Each deliverable will be submitted to Christian Aid's project lead and Monitoring and Evaluation lead, with internal quality assurance at each phase ensuring that findings remain traceable to primary data, that contribution claims are supported by evidence and not attribution overreach, and that recommendations are both evidence-based and actionable for Christian Aid, KCCWG, CISONECC and wider civil society coalitions.
