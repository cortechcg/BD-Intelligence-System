## Methodology and tools to be used

Methodology and tools to be used

1. Overall Approach

Cortech Consulting Group proposes a mixed-methods, comparative case-study design built explicitly on SPREAD's existing Outcome Harvesting practice and the Year 3/MTR reflection workshop, rather than a generic qualitative review. The design responds directly to the assignment's central proposition: that SPREAD combines peacebuilding, livelihoods, climate resilience and Disaster Risk Reduction as an integrated package, and that the value of this integration, where it exists, must be demonstrated through contrast rather than description. The methodology is therefore organised around deliberate pairing of stronger and weaker integration examples across South Omo/Ethiopia, Turkana/Kenya and Eastern Equatoria/South Sudan, so that the analysis can explain why integrated programming performed differently across sites rather than simply cataloguing activities delivered in each. The approach combines quantitative structured data drawn from Community Action Groups (CAGs), Village Savings and Loan Associations (VSLAs), micro-grants and early warning systems with qualitative Key Informant Interviews (KIIs), Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) and participatory ranking exercises with pastoralists/agro-pastoralists, fisherfolk, women-led and youth-led CSOs, traditional leaders, and local/national authorities. Data will be triangulated against the SPREAD baseline report (2024), the Mid-Term Review (2025) and Year 3/MTR reflection workshop outputs, and will be read against DCA's Conflict Prevention Action Guide 2024, Social Cohesion Action Guide 2024, Social Cohesion Index tool, resilience-building framework, and guidance on Market Systems Development and Financial Inclusion. This anchors every finding to an existing evidentiary base rather than treating the learning paper as a stand-alone survey exercise, which directly addresses the criterion on whether the methodology is clear, appropriate and sufficiently justified in sampling terms. 2. Sampling will be disaggregated by sex, age and disability at every site, and will deliberately capture both community members and CAG/VSLA participants alongside traditional leaders and local/national authorities, so that preferences for future integration can be compared across governance levels, not only across geography. Phase 3, Analysis and Quality Review (November 2026). Quantitative data from CAGs, VSLAs and market structures will be cleaned and analysed in SPSS to establish patterns in participation, access and perceived value across sites. Qualitative KII and FGD transcripts will be coded in NVivo against a framework built directly from the six assignment objectives and six proposed key learning questions, allowing systematic comparison of stronger and weaker integration cases. A review session with the SPREAD team will be held to deepen understanding of emerging findings before drafting, satisfying the ToR's explicit requirement for this quality-check step. Phase 4, Draft Reporting and Validation (21 November to 3 December 2026). A draft learning paper and two distinct draft policy briefs, one general brief on integration for donors, IGAD and governments, and one 4-8 page brief focused on human security and stability for EU, Denmark, IGAD and other governments, will be shared with the CMU and relevant thematic leads for discussion. The cross-border validation workshop, bringing together consortium members, IGAD and other stakeholders from all three countries, will be designed as an evidence-testing exercise: participants will be asked to interrogate the stronger/weaker case-study contrasts and the draft SMART recommendations, not simply receive a presentation of findings. Phase 5, Finalisation (by 15 December 2026). The final learning paper, both final policy briefs, case-study files and cleaned, anonymised raw data, the executive management summary and summary PowerPoint, will be finalised incorporating validation workshop feedback, and a facilitated results presentation and learning session will be delivered to DCA and consortium partners. 3. Method by Deliverable

Inception report: desk review plus KoboToolbox tool design, submitted to DCA/CMU for approval by 13 October 2026. Draft and final learning paper: SPSS-analysed structured data plus NVivo-coded KII/FGD data organised around the six assignment objectives, producing SMART recommendations on integration versus coordination. General policy brief: synthesised for donors, IGAD and governments on integration lessons generally. Human-security policy brief (4-8 pages): drawn from the same evidence base but framed specifically around contribution to stability and human security, for EU, Denmark, IGAD and other governments. Cross-border validation workshop: single joint session across the three countries, structured around draft findings and recommendations. Case-study files and cleaned data: anonymised and annexed to the final report. Executive summary and PowerPoint: distilled from the final learning paper. Results presentation: facilitated session with DCA and consortium partners. 4. Data Quality Assurance

All KoboToolbox instruments will be piloted in-country before full deployment, with harmonised skip logic and translation checks across the three national partner teams. SPSS outputs will be cross-checked against raw CAG/VSLA administrative data where available, and NVivo coding will be subject to a second-coder review on a sample of transcripts to check inter-coder consistency. Data will be triangulated against the SPREAD baseline and MTR at the analysis stage, and any discrepancy between community-reported and administrative data will be flagged and explained in the final report's limitations section, as required by the ToR. 5. Ethical Considerations

Data collection will follow informed consent procedures with all respondents, including CAG and VSLA members, traditional leaders and local authorities, with particular attention to conflict-sensitive framing given the cross-border, fragile operating context. Disaggregation by sex, age and disability will be applied throughout, and case-study files will be anonymised before annexation to the final report. Enumerators recruited under TUPADO, ICPDO, ROG and ECC will be briefed on do-no-harm principles and safeguarding standards consistent with the assignment's operating environment across South Omo/Ethiopia, Turkana/Kenya and Eastern Equatoria/South Sudan before fieldwork begins.
