## Our Understanding of the Assignment

OUR UNDERSTANDING OF THE ASSIGNMENT

Cortech Consulting Group understands that the Somali Disaster Management Agency (SoDMA), as Executing Agency for the Multi-National Climate Disaster Risk Financing Project (CDRF, ADRiFi Project, Somalia), financed by the African Development Bank Group under Grant No. 5550155002203 (GEF), Project ID P-Z1-K00-165, is procuring a consulting firm under Quality- and Cost-Based Selection (Reference No. SO-SODMA-ADRIFI-QCBS-02-2026) to design and demonstrate a practical, scalable, and locally manageable Impact-Based Forecasting (IBF) framework and prototype analytical package for priority climate-related disaster risks. The assignment sits within Component 1: Enabling environment for the adoption of climate risk financing instruments in Somalia, and is coordinated through the Project Implementation Unit (PIU) established by SoDMA.

The problem the assignment addresses is precise. Somalia faces recurrent drought, riverine and flash flooding, extreme heat, water scarcity, and rainfall variability, and although national and partner institutions produce a growing range of climate and early warning information, many products remain hazard-focused. They describe what the atmosphere or rivers may do, but do not consistently explain the likely consequences for people, assets, services, and priority locations, or which early actions should be taken. IBF closes that gap by combining hazard forecasts with exposure, vulnerability, coping-capacity, and historical impact information so that decision-makers understand what may happen, where impacts may occur, who or what may be affected, how serious impacts may be, and which early actions to take. The users of the outputs are SoDMA and the CDRF, ADRiFi PIU, Federal Member State institutions, the Ministry of Environment and Climate Change, relevant hydrometeorological, water, agriculture, and livestock authorities, and humanitarian and development partners, while official warning issuance and operational forecasting authority remain with the legally mandated government institutions.

We understand equally clearly what this assignment is not. The Terms of Reference state that it is not a contract to build a fully automated national real-time forecasting platform, a 24/7 operational service, or permanent ICT or hydrometeorological infrastructure. It excludes complex modelling from first principles, nationwide field validation, extensive primary data collection, creation of new national datasets, and long-term operation of the prototype after the consultancy. The prototype may be implemented through documented analytical workflows, GIS products, spreadsheets, scripts, open-source tools, or a lightweight dashboard concept, and it must operate as the forecasting and impact-analysis component of Somalia's developing national Multi-Hazard Early Warning System without creating a parallel warning system, data platform, or communication channel. An EOI that pitches a large platform build would misread the client's intent; ours does not. The stated principle of a realistic and cost-effective level of effort, a small multidisciplinary team combining compatible expert functions, limited travel, and maximum use of existing data will govern how we would structure the work at RFP stage.

Success at the end of six months is defined by the deliverable schedule itself: an agreed national IBF framework with documented data and institutional workflows; hazard-to-impact matrices and trigger logic for the mandatory hazards of drought and flood; prototype maps, impact bulletins, risk matrices, alert summaries, decision notes, and SMS/radio message templates with Somali-language summaries; standard operating procedures and data governance arrangements; trained users evidenced through at least two hands-on technical training sessions, one validation/handover session, and pre/post learning checks; and a phased, costed roadmap for institutionalization, digital development, maintenance, and expansion. The six milestones fall at Weeks 2, 6, 12, 18, 22, and 26, from the Inception Report and Detailed Work Plan through to the Final Consolidated IBF Package and Operational Roadmap, each subject to written acceptance by SoDMA and, where applicable, review or no-objection by the African Development Bank. Acceptance conditions are explicit: traceable sources, reproducible logic, editable and non-proprietary formats such as XLSX, CSV, GeoPackage, GeoJSON, and shapefiles, and full handover of source files, scripts, and formulas, with no avoidable proprietary lock-in.

The duty station is Mogadishu, Somalia, with limited travel to no more than two agreed test areas, selected during inception based on risk relevance, data availability, security, and feasibility, and demonstration covering one or two hazards through historical events, available forecasts, and scenario exercises. We recognise that access to data held by third-party institutions cannot be guaranteed; the firm must maintain a data catalogue with metadata, plan realistic alternatives and gap-filling options, and manage material access constraints through agreed adjustments to assumptions, sequencing, or test areas without expanding scope. We also recognise the review rhythm the client has set: consolidated comments normally within ten working days, revised deliverables with a comments-response matrix normally within five, and payments only after satisfactory completion and written acceptance.

Finally, we understand that Section 14 obligations are integral rather than peripheral. Consultations and products must reflect gender equality, disability inclusion, displacement, age, livelihood, and other relevant dimensions of vulnerability, including IDP populations within exposure layers; the methodology must be conflict-sensitive and consistent with applicable environmental and social safeguards; and any primary consultation must follow informed-consent, data-protection, safeguarding, and do-no-harm practices. All deliverables will be in English, with Somali versions or concise Somali summaries for public-facing and community communication products, consistent with the working languages of the assignment. Cortech, operating from its Mogadishu office with Somali and English proficiency across its team, reads this assignment as a disciplined, bounded exercise in building SoDMA's own capability to run a forecast-impact-action framework, and it is on that reading that our EOI is built.

## Proposed Technical Approach — Summary

PROPOSED TECHNICAL APPROACH, A SUMMARY

Cortech Consulting Group proposes a practical, government-anchored approach that delivers exactly what the Terms of Reference define: an agreed national Impact-Based Forecasting (IBF) framework and a reproducible prototype analytical package for drought and flood, built on available data and existing forecast products, and operating as the forecasting and impact-analysis component of Somalia's developing national Multi-Hazard Early Warning System. We have read the scope boundaries carefully. We will not propose a fully automated real-time platform, new national datasets, hardware procurement, or any parallel warning system or communication channel. Official warning issuance remains with the legally mandated government institutions, and our design work will reinforce, not displace, their authority.

Inception and mobilisation. Working from our Mogadishu office, we will mobilise within days of contract effectiveness and use the first two weeks to confirm the interpretation of the assignment with the SoDMA Project Coordinator through the CDRF, ADRiFi PIU. The Inception Report and Detailed Work Plan (Week 2) will set out the methodology, staffing and level-of-effort approach, stakeholder engagement plan, data-access plan, quality-assurance process, risk register, the proposed drought and flood focus, and candidate test areas assessed against risk relevance, data availability, security, and feasibility. Because third-party data access cannot be guaranteed, the inception risk register will pair each critical dataset with a realistic alternative or gap-filling option, so that constraints are managed through adjusted assumptions or sequencing rather than scope expansion.

Method family. The core of the assignment is a forecast-impact-action framework, and our method follows the structure of the Scope of Services. We will map institutional mandates, existing forecasts, approval and dissemination workflows, and operational gaps across SoDMA and partner institutions; consult Federal Member State institutions, technical agencies, humanitarian and development partners, and representative user groups to define the priority decisions the prototype must support; and compile a documented data catalogue covering hazard, exposure, vulnerability, coping-capacity, historical impact, disaster-loss, population, livelihood, infrastructure, food-security, health, water, and service-access data, with quality, ownership, licensing, and limitations assessed for each source. On this foundation we will design the Simplified National IBF Framework and Draft SOPs (Week 12), including impact categories, risk and warning levels, proposed thresholds and trigger logic for drought and flood, uncertainty treatment, and approval workflows. The Hazard-to-Impact Matrices and Prototype IBF Product Package (Week 18) will be delivered as documented analytical workflows in QGIS, spreadsheets, and open scripts, with editable templates for maps, impact bulletins, risk matrices, alert summaries, decision notes, SMS/radio messages, and Somali-language summaries. Our bilingual English and Somali team drafts these communication products directly, not through subcontracted translation.

Fieldwork footprint. In line with the ToR, demonstration and testing will cover the agreed hazards and no more than two agreed test areas, combining in-country engagement in Mogadishu with remote technical work to keep travel limited and cost-effective. Testing will use historical events, available forecasts, and scenario exercises, comparing prototype outputs with observed impacts where data permit. All consultations will follow informed-consent, data-protection, and do-no-harm practice, and the framework will differentiate impacts by gender, disability, displacement, age, and livelihood, consistent with Section 14 of the ToR.

Analysis, quality assurance, and deliverables. Reproducibility and editability are treated as acceptance conditions, not aspirations. Every threshold, formula, script, and map-production step will be documented and handed over in XLSX, CSV, GeoPackage, GeoJSON, and shapefile formats, avoiding proprietary lock-in. Each deliverable passes internal technical and language review before submission, and we will respond to SoDMA's consolidated comments with a revised version and a comments-response matrix within the agreed five working days. The Prototype Demonstration, Validation, and Capacity-Transfer Report (Week 22) will evidence at least two hands-on technical training sessions and one validation and handover session, with pre and post learning checks. The Final Consolidated IBF Package and Operational Roadmap (Week 26) will hand over all source files, the data catalogue, SOPs, user manual, M&E framework, and a phased, costed roadmap that distinguishes current resources from future financing needs, ready for SoDMA approval and, where applicable, African Development Bank no-objection.

Team mapping. A small multidisciplinary team will cover the six indicative functions, combining compatible roles where credible. The Team Leader / Impact-Based Forecasting Specialist leads framework design and stakeholder facilitation; the Climate / Hydrometeorology Specialist leads thresholds and trigger logic; the GIS, Data, and Prototype Systems Specialist builds the reproducible prototype; the DRM, Vulnerability, and Early Action Specialist leads hazard-to-impact matrices, early actions, and SOPs; the Risk Communication and Capacity-Transfer Specialist leads product templates and training; and our Mogadishu-based National Liaison / Inclusion function ensures Somali-language delivery, conflict sensitivity, and Federal Member State engagement throughout. The full team structure will be presented at the Request for Proposals stage as the ToR provides.
