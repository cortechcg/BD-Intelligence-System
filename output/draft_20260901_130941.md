## Introduction, Background & Conceptual Framework

SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND

5.1 Context and Strategic Importance

SOS Children's Villages Somalia (SOS CV Somalia) delivers integrated programming in child protection, family strengthening, youth employability, education, health, emergency response, and community development from its Banadir and Baidoa program locations, within one of the most complex humanitarian and development environments in the world. The 2025 review of the Country Strategy (2023, 2026) recorded approximately 95% achievement of strategic Key Performance Indicators, with strong results in humanitarian action, education access, and alternative care scale-up, but it also surfaced three structural risks that must shape the next cycle: the phase-out of child sponsorship by SOS Norway and SOS Finland, a persistent self-reliance gap among Alternative Care and Family Strengthening households exiting the programme, and the 2024 Parliamentary rejection of the Child Rights Bill. The Country Strategy (2027, 2030) must therefore be developed against the 2026 Somalia Humanitarian Needs Overview (2026 HNO), the Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP 2026), the National Transformation Plan 2025, 2029, the SDGs, and the SOS Children's Villages International Federation Strategy 2030, at a moment when Somalia's 2024 Disability Act and evidence of exclusion risks for children with disabilities in Baidoa vulnerability assessments raise the bar for disability inclusion.

5.2 Purpose and Objectives

The purpose of this consultancy is to facilitate the development of a comprehensive, results-oriented, and future-focused Country Strategy (2027, 2030) that will guide SOS CV Somalia's strategic priorities, programming, partnerships, advocacy, organizational development, and resource mobilization efforts. The assignment requires a participatory, evidence-based, and inclusive process spanning a comprehensive desk review, a situational analysis grounded in PESTEL and SWOT, extensive consultations with internal and external stakeholders from children and caregivers through line ministries (MoH, MoECHE, MOFHRD, MOYS, MOLSA), institutional donors (ECHO, GAC, FCDO, BMZ), PSAs (CVW, SOS Norway, SOS Sweden), UNICEF, the Somalia Child right Coalitions (SCRC), the Joining Forces Alliance (JFA) Somalia chapter, and private sector actors such as Hormuud telecom. Distinctively, the ToR requires an execution-oriented planning approach per the Kaplan & Norton execution premium framework, adapted for the humanitarian and nonprofit context, so that the strategy document is paired with a concrete mechanism for sustained implementation.

5.3 Our Interpretation of the Assignment

Cortech reads this assignment as a response to a diagnosed execution gap, not a strategy-drafting exercise: the 2023, 2025 Strategic Review found well-designed programme strategies undermined by inconsistent operational follow-through, citing the Family Strengthening self-reliance design gap, the under-resourced advocacy function, and delayed rollout of finalized care guidelines. The Strategy Map, Balanced Scorecard, Strategic Initiative Portfolio with StratEx distinct from OpEx, cascaded scorecards for each programme stream and for the Banadir and Baidoa locations, and the Office of Strategy Management model are therefore the substance of the assignment, on equal footing with the strategy narrative itself. One implication the documents imply but do not spell out is a hard sequencing constraint inside the 12-week timeline: the four targeted evidence pieces, an updated Child Rights Situation Analysis (the last full CRSA dates to 2016), a comparative evaluation of community-based Alternative Care versus Family-Like Care on outcomes and cost, a mapping of the local fundraising and funding landscape, and an EPHS handover readiness assessment across all SOS CV Somalia-managed health facilities, must all be commissioned or incorporated before the Situational Analysis Report can be finalized in week three, which means their scoping must begin during contracting and inception in week one, in parallel with safeguarding briefings and security clearance for Baidoa travel under INSO access-negotiation protocols. A second implication is that the strategy must be drafted around a moving baseline, since Tier 1 recommendations from the review, the PSA sponsorship transition plan, recruitment of a dedicated Advocacy Officer, and initiation of the Board of Directors establishment process, are being implemented in parallel during 2026 and must be built on rather than duplicated, including through an interim NMT-level strategic-oversight mechanism pending Board establishment.

Stage one, develop the strategy, corresponds to the desk review, PESTEL, SWOT, stakeholder mapping, and hypothesis testing that produce the Situational Analysis and Strategic Assessment Reports. Stages five and six, monitor and learn, then test and adapt, are institutionalized in the operational and quarterly strategy review meeting cadence, the strategy performance dashboard template, and the 2029 mid-cycle checkpoint at which causal assumptions are re-tested against MEAL evidence.

We treat the Strategy Map and the Theory of Change as the ToR does: distinct instruments where the Strategy Map feeds into the Theory of Change. The Theory of Change articulates how SOS CV Somalia's interventions produce change for children, youth, families, and communities, including the causal pathways behind contested choices such as community-based care (foster, kinship, Kafala) versus Family-Like Care; the Strategy Map expresses the organizational cause-and-effect logic by which Learning & Growth investments drive Internal Process improvements that deliver Financial/Donor Sustainability and ultimately Mission/Beneficiary Impact. Both are disciplined by the RBPMR and by the MEAL framework deliverable.

This framework fits the assignment because it directly answers the diagnosed problem. The 2023, 2025 review did not find weak strategy; it found an execution gap between sound design and operational follow-through. A conventional strategic-planning lens would reproduce that gap. The execution premium lens converts each hypothesis test into an owned initiative, each initiative into a scorecard line, and each scorecard line into a governance routine that the interim NMT-level oversight mechanism, and eventually the Board of Directors, can sustain without external support.

## Technical Methodology

PROPOSED APPROACH AND METHODOLOGY

1. Overall Approach

Cortech Consulting proposes a participatory, evidence-based, and execution-oriented approach to developing the SOS CV Somalia Country Strategy (2027, 2030), designed around the central finding of the 2023, 2025 Strategic Review: that the organization's principal challenge is not strategy formulation but a recurring execution gap between well-designed programme strategies and consistent operational follow-through. Our design therefore pairs the conventional strategic planning cycle (desk review, situational analysis, consultation, visioning, drafting, validation) with the Kaplan & Norton execution premium framework, adapted for the humanitarian and nonprofit context. Every strategic objective agreed through the participatory process will leave the process carrying a linked measure, target, and named strategic initiative on a Balanced Scorecard, positioned within a multi-perspective Strategy Map (Mission/Beneficiary Impact, Financial/Donor Sustainability, Internal Process, Learning & Growth), and connected to a governance mechanism, the Office of Strategy Management function and the defined cadence of operational and quarterly strategy review meetings, that sustains execution after our engagement ends. We treat the Strategy Map as distinct from, and feeding into, the Theory of Change, and the Results Framework as a Balanced Scorecard rather than a standalone list of indicators, exactly as the ToR requires.

Second, the process is structured as a hypothesis-testing exercise, not a blank-page visioning retreat. The five strategic hypotheses from the 2023, 2025 review, whether community-based Alternative Care (foster, kinship, Kafala) should become the primary modality in place of Family-Like Care; whether the SOS College of Health Sciences warrants a standalone strategic objective; the scope of SOS CV Somalia's role in the national Child Helpline partnership; the target proportion of locally-generated income by 2030 given PSA sponsorship phase-out; and whether advocacy should become a standalone programme stream, will each be framed at inception as a testable proposition with defined evidence requirements, then adjudicated against the four commissioned evidence pieces and consultation findings during the 3-day Strategy Development Workshop. Third, the process is sequenced to respect the ToR's gates: the four targeted evidence pieces are commissioned in Week 1 and completed before the Situational Analysis Report is finalized; every deliverable passes through the Strategy Development Reference Group with the standard five-working-day review window built into the workplan; and the draft strategy is checked for consistency with the parallel 2026 Tier 1 actions (PSA sponsorship transition plan, Advocacy Officer recruitment, Board of Directors establishment process) so that it builds on rather than duplicates them.

2. Phase-by-Phase Breakdown

Phase 1, Contracting and Inception (Week 1). The Team Leader and national co-consultant hold an inception meeting with the National Director and the Head of MEAL/National planning and review coordinator, receive the reference materials (the 2023, 2025 MA Somalia national strategic review and forward intelligence report, RBPMR, annual reports 2023, 2025, the current National Strategy 2023, 2026, Federation Strategy 2030 guidance, the National Transformation Plan 2025, 2029, and internal MEAL, financial, and risk documentation), and agree the consultation frame with the Strategy Development Reference Group. All team members, enumerators, and facilitators sign the Child and Youth Safeguarding Policy code of conduct and complete the safeguarding briefing in Week 1, before any child contact. Output: Inception Report containing methodology, work plan, consultation framework and tools, the hypothesis-testing matrix, and terms of reference for the four commissioned evidence pieces.

Phase 2, Situational Analysis (Weeks 2, 3). Parallel workstreams: (1) structured desk review coded in NVivo against a framework covering PESTEL, SWOT, the child rights and protection landscape, funding and partnership analysis, and the organizational readiness dimensions named in the ToR (Board re-establishment progress, staff turnover, Dynamics 365 migration, supply chain resilience for emergency nutrition and health commodities); (2) the four targeted evidence pieces, an updated Child Rights Situation Analysis refreshing the 2016 CRSA, a comparative evaluation of community-based Alternative Care versus Family-Like Care on outcomes and cost, a mapping of the local fundraising and funding landscape in Somalia including digital mechanisms such as the Impact Fund for Children, and an EPHS handover readiness assessment across all SOS CV Somalia-managed health facilities; (3) stakeholder consultations, approximately 30 to 35 key informant interviews with the NMT, programme leads, line ministries (MoH, MoECHE, MOFHRD, MOYS, MOLSA), institutional donors (ECHO, GAC, FCDO, BMZ), PSAs (CVW, SOS Norway, SOS Sweden), UNICEF, SCRC, the JFA Somalia chapter, and Hormuud telecom, plus 10 to 12 focus group discussions with children, youth, caregivers, women, persons with disabilities, and community-based organizations in Banadir and Baidoa, facilitated in Somali by our national co-consultants. FGD attendance registers and structured consultation records are captured digitally in KoboToolbox; qualitative transcripts are coded in NVivo; any quantitative reanalysis of MEAL datasets (for example, Family Strengthening self-reliance exit data) is run in SPSS. The analysis explicitly covers geographic expansion opportunities: community-based Alternative Care in Southwest state Somalia and Family Strengthening expansion into Jubaland and Hirshabelle, where UASC caseloads are highest. Output: Situational Analysis Report.

Phase 3, Strategy Development Workshop and Strategic Assessment (Weeks 4, 6). We facilitate the 3-day strategy development workshop for the SOS Somalia programme and operational leadership team. Day 1 validates the situational analysis and adjudicates the five strategic hypotheses against the commissioned evidence; Day 2 builds the draft Strategy Map across the four perspectives and the Theory of Change; Day 3 drafts objectives with measure, target, and named strategic initiative, and screens candidate initiatives for the Strategic Initiative Portfolio. Outputs: Strategy Development Workshop report and, in Weeks 5, 6, the Strategic Assessment Report consolidating key findings, lessons learned, SWOT, and strategic recommendations.

Phase 4, Drafting (Weeks 7, 8). The Team Leader drafts the Country Strategy (2027, 2030) with vision, mission, values, positioning, Theory of Change, Strategy Map, strategic priorities and objectives, cross-cutting themes (gender, age, and diversity mainstreaming; protection mainstreaming; climate resilience; disability inclusion anchored in the 2024 Disability Act and the Baidoa vulnerability assessment findings on children with disabilities), localization, partnerships, advocacy and influencing, resource mobilization addressing funding concentration risk and multi-year funding cycles, and organizational development. The Strategic Initiative Portfolio with indicative StratEx recommendations, kept distinct from core OpEx, is drafted in parallel.

Phase 5, Validation and Finalization (Weeks 9, 12). A validation workshop with internal and external stakeholders reviews the draft; feedback is logged in a comment-disposition matrix showing how each input was addressed. Weeks 11, 12 deliver the final package: Final Country Strategy, Results Framework and KPI matrix structured as a Balanced Scorecard, risk management framework updating the register across funding volatility, security/access, climate shocks, programmatic self-reliance gaps, advocacy/policy risk, and cybersecurity, cascaded balanced scorecards per programme stream and for the Banadir and Baidoa program locations, organizational alignment and strategy governance plan (including the interim NMT-level strategy committee pending Board establishment and its transition to full Board governance), communication and change management plan linked to Dynamics 365 performance management, resource capacity and budget alignment plan distinguishing StratEx from OpEx, strategy performance dashboard template for quarterly strategy review meetings, implementation roadmap with the formal mid-cycle 2029 checkpoint re-testing Strategy Map and Theory of Change causal assumptions against MEAL evidence (distinct from routine quarterly reviews), and the executive PowerPoint presentation.

3. Method by Deliverable

Each deliverable is produced by a named method: the Inception Report from the inception meeting and document handover; the Situational Analysis Report from NVivo-coded desk review, the four commissioned evidence pieces, KIIs, and Kobo-recorded FGDs; the Strategy Map and Theory of Change from facilitated cause-and-effect mapping on Workshop Day 2; the Balanced Scorecard and Strategic Initiative Portfolio from Day 3 objective-setting and initiative screening scored on impact and feasibility; cascaded scorecards from follow-up working sessions with each programme stream lead and the Banadir and Baidoa location managers; governance, communication, and capacity plans from structured design sessions with the NMT and HR; and the roadmap and dashboard from a final integration session with the Head of MEAL/National planning and review coordinator, so that the review cadence is owned internally before we exit.

4. Data Quality Assurance

Quality is assured through: (1) triangulation of every strategic recommendation across at least two independent sources (desk review, commissioned evidence, KII, FGD); (2) daily debriefs during fieldwork and same-day upload of KoboToolbox records with logic checks; (3) dual coding of a sample of NVivo transcripts to test inter-coder reliability; (4) internal peer review of every deliverable by a Cortech senior reviewer not embedded in the assignment before submission to the Reference Group; and (5) an audit trail linking each hypothesis verdict, scorecard measure, and initiative back to its evidence base, so the Reference Group can interrogate provenance during its five-day review.

5. Ethical Considerations

All consultations involving children and youth follow SOS CV Somalia's Child and Youth Safeguarding Policy: signed code of conduct and completed safeguarding briefing for all team members before child contact, age-appropriate informed consent and assent protocols in Somali, same-sex facilitation for FGDs with women and girls supported by our gender-balanced team, and referral pathways agreed with SOS CV Somalia in advance. Individual responses are anonymized in all reporting; organizational, donor, and personal data are held confidentially and not retained beyond the assignment; all deliverables remain the sole property of SOS Children's Villages Somalia. Field travel, including to Baidoa, follows SOS CV Somalia security clearance procedures and INSO access-negotiation protocols where relevant, and the workplan carries contingency for access constraints, with remote KII and FGD modalities pre-designed as fallback so the 12-week timeline holds.

## Sampling Strategy & Data Analysis Plan

SAMPLING AND PARTICIPANT SELECTION

This assignment is a strategy development process, not a population survey, and does not require a probability sample. Consultation participants will instead be selected purposively against a stakeholder map agreed with the Head of MEAL/National planning and review coordinator at inception, ensuring coverage of every constituency named in the ToR: children, youth, caregivers, women, persons with disabilities, and community-based organizations at community level in Banadir and Baidoa; SOS CV Somalia management and staff across programme streams; line ministries (MoH, MoECHE, MOFHRD, MOYS, MOLSA); institutional donors (ECHO, GAC, FCDO, BMZ); PSA partners including CVW, SOS Norway and SOS Sweden; UNICEF, the Somalia Child right Coalitions (SCRC) and the Joining Forces Alliance (JFA) Somalia chapter; and private sector actors such as Hormuud telecom. Selection criteria will privilege maximum variation (by gender, age, disability status, care modality, and location) and information richness on the five strategic hypotheses the process must test. FGDs with children and youth will be gender-segregated, facilitated in Somali by our national co-consultants, and conducted only after the safeguarding briefing, signed code of conduct, and age-appropriate informed consent/assent required under SOS CV Somalia's Child and Youth Safeguarding Policy. Any purposive quotas and the final consultation matrix will be documented in the Inception Report for Strategy Development Reference Group approval.

SECTION 2, DATA ANALYSIS PLAN

Quantitative data for this assignment derives principally from secondary sources: KPI achievement data from the 2023, 2025 MA Somalia national strategic review, MA Somalia annual reports 2023, 2024 and 2025, MEAL and financial records, and the four commissioned evidence pieces (the updated CRSA, the comparative evaluation of community-based Alternative Care versus Family-Like Care on outcomes and cost, the local fundraising and funding landscape mapping, and the EPHS handover readiness assessment). These datasets will be cleaned, coded and analyzed in SPSS, with Excel supporting data organization. Analysis will be primarily descriptive: trend analysis of KPI performance across the 2023, 2026 strategy period, cross-tabulations of programme outcomes by location (Banadir, Baidoa), care modality, gender, age and disability status, and cost-per-outcome comparisons between care models to inform the hypothesis on whether community-based care (foster, kinship, Kafala) should become the primary Alternative Care modality. Where sample sizes in the commissioned studies permit, we will apply chi-square and t-tests to establish whether observed differences between care modalities or between exiting and continuing Family Strengthening households are statistically significant, directly informing the self-reliance gap analysis. Funding data will be analyzed for concentration risk, modelling scenarios for locally-generated income shares by 2030 under PSA sponsorship phase-out.

Qualitative data from KIIs, FGDs and workshop outputs will be transcribed, translated from Somali where required, and coded in NVivo using a hybrid framework: deductive codes drawn from the PESTEL and SWOT domains, the four Balanced Scorecard perspectives, and the five strategic hypotheses, complemented by inductive codes for emergent themes. All individual responses will be anonymized in coding and reporting, consistent with the data protection conditions in Section 11.

Triangulation will be systematic rather than incidental. We will construct an evidence matrix mapping each strategic hypothesis and each SWOT/PESTEL finding against at least three independent sources (document, quantitative dataset, stakeholder group), flagging convergence, divergence and gaps. Divergent findings will not be smoothed over; they will be tabled for adjudication at the 3-day strategy development workshop, so that strategic choices in the Draft Country Strategy (2027, 2030) rest on tested, contested and validated evidence rather than single-source assertion. The full analysis protocol, coding framework and evidence matrix template will be presented in the Inception Report for review by the Strategy Development Reference Group within the standard five working day window.

## Quality Assurance & Ethical Considerations

QUALITY ASSURANCE FRAMEWORK

Cortech Consulting Group applies an ISO-certified quality management system to every deliverable produced under this assignment, with quality gates tied to the specific stage-review arrangement the ToR establishes: submission of each deliverable in draft to the Strategy Development Reference Group, a five-working-day review window, and formal approval before the next stage begins. Our internal quality process runs one step ahead of that external gate so that the Reference Group receives documents that have already passed peer review.

During the Contracting and Inception phase (Week 1), the Team Leader and the Head of MEAL/National planning and review coordinator will jointly agree quality benchmarks for each of the eighteen deliverables, including the requirement that the Results Framework be structured as a Balanced Scorecard with a linked measure, target, and named strategic initiative for every objective, and that the Strategy Map remain distinct from, and feed into, the Theory of Change. The Inception Report will carry a deliverable acceptance matrix reflecting these benchmarks.

During the Situational Analysis phase (Weeks 2-3), data quality checks include source triangulation across the desk review, the four commissioned evidence pieces (updated CRSA, community-based Alternative Care versus Family-Like Care comparison, local fundraising landscape mapping, EPHS handover readiness assessment), key informant interviews, and focus group discussions. All qualitative data are coded in NVivo with a second-coder consistency check on at least 20 percent of transcripts; consultation logs are reconciled weekly against the stakeholder map to confirm coverage of children, youth, caregivers, women, persons with disabilities, and community-based organizations in both Banadir and Baidoa.

At the Strategic Assessment, Draft Country Strategy, and Validation stages, every document passes internal peer review by a senior consultant not engaged in drafting, followed by a completeness check against the ToR deliverable table before release to the Reference Group. Client feedback is logged in a comments-resolution matrix showing how each Reference Group and validation workshop comment was addressed, giving the National Director a transparent audit trail from draft to Final Country Strategy (2027-2030).

ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS AND SAFEGUARDING

This assignment involves direct consultation with children, youth, caregivers, women, and persons with disabilities, including focus group discussions in Banadir and Baidoa and evidence gathering on unaccompanied and separated children (UASC). Cortech Consulting Group is ISO certified and maintains an organizational child safeguarding policy and PSEA policy; these will operate alongside, and be subordinated to, SOS CV Somalia's Child and Youth Safeguarding Policy for the duration of the engagement.

The ToR requires that "the consultant and any enumerators or facilitators engaged shall sign the organization's safeguarding code of conduct and complete a safeguarding briefing prior to engaging directly with children and shall obtain age-appropriate informed consent/assent for all child participants." We will meet this in full: all team members, including the national co-consultant and any Somali-language facilitators, will sign the safeguarding code of conduct during Week 1, complete the safeguarding briefing before any child contact, and be trained on referral pathways so that any disclosure or protection concern is escalated immediately through SOS CV Somalia's designated safeguarding focal point rather than handled by the team.

Informed consent will be obtained from all adult participants in Somali or English as appropriate; for children, consent will be sought from parents or legal guardians and age-appropriate assent from the children themselves, using plain-language scripts reviewed at inception. Participation is voluntary and may be withdrawn at any point without consequence. FGDs with women, girls, and persons with disabilities will be facilitated by female team members, consistent with the ToR's expectation of a gender-balanced team, and disability-inclusive facilitation will reflect the exclusion risks for children with disabilities identified in the Baidoa vulnerability assessments and the requirements of the 2024 Disability Act.

On data protection, we will meet the ToR's confidentiality conditions in full: all organizational, donor, and personal data will be treated as confidential, individual responses anonymized in every report, and no confidential internal documents retained beyond the assignment. All deliverables, data, and materials remain the sole property of SOS Children's Villages Somalia. Field travel will follow SOS CV Somalia security clearance procedures and, where relevant, INSO access-negotiation protocols, with do-no-harm considerations governing the timing, location, and composition of all community consultations.
