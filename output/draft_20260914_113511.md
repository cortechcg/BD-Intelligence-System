## Introduction, Background & Conceptual Framework

5. INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND

5.1 Context and Strategic Importance

The countries of Kenya, South Sudan and Ethiopia share border regions marked by complex socio-economic, political and environmental challenges, and DCA, together with TUPADO (Kenya), ROG and ICPDO (South Sudan) and ECC (Ethiopia), is implementing the European Union-funded SPREAD programme (Strengthening Integrated Peace, Resilience and Disaster Risk Reduction for Cross-border Communities) across South Omo/Ethiopia, Turkana/Kenya and Eastern Equatoria/South Sudan, collectively the Karamoja Cluster. Now 42 months into implementation and in Year 3, SPREAD has combined peacebuilding, livelihoods, climate resilience and DRR interventions "rather than delivering them as parallel, siloed activities," introducing Community Action Groups (CAGs), Village Savings and Loan Associations (VSLAs), micro-grants and early warning systems alongside pre-existing market committees and savings groups. As the programme moves toward its concluding phase, DCA is commissioning a series of learning papers to critically examine specific thematic areas of SPREAD's work, and this assignment, Learning Paper 2, is the vehicle for documenting what integration has meant operationally and where it has added value beyond the sum of its parts. 5.2 Purpose and Objectives

The purpose of the assignment is to establish a shared, evidence-based understanding of what integration means and delivers in the SPREAD context and to translate this into actionable guidance for donors, IGAD and governments. The six stated objectives run from establishing a shared operational definition of integration and documenting sequencing and coordination between components, through examining trade-offs between livelihoods and climate resilience/adaptation, using community case studies of stronger and weaker integration, assessing how communities relate to SPREAD-introduced structures alongside pre-existing ones, and producing a learning paper and policy brief with recommendations on what should be integrated, at what level, and what can simply be coordinated. The sixth objective extends this into a dedicated policy brief on how the integrated peace-resilience approach contributes to local and regional stability and human security, targeted first at the EU and Danish policy audiences. 5.3 Our Interpretation of the Assignment

Cortech reads this assignment as a diagnostic and evidentiary exercise, not a standard programme evaluation: DCA is not asking whether SPREAD delivered its outputs but whether combining peacebuilding, livelihoods, climate resilience and DRR in one design produced value that separate, coordinated delivery would not have achieved, and where that value did not materialise. This requires a method built to trace contribution after the fact, comparing sites of stronger and weaker integration rather than measuring a single programme logic against its own targets. One constraint the documents imply but do not state explicitly is that because the consultant reports to the Project Consortium Manager with technical oversight from thematic leads who designed the interventions under review, the learning paper's requirement to document "weaker examples of integration" places the assignment in the position of assessing choices made by the same consortium managing it day to day; the case-study protocol and validation workshop design must therefore be structured to protect the credibility of critical findings, for instance by triangulating community testimony independently of thematic-lead framing before it reaches the draft learning paper. A second implied constraint is timing: with inception approval due 13 October 2026 and data collection required to close by 31 October across three countries, research assistants in Turkana, South Omo and Eastern Equatoria must be identified and briefed during the inception period itself, since recruitment, training and fielding cannot be sequenced as separate stages within the gap available. 5.4 Key Evaluation Questions

The proposed key learning questions to be finalised jointly with the consultant, and which this technical proposal addresses directly under the ToR sufficiency criterion, are: what does integration mean operationally in the SPREAD context and which sequencing approaches (peace preceding livelihood investment, economic cooperation opening space for dialogue, or natural-resource agreements preceding irrigation or agricultural investment) work best in which contexts; what challenges and trade-offs arise from integrating peace and resilience at community level and how this relates to reducing pressure on natural resources through diversified income opportunities; drawing on community case studies of stronger and weaker integration, why integrated programming worked better in some locations than others and what community members, leaders and local authorities identify as their preferences for greater integration; how SPREAD structures (CMDRR, CAGs, observatory platforms) have been perceived by communities and government and how they complement pre-existing market committees, peace committees and savings groups; which components of integrated peace, livelihoods, climate resilience and DRR programming genuinely need to be integrated, at what level, and which can simply be coordinated, and what this implies for future funding models including anticipatory action; and how the SPREAD integrated peace-resilience approach is positively contributing to stability and human security locally and regionally. 5.5 Deliverables

Cortech's work plan is built to produce the full named deliverable set on the dates DCA has specified: an inception report (desk review findings, methodology, case-study selection criteria, data collection tools, work plan and ethical/risk mitigation measures) and inception briefing with the SPREAD CMU and thematic leads; a draft learning paper and draft policy brief; a cross-border validation workshop; a final learning paper with SMART, evidence-based recommendations on integration versus coordination; a final concise policy brief on integration; a 4-8 page policy brief targeted at policymakers and donors, including the EU, Denmark, IGAD and other governments, on the relationship between integrated peace/resilience programming, human security and stability; case-study files and cleaned raw data, anonymised where required, annexed to the final report; an executive management summary of 2-4 pages and a summary PowerPoint presentation; and a facilitated results presentation and learning session with DCA and consortium partners. 5.6 Understanding of Success

Success on this assignment is measured by whether the final learning paper and both policy briefs give DCA, TUPADO, ICPDO, ROG, ECC, the EU, Denmark, IGAD and national governments a defensible, evidence-based answer to a decision they must make now, as SPREAD approaches its concluding phase: which components of peacebuilding, livelihoods, climate resilience and DRR genuinely require integration, at what organisational and geographic level, and which can be coordinated separately, and what this means for future funding models including anticipatory action. It is not sufficient to describe SPREAD's activities; the outputs must demonstrate, through paired stronger and weaker community case studies validated at a single cross-border workshop, where integration created value beyond the sum of its parts, so that the recommendations are usable rather than descriptive. Conceptual Framework

Cortech's analytical lens for this assignment combines three complementary layers, each chosen because it answers a distinct part of what DCA is asking rather than because it is a default evaluation tool. The first layer is Outcome Harvesting, which SPREAD already uses as its own reflection practice and which the ToR explicitly asks the consultant to build on. Outcome Harvesting does not begin from a predefined results chain; it identifies outcomes that have already occurred in the community, whether attributable to CAGs, VSLAs, CMDRRs, observatory platforms, market committees, peace committees or savings groups, and works backward to establish what combination of factors, including the sequencing of peacebuilding, livelihoods, climate resilience and DRR activity, actually produced them. This fits the assignment's core question directly, because the question is not whether SPREAD's logframe was achieved but whether integration itself was a causal ingredient. The second layer draws on OECD DAC evaluation criteria, applied selectively to the two dimensions the ToR is actually testing: coherence, to assess whether peacebuilding, livelihoods, climate resilience and DRR components reinforced or worked against each other at the same sites, and effectiveness, to assess whether the combined package produced outcomes that would not have occurred through parallel, uncoordinated delivery. Using DAC criteria this way keeps the analysis auditable and comparable across South Omo, Turkana and Eastern Equatoria without importing the full battery of criteria the assignment does not require. The third layer is a contribution-tracing structure applied specifically to the stronger-versus-weaker case-study design: for each paired site, the team will document the sequencing that occurred, the structures involved, and the community-reported outcome, then test alternative explanations (external factors, pre-existing structures acting alone, security context) before attributing added value to integration. This structure is what allows the learning paper to move from description to the SMART, evidence-based recommendations on integration versus coordination that DCA has specified as the final deliverable, and it is applied consistently across all three countries through the harmonised tools coordinated with TUPADO, ICPDO, ROG and ECC.

## Technical Methodology

METHODOLOGY

1. Overall Approach

Cortech Consulting Group's approach to Learning Paper 2 for SPREAD treats "integration" as an empirical question, not an assumption. The Terms of Reference are explicit that SPREAD was designed to combine peacebuilding, livelihoods, climate resilience and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) interventions "rather than delivering them as parallel, siloed activities," through structures such as Community Action Groups (CAGs), Village Savings and Loan Associations (VSLAs), micro-grants and early warning systems, layered onto pre-existing market committees and savings groups. Our methodology is built to establish, with community-level evidence from South Omo/Ethiopia, Turkana/Kenya and Eastern Equatoria/South Sudan, where this integration has added value beyond the sum of its parts, where it has not, and what this implies for future programming and financing models, including anticipatory action. The design is a mixed-methods, comparative case-study approach combining Key Informant Interviews (KIIs), Focus Group Discussions (FGDs) and participatory ranking exercises, built explicitly on SPREAD's existing Outcome Harvesting practice rather than introducing a parallel evaluative framework. This is a learning assignment, not a programme evaluation: the unit of analysis throughout is the interaction between peacebuilding, livelihoods, climate resilience and DRR components at community level, and the central comparative device is the pairing of stronger and weaker integration sites within and across the three countries. Data collection will be coordinated through the national partner teams, TUPADO (Kenya), ICPDO and ROG (South Sudan), and ECC (Ethiopia), using harmonised tools designed centrally by the Cortech team to guarantee comparability, rather than run as three independent national studies. All primary data will be captured on KoboToolbox for real-time monitoring of coverage across sites, disaggregated by sex, age and disability, and triangulated against the SPREAD baseline (2024), the Mid-Term Review (2025) and Year 3/MTR reflection workshop outputs. 2. ## Sampling Strategy & Data Analysis Plan

Sampling and Selection Logic

This assignment does not call for a probability sample. The Terms of Reference request community case studies, stronger and weaker examples of integration, and direct engagement with community members, leaders and local authorities rather than population-representative estimates, so Cortech will apply purposive selection throughout. Case-study sites in South Omo, Turkana and Eastern Equatoria will be selected jointly with TUPADO, ICPDO, ROG and ECC against explicit criteria set out in the inception report, including strength of observed integration (drawing on the 2025 Mid-Term Review and reflection workshop outputs), presence of CAGs, VSLAs and CMDRR structures, and coverage of livestock, fishing, farming and formal/informal cross-border trade (ICBT) livelihoods. Within each site, KII and FGD participants will be purposively drawn to represent CAG and VSLA members, traditional leaders, local/national authorities, market committee members, women- and youth-led CSOs, and both women/youth-led and mixed markets, with quota-based attention to sex, age and disability so that disaggregation is possible at the analysis stage rather than assumed at the sampling stage. This logic privileges information richness and contrast between stronger and weaker integration cases over statistical generalisability, consistent with a learning paper rather than a population-based evaluation. SECTION 2. DATA ANALYSIS PLAN

Quantitative inputs generated through the assignment, principally participatory ranking exercise scores and any structured indicators drawn from CAG, VSLA and market committee records, will be cleaned, coded and entered into SPSS. Analysis will be primarily descriptive, using frequencies and cross-tabulations to compare ranking patterns across South Omo, Turkana and Eastern Equatoria and across stronger versus weaker integration case-study sites, disaggregated by sex, age and disability as specified in the ToR. Where sample sizes and data structure allow, Cortech will apply non-parametric tests (for example chi-square comparisons) to assess whether differences in perceived integration value between sites are more than incidental, always reporting these results with appropriate caution given the case-study, non-probability design. Qualitative data from KIIs, FGDs and Outcome Harvesting-informed reflection sessions will be transcribed, cleaned and coded in NVivo using a coding framework built directly from the six proposed learning questions and the six assignment objectives, with dedicated nodes for CAG, VSLA, CMDRR and observatory-platform experience, sequencing choices, and trade-offs between livelihoods and climate resilience/adaptation. Thematic analysis will proceed by first-cycle descriptive coding, followed by pattern coding to surface convergence and divergence between stronger and weaker integration cases and across the three consortium partner geographies. Triangulation will bring the two strands together at the case-study level: ranking scores and any quantitative indicators will be read against the coded qualitative narrative for the same site to establish whether reported community preferences align with observed integration outcomes, and any divergence will be explicitly flagged and explored in the learning paper as a finding in its own right, in line with the requirement to explain limitations in the final report.

SECTION 2, ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS AND SAFEGUARDING

This assignment engages pastoralists/agro-pastoralists, fisherfolk, farmers and farmer groups, cross-border traders, women- and youth-led CSOs, traditional leaders, local and national authorities, and CAG and VSLA members across three fragile, conflict-affected border regions: South Omo (Ethiopia), Turkana (Kenya) and Eastern Equatoria (South Sudan). Cortech Consulting Group's ISO certification, child safeguarding policy and PSEA compliance apply directly to fieldwork with these populations, and all research assistants and enumerators recruited and trained for this assignment, including Edwin Aldrin Adenya, will sign confidentiality undertakings and receive briefing on DCA's Code of Conduct for Contractors before deployment. Given the conflict-sensitivity of questions on peacebuilding structures and natural-resource pressure, data collection will follow a do-no-harm approach: KIIs and FGDs will avoid probing on active disputes in ways that could expose respondents or CAG/VSLA members to retaliation, and interviewers will be trained to recognise and refer protection concerns rather than record them as data points. Informed consent will be obtained verbally or in writing depending on literacy levels and local context, with respondents told the purpose of the learning paper, the intended audiences (the European Union, Denmark, IGAD and governments), the voluntary nature of participation, and their right to withdraw without consequence, before any KII, FGD or participatory ranking exercise begins. Particular care will be taken with traditional leaders and local authorities, whose views on integration versus coordination may carry political sensitivity across the Kenya-Ethiopia-South Sudan border, and with women- and youth-led CSOs, where separate or single-sex facilitation will be used where appropriate to support open participation. All case-study files and raw data will be anonymised prior to annexation to the final report, consistent with the ToR's requirement for "cleaned raw data (anonymised where required)," and data will be stored and transmitted according to Cortech's data protection procedures, with access limited to the assignment team and CMU. Disaggregation by sex, age and disability will be built into the sampling frame from the inception stage, not applied retrospectively. Risk management on this assignment is treated as a continuous function of the Lead Consultant, exercised at each stage of the twelve-week sequence rather than as a one-off inception exercise.
Given that fieldwork spans three national jurisdictions with distinct security, administrative and linguistic conditions, the primary control is structural: routing all access, scheduling and stakeholder engagement decisions through the established national partner teams, TUPADO in Kenya, ICPDO and ROG in South Sudan, and ECC in Ethiopia, so that local knowledge of the operating environment in South Omo, Turkana and Eastern Equatoria informs field planning in real time rather than being retrofitted after a problem arises.
Risks are logged and reviewed at each milestone gate, inception approval, completion of data collection, draft submission and the cross-border validation workshop, with the Consortium Management Unit informed promptly of any material change that could affect the 15 December 2026 delivery date.
The second pillar of the approach is methodological discipline aimed specifically at protecting the credibility of the integration versus coordination findings that the learning paper must deliver.
Because the assignment's value rests on being able to compare stronger and weaker examples of SPREAD integration across CAGs, VSLAs and CMDRR structures, controls against sampling bias, response bias and cross-country inconsistency are treated as core risk items, not secondary quality checks.
Sensitive topics, including community perceptions of conflict, resource pressure and institutional trust, are handled through conflict-sensitive, GESI-aware facilitation protocols agreed with SPREAD technical and MEAL staff during inception, ensuring that data collection itself does no harm in an active cross-border conflict-affected environment.

## Work Plan and Implementation Schedule

Cortech Consulting Group will deliver Learning Paper 2 for SPREAD over the approximately 12-week period specified in the Terms of Reference, running from consultant on-boarding on 29 September 2026 through final deliverable submission on 15 December 2026.
The assignment is structured as five sequential phases, each gated by an approval or milestone stated in the ToR, with field-level activities in South Omo, Turkana and Eastern Equatoria coordinated in parallel through the national partner teams to keep the overall schedule intact.
This phase refines the case-study selection criteria, KII/FGD guides and community case-study protocol in consultation with SPREAD technical and MEAL staff, and finalises the sampling frame across peacebuilding/resilience structures, livestock, fishing, farming and cross-border trade (ICBT).
Output: Inception Report, including methodology, sampling justification, work plan and ethical/risk mitigation measures, followed by an Inception Briefing with the Consortium Management Unit (CMU) and thematic leads.
This phase proceeds to field data collection only once the Inception Report and tools are approved, matching the ToR's 13 October 2026 milestone.
**Phase 2: Field Data Collection (Weeks 3-5, 14 to 31 October 2026)**
Data collection is coordinated across Kenya (TUPADO), South Sudan (ICPDO, ROG) and Ethiopia (ECC) using harmonised tools to guarantee cross-country comparability.
Research Assistant/Enumerator Edwin Aldrin Adenya will support recruitment and training of country-based enumerators, with KIIs and FGDs conducted with pastoralists/agro-pastoralists, fisherfolk, farmers and farmer groups, formal and informal cross-border traders, CAG and VSLA members, women- and youth-led CSOs, traditional leaders, and local/national authorities, disaggregated by sex, age and disability.
Community case studies are selected in matched pairs of stronger and weaker integration sites to compare how CAGs, VSLAs, CMDRRs and observatory platforms interact with pre-existing market committees, peace committees and savings groups.
This phase runs in parallel across the three countries rather than sequentially by country, closing by 31 October 2026 as fixed in the ToR.
**Phase 3: Analysis and Draft Reporting (Weeks 6-8, 1 to 21 November 2026)**
Data is cleaned, triangulated against earlier SPREAD assessments and analysed jointly with a review session with the SPREAD team to deepen understanding of findings, drawing on SPREAD's Outcome Harvesting practice.
This phase runs the drafting of the learning paper and the two policy briefs in parallel: one on integration versus coordination for donors, IGAD and governments, and one on human security and regional stability for EU and Danish policymakers with onward framing for a wider public.
Output: draft learning paper and both draft policy briefs, submitted to DCA by 21 November 2026 for review by the CMU and relevant thematic leads.
**Phase 4: Validation (Weeks 9-10, 22 November to 3 December 2026)**
Following DCA and CMU review comments, Cortech facilitates the single cross-border validation workshop bringing together Consortium members (TUPADO, ICPDO, ROG, ECC), IGAD and other key stakeholders from all three countries, held by 3 December 2026 as specified in the ToR.
**Phase 5: Finalisation and Delivery (Weeks 11-12, 4 to 15 December 2026)**
Validation feedback is incorporated into the final learning paper with SMART, evidence-based recommendations on integration versus coordination, both final policy briefs, anonymised case-study files and cleaned raw data, the executive management summary and summary PowerPoint, concluding with a facilitated results presentation and learning session with DCA and consortium partners, all delivered by 15 December 2026.
### Sequencing Note

Inception, analysis/drafting, and finalisation are sequential and gated by DCA/CMU approval, in line with the ToR requirement that field work begin only after tool approval.
Within Phase 2, the three-country field programmes run in parallel under harmonised protocols, and within Phase 3, the learning paper and both policy briefs are drafted concurrently by the same evidence base to keep their distinct audiences and messaging aligned without delaying either output.
