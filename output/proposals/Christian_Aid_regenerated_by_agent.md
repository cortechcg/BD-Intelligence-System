## Introduction And Framework

# SECTION 1 — INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND

## 5.1 Context and Strategic Importance

Cortech understands that the "Catalyzing Multilateral Development Banks Financing for Energy access in Africa" project sits at a pivotal juncture for Christian Aid's engagement with the African Development Bank (AfDB) and other Multilateral Development Banks (MDBs). Implemented over January 2024 to December 2026 with support from the Charles Stewart Mott Foundation, the project has sought to shift AfDB policy, practice and investment away from fossil fuel dependency towards decentralised sustainable renewable energy, working through two national partners — the Kenya Climate Change Working Group (KCCWG) and the Civil Society Network on Climate Change (CISONECC) — to strengthen civil society voice around Country Strategy Papers, Mission 300 Energy Compacts and other AfDB decision-making processes. The end-of-project evaluation is being commissioned at a moment when Christian Aid needs credible, evidence-based learning to shape its next phase of advocacy, partnerships and MDB engagement, making this assignment as much a strategic planning input as a compliance exercise.

## 5.2 Purpose and Objectives

The evaluation's stated purpose is to assess the extent to which the Energy Access Project achieved its intended objectives, outcomes and results, and to generate evidence-based learning to inform future programming, advocacy, partnerships and engagement with the AfDB. Cortech notes that the ToR explicitly frames this as serving two functions simultaneously: an accountability function — assessing what was achieved against planned results — and a learning function — identifying what worked, what did not, why, and how future interventions can be improved. The six specific objectives set out in Section 3.0 of the Terms of Reference, spanning AfDB policy contribution, civil society coordination and advocacy capacity, value-for-money of engagement modalities, GESI considerations, sustainability enablers and risks, and actionable recommendations, will each be treated as a discrete line of enquiry in Cortech's evaluation matrix rather than folded into a single narrative account.

## 5.3 Our Interpretation of the Assignment

Cortech interprets this assignment as a contribution-based, mixed-methods evaluation that must anchor its effectiveness findings in the project's own logframe and Theory of Change, rather than a purely narrative account of outputs — a distinction the ToR itself draws in Section 6.0. A constraint the documents imply but do not spell out is a timing tension: the project's implementation period runs through December 2026, yet the evaluation's indicative timeframe (Section 8.0) places data collection and reporting entirely within September–October 2026. This means the evaluation will necessarily assess a project still in its final implementation quarter, and Cortech will need to distinguish, in its findings, between results that are fully closed out and those that are still in motion — a nuance the inception report will make explicit to avoid overstating "achievement" on indicators tied to activities scheduled for the final months of the project.

## 5.4 Key Evaluation Questions

Cortech will structure the evaluation matrix directly around the eight lines of enquiry set out in Section 5.0 of the ToR: Effectiveness ("to what extent were the project's results, per the Theory of Change, achieved against the indicators and targets set out in the logframe?" and "which interventions were most effective in influencing AfDB policy and advocacy processes?"); Relevance ("how well did the project fit the priorities, capacity needs and advocacy opportunities of African CSOs, coalitions and faith actors... and how well did it adapt as AfDB and MDB priorities shifted?"); Coherence and partnership (internal reinforcement across convenings, dialogues, CSO positioning, AfDB engagement and the learning platform, and external alignment with other AfDB/MDB advocacy coalitions, national energy policy and NDC processes); Sustainability and learning ("what results, partnerships, advocacy outcomes, knowledge products or coordination mechanisms are likely to continue beyond the project period?"); Efficiency (whether funding, staff time and KCCWG/CISONECC partner capacity were converted into outputs and outcomes in a timely, economical way, and whether more cost-efficient modalities existed); Impact and contribution (the extent to which the project contributed to observed changes in AfDB/MDB policies, strategies, financing priorities and public narratives on DRE, and what alternative or contributing factors should be accounted for); Sustainability (which results, partnerships or coordination mechanisms such as the learning platform are likely to continue, and what would be needed to sustain them); and the Cross-Cutting GESI lens (how gender equality and social inclusion were considered in design, advocacy positions and stakeholder engagement, and whether participation and outcome data were disaggregated).

Cortech will apply the analytical architecture the ToR itself prescribes in Section 5.0: the OECD-DAC evaluation criteria, a Theory of Change validation question, and a GESI cross-cutting lens, elaborated into a full evaluation matrix in the inception report as required by Section 6.0. The Theory of Change validation strand is treated as the connective layer across the matrix rather than a standalone exercise. Because attribution of AfDB and MDB policy shifts to a single project is inherently difficult to isolate, given the presence of other advocacy coalitions and external events, Cortech will apply outcome harvesting and contribution analysis as named in the ToR, rather than a simple before-after comparison. Outcome harvesting will be used to identify actual changes in AfDB Country Strategy Papers, Mission 300 Country Compacts, stakeholder positions or public narratives and work backwards to establish plausible project contribution; contribution analysis will then be used to construct and test a contribution story against alternative explanatory factors, satisfying the ToR's explicit instruction that the evaluation "explain not just what changed but why." GESI is applied as a cross-cutting lens throughout — disaggregating the stakeholder mapping matrix by country, stakeholder type and gender — rather than confined to a single evaluation question, consistent with how the ToR positions gender equality and social inclusion as a lens across design, advocacy positions and stakeholder engagement.

## Methodology

# 4. Proposed Approach and Methodology

## 4.1 Overall Approach

Cortech Consulting Group proposes a participatory, mixed-methods evaluation design built around the eight evaluation questions set out in Section 5.0 of the Terms of Reference — Effectiveness, Relevance, Coherence and Partnership, Sustainability and Learning, Efficiency, Impact and Contribution, Sustainability, and the cross-cutting GESI lens. Rather than producing a narrative account of activities delivered, the evaluation will be anchored throughout in the project's logframe and Theory of Change, testing observed results against the specific indicators and targets set for the two project objectives: strengthening civil society engagement with AfDB and other Multilateral Development Banks (MDBs) for enhanced just and accountable financing of decentralized sustainable renewable energy in Africa, and increased di-investment of public finance away from fossil fuels and uptake of locally led sustainable renewable energy systems.

Because the project's central claim — that civil society and faith actor engagement shifted AfDB policy, financing priorities and public narratives on decentralised renewable energy (DRE) — involves an attribution challenge that simple before-after comparison cannot resolve, Cortech will apply contribution analysis as the primary method for the Impact and Contribution and Effectiveness questions, supplemented by outcome harvesting where policy or advocacy changes were not explicitly predicted in the Theory of Change (for example, unanticipated shifts in AfDB Country Strategy Papers or Mission 300 Country Compacts language). This dual approach allows the evaluation to test the Theory of Change's critical assumptions directly, distinguishing the project's contribution from the roles played by other actors and external events such as parallel MDB reform coalitions or national NDC processes.

The design recognises that the evaluation must serve stakeholders across two distinct national contexts — Kenya, where the Kenya Climate Change Working Group (KCCWG) has led implementation, and Malawi, where the Civil Society Network on Climate Change (CISONECC) has led implementation — and across stakeholder categories that include CSOs, faith actors, AfDB officials, private-sector actors, media and government representatives. All data collection instruments will therefore be built around a stakeholder mapping matrix disaggregated by country, stakeholder type and gender, ensuring the GESI lens is embedded in sampling and analysis rather than treated as a separate section.

## 4.2 Phase-by-Phase Breakdown

**Phase 1 — Contracting, Inception and Document Review (Weeks 1–3).** Following contracting and an initial briefing with Christian Aid's project lead and Monitoring and Evaluation lead in Weeks 1–2, the Lead Evaluation Consultant will conduct a structured review of the project proposal, logframe, Theory of Change, annual technical and donor reports to Charles Stewart Mott Foundation, research and policy briefs, meeting reports from AfDB annual meetings and CSO forums, partner reports from KCCWG and CISONECC, media outputs and relevant AfDB policy frameworks, including Mission 300 Country Compacts and Country Strategy Papers for Kenya and Malawi. This review produces the Inception Report in Week 3, containing the full evaluation matrix linking each OECD-DAC criterion and Section 5.0 question to sub-questions, indicators, data sources and methods; the stakeholder mapping matrix; data collection tools; and the confidentiality and consent protocol.

**Phase 2 — Data Collection and Stakeholder Consultations (Weeks 4–5).** Primary data collection will combine key informant interviews (KIIs) and, where feasible, focus group discussions (FGDs) with CSOs, faith actors, AfDB officials, private-sector actors, media and government representatives in Kenya and Malawi, together with KCCWG and CISONECC staff and partner organisations. All instruments will be digitised in KoboToolbox for structured capture of participant metadata (country, stakeholder type, gender, role) and closed-response items, enabling real-time tracking of sample coverage against the mapping matrix.

**Phase 3 — Data Analysis and Draft Report (Weeks 5–6).** Qualitative transcripts from KIIs and FGDs will be coded thematically in NVivo against the evaluation matrix's sub-questions, with nodes structured around the OECD-DAC criteria to allow cross-case comparison between Kenya and Malawi. Quantitative and disaggregated participation data exported from KoboToolbox will be analysed in SPSS to produce frequency and cross-tabulation outputs by country, stakeholder type and gender, informing the GESI assessment. Contribution analysis and outcome harvesting findings will be synthesised into the Draft Evaluation Report.

**Phase 4 — Validation and Finalisation (Weeks 7–8).** Emerging findings will be presented at a Validation Session with Christian Aid and selected partners, generating a documented record of feedback that is incorporated into the Final Evaluation Report, Executive Summary and Learning Brief delivered in Week 8.

## 4.3 Deliverable-Specific Methods

**Inception Report (Week 3).** Method: desk review of the documents listed above plus construction of the evaluation matrix and stakeholder mapping matrix. Respondents: none (document-based); informed by preliminary scoping calls with Christian Aid's project lead and M&E lead. Output: Inception Report containing methodology, workplan, data collection tools, evaluation matrix and ethics protocol, submitted for Christian Aid's approval before fieldwork commences.

**Draft Evaluation Report (Weeks 5–6).** Method: semi-structured KIIs (approximately 45–60 minutes) with KCCWG and CISONECC staff, AfDB officials engaged during the project period, government departments in Kenya and Malawi, faith actors, media contacts who covered DRE stories, and private-sector actors identified through the project's convenings; FGDs with community-level or off-grid/marginalised stakeholder representatives where the project engaged them directly; document-based contribution analysis tracing indicator-level results in the logframe to specific project interventions (policy research, multi-stakeholder dialogues, capacity building, AfDB annual meeting engagement, Country Strategy Paper submissions). Instruments: KII and FGD guides structured around the eight Section 5.0 questions, administered and logged via KoboToolbox; NVivo coding for thematic synthesis. Output: Draft Evaluation Report with preliminary findings, conclusions and recommendations against each OECD-DAC criterion.

**Validation Session (Week 7).** Method: facilitated workshop with Christian Aid and selected partners (including KCCWG and CISONECC representatives) presenting emerging findings for verification, challenge and correction. Output: a validation record capturing agreed adjustments, feeding directly into the Final Evaluation Report.

**Final Evaluation Report, Executive Summary and Learning Brief (Week 8).** Method: incorporation of validation feedback into the report structure, cross-checked against the evaluation matrix to confirm every Section 5.0 question has been addressed with supporting evidence. Output: Final Evaluation Report with evidence-based conclusions and lessons learned; a concise Executive Summary; and a Learning Brief distilling advocacy lessons and recommendations for Christian Aid, KCCWG, CISONECC and wider civil society actors on future engagement with AfDB and other MDBs.

## 4.4 Data Quality Assurance

Data quality will be assured through triangulation across at least three sources for each major finding — document review, KII/FGD testimony and, where available, quantitative participation or media-coverage data — before a finding is treated as validated. KoboToolbox form logic will include mandatory disaggregation fields (country, stakeholder type, gender) to prevent incomplete records reaching analysis. NVivo coding will be conducted by the Lead Evaluation Consultant with a second coder cross-checking a sample of transcripts to confirm coding consistency against the evaluation matrix's sub-questions. SPSS outputs will be checked for internal consistency against KoboToolbox raw exports prior to inclusion in the Draft Evaluation Report. All findings presented in the Draft Evaluation Report will be explicitly flagged as preliminary pending the Validation Session, ensuring the final account reflects a shared and credible understanding among Christian Aid and partners rather than the evaluator's inference alone.

## 4.5 Ethical Considerations

Given that respondents will include AfDB officials, government representatives and media actors, informed consent will be obtained verbally or in writing before each KII/FGD, with the purpose of the evaluation and intended use of contributions explained in full. Respondent confidentiality will be protected through anonymised reporting by default, with direct attribution used only where explicit consent has been given and recorded in KoboToolbox. All raw data — recordings, transcripts and KoboToolbox exports — will be stored on password-protected, access-restricted systems and retained only for the duration necessary to complete the assignment. The evaluation will adhere to OECD-DAC/UNEG evaluation ethics norms throughout, and the confidentiality, data-storage and anonymisation protocols will be set out in full in the Inception Report for Christian Aid's review before data collection begins.

## Analysis Plan

## SECTION 1 — SAMPLING AND PARTICIPANT SELECTION LOGIC

This assignment does not involve a survey of a defined population and therefore does not require a probability sample or a stated margin of error. The evidence base rests instead on a purposively selected set of key informants and focus group participants who hold direct knowledge of the project's design, implementation and influence on AfDB and other Multilateral Development Banks (MDBs). Cortech will build a stakeholder mapping matrix, as called for in Section 6.0 of the Terms of Reference, disaggregated by country (Kenya and Malawi), stakeholder type (CSO, faith actor, AfDB official, private sector, media and government) and gender. Respondents within each cell of the matrix will be selected purposively in consultation with Christian Aid's project lead, Kenya Climate Change Working Group (KCCWG) and Civil Society Network on Climate Change (CISONECC), prioritising individuals who were directly engaged in convenings, multi-stakeholder dialogues, AfDB Country Strategy Papers processes, Mission 300 Country Compacts engagement, or the learning platform, and who can therefore speak with authority to specific Theory of Change pathways and logframe indicators. Where a stakeholder category proves difficult to reach directly, for example AfDB officials or government representatives at Country Strategy Paper level, Cortech will use a short referral chain beginning with contacts identified by Christian Aid and KCCWG/CISONECC and extending only as far as needed to secure at least one respondent per country per stakeholder category. This logic privileges depth of insight and evidentiary relevance for outcome harvesting and contribution analysis over numerical representativeness, which is appropriate for an evaluation whose central task is to trace and substantiate a project's contribution to policy and advocacy change rather than to estimate a population parameter.

## SECTION 2 — DATA ANALYSIS PLAN

Quantitative data for this evaluation will be limited to logframe indicator values, participation records from convenings and dialogues, and disaggregated stakeholder engagement data (by country, stakeholder type and gender). These will be entered and analysed in SPSS to produce descriptive statistics, principally frequencies, proportions and trends against baseline and end-of-project targets, allowing the team to state precisely where results per the Theory of Change met, exceeded or fell short of the logframe targets, and to disaggregate participation in convenings and dialogues by gender and stakeholder type as required by the GESI cross-cutting lens. This quantitative layer anchors the effectiveness assessment in measurable results rather than narrative alone, consistent with Section 6.0's instruction that the review "underpin an assessment of achievement against the specific indicators and targets."

Qualitative data from key informant interviews, focus group discussions and the document review, including project proposals, annual technical and donor reports, policy briefs and AfDB policy frameworks, will be transcribed, coded and analysed in NVivo using a coding framework built directly from the evaluation matrix: each OECD-DAC criterion (effectiveness, relevance, coherence and partnership, sustainability and learning, efficiency, impact and contribution, sustainability) and the GESI cross-cutting lens will form a top-level node, with sub-nodes for Theory of Change assumptions and the specific evaluation questions in Section 5.0. Thematic analysis will identify recurring enabling factors, challenges and change narratives, which will then feed into outcome harvesting or contribution analysis to test attribution of observed shifts in AfDB and MDB policy, financing priorities and stakeholder positions.

Triangulation will occur at the evaluation-matrix level: for each sub-question, findings from SPSS-analysed indicator and participation data, NVivo-coded interview and FGD themes, and document review evidence will be compared side by side, with convergence strengthening confidence in a finding and divergence flagged for clarification during the validation session with Christian Aid and selected partners prior to finalisation of the report.

At **Document Review and Inception Report (Week 3)**, all data collection instruments — KII guides, FGD protocols, and the stakeholder mapping matrix disaggregated by country (Kenya and Malawi), stakeholder type (CSO, faith actor, AfDB official, private sector, media, government) and gender — will undergo internal peer review by a second Cortech evaluator not involved in instrument design, checking alignment with logframe indicators and avoidance of leading questions. The Inception Report will require written sign-off from Christian Aid before Data Collection begins, constituting the first formal client feedback loop.

During **Data Collection and Stakeholder Consultations (Weeks 4–5)**, daily debrief calls between the Lead Consultant and field-based researchers in Kenya and Malawi will cross-check KII notes against the stakeholder mapping matrix to confirm sample representativeness, with gaps in AfDB official, government or media respondent categories flagged for corrective follow-up within the same week.

At **Data Analysis and Draft Report (Weeks 5–6)**, qualitative data coded in NVivo will be reviewed by a second analyst to confirm that outcome harvesting and contribution analysis claims are traceable to at least two independent sources before being logged as findings, with the Draft Evaluation Report passing internal technical review before submission.

The **Validation Meeting (Week 7)** constitutes a structured client feedback gate, testing emerging findings against the lived understanding of Christian Aid, KCCWG and CISONECC, before the **Final Report and Learning Brief (Week 8)** are finalised.

# SECTION 2 — ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS AND SAFEGUARDING

Cortech Consulting Group's engagement on this evaluation will operate under its ISO-certified quality management system, together with its Child Safeguarding Policy and PSEA (Protection from Sexual Exploitation and Abuse) Policy, both binding on every team member and sub-consultant engaged on this assignment regardless of role or duration of engagement.

Section 10 of the Terms of Reference requires that "informed consent is obtained from all interview and focus-group participants, clearly explaining the purpose of the evaluation and how their contributions will be used." Cortech will operationalise this through a written and verbal consent script, administered before every KII and FGD in Kenya and Malawi, explaining the evaluation's purpose, voluntary participation, and the respondent's right to withdraw or decline specific questions, with consent records logged separately from response data.

Because respondents include AfDB officials, government representatives and media actors, as flagged in Section 10, the Inception Report will set out a tiered confidentiality protocol: interview notes will be anonymised by respondent code rather than name in all working files; direct quotation or attribution in the Final Evaluation Report will occur only where explicit, written consent has been obtained; and raw data will be stored on password-protected, access-restricted drives accessible only to the core evaluation team, consistent with the OECD-DAC/UNEG evaluation ethics norms referenced in Section 9 and Section 10.

The GESI cross-cutting lens set out in Section 5.0 requires attention to representation of women, youth and off-grid/marginalised communities in convenings and dialogues. Cortech's Child Safeguarding Policy will govern any community-level consultation in Kenya or Malawi involving a respondent under 18, requiring guardian consent and age-appropriate facilitation, while the PSEA Policy will govern field team conduct toward all community respondents, including off-grid and marginalised groups identified through the stakeholder mapping matrix.

Do-no-harm principles will guide question design to avoid re-traumatisation or reputational exposure of CSO, faith actor and government respondents discussing sensitive AfDB engagement experiences, with any safeguarding concern arising during fieldwork escalated immediately to Christian Aid's project lead under a documented referral procedure.

## Work Plan

# Work Plan and Timeline

## Narrative

The work plan is structured around the eight-week indicative timeframe fixed in Section 8.0 of the Terms of Reference, running from Week 1 (contracting and initial briefing) to Week 8 (final report and learning brief). Cortech's phasing follows the sequence the client has already specified — document review and inception, data collection and stakeholder consultations, analysis and drafting, validation, and finalisation — rather than substituting a generic evaluation cycle. Each phase closes with a defined output that gates entry into the next, ensuring that the evaluation of the Catalyzing Multilateral Development Banks Financing for Energy access in Africa project remains anchored to the logframe and Theory of Change throughout, and not to a narrative account assembled only at the end.

**Phase 1: Contracting, Briefing and Document Review (Weeks 1–3).** Following contract signature, the Lead Evaluation Consultant will hold an initial briefing with Christian Aid's project lead and the Monitoring and Evaluation lead to confirm reporting lines, access to project documents, and introductions to KCCWG and CISONECC. Document review will draw on the project proposal, logframe, Theory of Change, annual technical and donor reports, research and policy briefs, meeting reports, partner reports, media outputs, and relevant AfDB frameworks including Mission 300 Country Compacts and Country Strategy Papers. This review underpins the Inception Report, which sets out the full evaluation matrix linking each OECD-DAC criterion and Section 5.0 question to sub-questions, indicators, data sources and methods, together with the stakeholder mapping matrix disaggregated by country (Kenya and Malawi), stakeholder type (CSO, faith actor, AfDB official, private sector, media, government) and gender. The Inception Report is delivered at the end of Week 3, as the ToR requires.

**Phase 2: Data Collection and Stakeholder Consultations (Weeks 4–5).** Key informant interviews and, where relevant, focus group discussions proceed concurrently in Kenya and Malawi, coordinated with KCCWG and CISONECC respectively. Interviews with AfDB officials, government representatives and media actors follow the informed-consent and confidentiality protocol set out in the Inception Report, consistent with OECD-DAC/UNEG evaluation ethics norms. Fieldwork in the two countries runs in parallel rather than sequentially, given the eight-week ceiling, with the Lead Consultant supported by a country-based researcher in each location to maintain pace without compromising sample coverage across CSO, faith actor, private sector and government respondents.

**Phase 3: Data Analysis and Draft Report (Weeks 5–6).** Analysis begins as fieldwork data becomes available, overlapping with the tail end of Phase 2 to protect the overall timeline. Outcome harvesting and contribution analysis are applied to test the assumptions underlying the project's Theory of Change and to assess contribution to AfDB and MDB policy and financing shifts, distinguishing the project's contribution from other actors and external events. Achievement is assessed against the specific indicators and targets in the logframe, addressing effectiveness, relevance, coherence and partnership, sustainability and learning, efficiency, impact and contribution, and the GESI cross-cutting lens. The Draft Evaluation Report is produced by the end of Week 6.

**Phase 4: Validation (Week 7).** Emerging findings are presented to Christian Aid and selected partners in a validation session, a distinct gate rather than a step folded into reporting. This session tests preliminary conclusions against stakeholders' lived understanding of the project before findings are locked, consistent with Section 6.0's requirement that conclusions be checked and shared.

**Phase 5: Finalisation (Week 8).** Validation feedback is incorporated into the Final Evaluation Report, accompanied by the Executive Summary and the short learning brief highlighting advocacy lessons and recommendations for Christian Aid, partners and civil society coalitions engaging the AfDB and other MDBs.

Document review, tool design, and stakeholder mapping in Phase 1 proceed largely in sequence, since the matrix must be approved before fieldwork begins; within Phases 2 and 3, however, Kenya and Malawi data collection, and early-stage analysis, run in parallel to preserve the eight-week window.
