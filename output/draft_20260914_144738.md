## Technical proposal summarizing previous related experience, understanding of the TOR, key research questions, methodology and tools to be used

METHODOLOGY

1. Overall Approach

Cortech Consulting Group proposes a learning-focused, mixed-methods design that treats "integration" as the central unit of analysis, not a background descriptor. SPREAD is now in its third year of implementation and moving toward its concluding phase; the assignment is therefore not a standard evaluation of outputs but a structured process of extracting operational meaning from three years of programme experience across Turkana/Kenya, South Omo/Ethiopia and Eastern Equatoria/South Sudan, the Karamoja Cluster cross-border zone. The methodology is designed to answer directly what the Terms of Reference itself asks: what integration has meant operationally in SPREAD's design and inception, when it has added value beyond the sum of Peacebuilding, Livelihoods, Climate Resilience and Disaster Risk Reduction (DRR) delivered in parallel, what trade-offs this has produced, and what this implies for future funding and programming models. The design combines document-based reconstruction of SPREAD's integration logic with field-based testing of that logic against lived community experience. It builds explicitly on SPREAD's existing Outcome Harvesting practice rather than introducing a parallel evaluative framework, and it is structured around community case studies that will deliberately include both stronger and weaker examples of integration, as required by the ToR, so that the paper can explain contextual variation rather than assert a single success narrative. The methodology is anchored on four analytical moves that map directly to the six proposed key learning questions: (i) establishing a shared operational definition of integration through desk review and stakeholder engagement; (ii) mapping sequencing patterns, including whether peace preceded livelihood investment, whether economic cooperation opened space for dialogue, or whether natural-resource agreements preceded irrigation or agricultural investment; (iii) surfacing challenges and trade-offs, including the relationship between livelihoods diversification and reduced pressure on natural resources; and (iv) assessing how communities relate to SPREAD-introduced structures, Community Action Groups (CAGs), Village Savings and Loan Associations (VSLAs), micro-grants and early warning systems, alongside pre-existing structures such as market committees, peace committees and savings groups. 2. Phase-by-Phase Breakdown

Phase 1, Inception (weeks 1 to 2, targeting the 13 October 2026 milestone). This review will produce a working operational definition of integration and a first map of sequencing hypotheses to be tested in the field. In parallel, the consultant will develop case-study selection criteria, KII and FGD guides, and a community case-study protocol in consultation with SPREAD technical and MEAL staff, satisfying Expected Task 2 of the ToR. All instruments will be piloted in KoboToolbox before use. Phase 2, Field Data Collection (October, through 31 October 2026). Data collection will be coordinated across the three countries through the national partner teams, TUPADO in Kenya, ICPDO and ROG in South Sudan, and ECC in Ethiopia, using harmonised Kobo tools to preserve cross-country comparability. Edwin Aldrin Adenya, supported by locally recruited research assistants trained by the Lead Consultant, will support enumeration and FGD/KII facilitation in line with the ToR's administrative provision that the consultant recruit and train research assistants directly. Phase 3, Analysis and Quality Review (early to mid-November 2026). Qualitative data (KII and FGD transcripts, case-study narratives) will be coded in NVivo against a codebook built around the four analytical moves above. Quantitative and ranking-exercise data will be cleaned and analysed in SPSS, disaggregated by sex, age and disability. A dedicated review session with the SPREAD team, satisfying Expected Task 4, will be held to deepen understanding of emerging findings before drafting begins. Phase 4, Reporting and Validation (21 November to 15 December 2026). A draft learning paper and draft policy briefs will be prepared and discussed with the Consortium Management Unit (CMU) and relevant thematic leads, followed by a single cross-border validation workshop bringing together Consortium members, IGAD and other stakeholders, and a final revision cycle incorporating validation feedback. 3. Respondents: SPREAD CMU and thematic leads (consultation only, no primary data). Output: inception report covering desk review findings, methodology, case-study selection criteria, data collection tools, work plan and ethical/risk mitigation measures, presented at an inception briefing. Field Data Collection. Instruments: KIIs with traditional leaders, local and national authorities, and private-sector actors; FGDs with CAG and VSLA members, farmers and farmer groups, fisherfolk, and women- and youth-led CSOs; participatory ranking exercises with mixed and women/youth-led markets. Sampling will span all three countries and cover peacebuilding and resilience structures, livestock, fishing, farming and formal and informal cross-border trade (ICBT). Output: cleaned Kobo datasets and case-study narrative files per country. Draft and Final Learning Paper. Instrument: triangulated analysis across NVivo-coded qualitative data, SPSS-analysed ranking and disaggregated data, and the Phase 1 desk review, structured around the six key learning questions. Output: validated findings feeding the final report, by 3 December 2026. Case-Study Files, Executive Summary, PowerPoint and Learning Session.
