Technical Proposal ## Research design and methods Cortech Consulting Group proposes an evaluation and beneficiary verification design built around two distinct, grant-specific evaluation matrices rather than a single generic instrument. Grant 1, implemented in Southwest State (Baidoa and Hudur), and Grant 2, implemented in Hirshabelle (Jowhar and Adan Yabaal, Matabaan), pursue different theories of change, reach different population groups, and operate under different security and access conditions. Our approach therefore separates the two grants at every stage of the design, from the evaluation matrix through to sampling, tool design, fieldwork sequencing, and reporting, while allowing shared quality-assurance protocols to run across both. **Evaluation matrix.** For each grant, Cortech will construct an evaluation matrix that maps the seven OECD-DAC criteria, in the sequence Relevance, Coherence, Effectiveness, Efficiency, Impact/emerging impact, Sustainability, and Data Integrity, together with Beneficiary Verification as a distinct assessment strand, to specific data sources, tools, sampling considerations, analysis methods, and stated limitations. For Grant 1, Relevance will be assessed against the verifiable selection process for peace committee members, elders and local leaders, participants of reconciliation/integration activities, youth bloggers, vulnerable individuals (unemployed youth, women, former Al-Shabaab members, IDPs and minority clans), and local government officials across Baidoa and Hudur, drawing on grantee selection criteria, community consultation records and key informant interviews. For Grant 2, the equivalent Relevance line will examine how women and youth leaders, community member participants, and media outreach audiences were identified across Jowhar, Adan Yabaal and Matabaan. Coherence will be assessed through document review of GCERF's investment strategy and interviews with other actors operating in the same P/CVE, social cohesion and livelihoods space in Southwest State and Hirshabelle, to establish complementarity and avoid duplication. Effectiveness will compare reported achievement against each grant's own outcome structure (Outcomes A, B and C as set out in the grant programme descriptions) using outcome-level indicators, output data, and triangulated qualitative evidence on plausibility. Efficiency will examine whether the number of verified beneficiaries is consistent with resources deployed and implementation arrangements, particularly given the operational constraints of working in Baidoa, Hudur, Jowhar, Adan Yabaal and Matabaan. Impact/emerging impact will draw on perception and behaviour-change data collected through key informant interviews and focus group discussions with peace committee members, elders, women and youth leaders, and trauma-recovery participants. Sustainability will specifically probe, for both grants' livelihoods and vocational/TVET components, whether beneficiaries are still generating income without project support and whether observable linkages to markets, financial services or ongoing mentorship exist. Data Integrity will assess how beneficiary counting was conducted and documented over time in each grant's monitoring system, identifying gaps or inconsistencies and their likely cause and effect on reported results. The two matrices will be presented in full within the Inception Report due 10 November 2026, once Cortech has reviewed the grant theories of change, results frameworks, targets, beneficiary lists and endline reports that GCERF will make available at inception. We flag this dependency explicitly: the evaluation matrix, sampling frame and data collection tools presented at proposal stage are preliminary and will be finalised in the Inception Report against the grant-specific documentation GCERF provides, consistent with the RFP's own note that "the proposal technical approach and budget are considered preliminary." **Beneficiary verification sampling.** Using the reach figures set out in the RFP's own grant overview tables, Grant 1 reports a combined total reach across Outcomes A, B and C of approximately 1,408 individuals (48 peace committee members, 240 elders/local leaders, 480 reconciliation/integration participants, 120 youth bloggers, 120 vulnerable individuals in livelihoods activities, 160 local government officials, and 240 joint dialogue participants). Grant 2 reports a combined total reach across Outcomes A, B and C of approximately 5,080 individuals (1,800 women and youth leaders, 1,100 community member participants in leadership activities, 600 trauma-recovery participants, 700 media outreach audience members, 800 community dialogue participants, and 80 livelihoods/TVET beneficiaries). Applying the RFP's approximately 10% verification benchmark yields an indicative minimum sample of around 141 individuals for Grant 1 and around 508 individuals for Grant 2. Cortech will treat this 10% figure as a floor, not a ceiling, and will refine the final sample size at inception using a finite population correction calculation against each population group's actual sampling frame, since several categories (for example, the 48 peace committee members or the 80 Grant 2 livelihoods/TVET beneficiaries) are small, closed populations where a flat 10% draw would be too small to be statistically defensible. For these finite groups we will apply a corrected sample size formula that accounts for the known population size, rather than sampling 10% mechanically, and will document the resulting sample size and its confidence interval in the Inception Report. Stratification will be applied, as the RFP requires, "where feasible" by grant, location, sex/age, beneficiary category and type of support received. Concretely, this means the Grant 1 sample will be drawn separately for Baidoa and Hudur and disaggregated by the seven population categories listed above (peace committee members, elders/local leaders, reconciliation/integration participants, youth bloggers, vulnerable individuals, local government officials, and joint dialogue participants), with sex and age recorded where safe and appropriate. The Grant 2 sample will be drawn separately for Jowhar, Adan Yabaal and Matabaan, disaggregated by women and youth leaders, community participants, trauma-recovery participants, media audience members, community dialogue participants, and livelihoods/TVET beneficiaries. Within each stratum, systematic random selection will be applied to grantee-held beneficiary lists once these are shared at inception, with replacement protocols agreed in advance for non-contactable or non-consenting individuals. **Verification modality.** Modality will be selected per population group and location, justified against access and security conditions rather than applied uniformly. For peace committee members, elders/local leaders and local government officials in Baidoa and Hudur, who are typically identifiable, contactable and less exposed to disclosure risk, in-person or phone-based structured interviews are proposed, with phone verification prioritised where road access or security clearance timelines are uncertain. For vulnerable individuals in livelihoods activities, including former Al-Shabaab members, IDPs and minority clan members, phone-based verification will be the default modality to protect confidentiality and reduce exposure, with in-person verification used only where informed consent and a secure, private location can be assured. For youth bloggers, remote verification by phone or messaging platforms is proposed, reflecting their existing digital engagement with the programme. In Hirshabelle, women and youth leaders and community dialogue participants in Jowhar will be prioritised for in-person verification given comparatively more predictable access, while Adan Yabaal and Matabaan verification will lean toward phone-based or hybrid approaches pending confirmation of access conditions at inception, consistent with the RFP's instruction to select "phone calls, interviews, or other feasible and ethical verification modalities." Trauma-recovery participants will be verified only through modalities and question sequencing agreed with GCERF and the grantee at inception, given the sensitivity of the subject matter, with a strong preference for phone-based contact conducted by a trained qualitative team member rather than a first-time enumerator. **Tool design.** One data collection tool will be developed per population group, applied consistently across geographic areas within each grant, in line with the RFP's recommendation. Tools will be piloted with a small number of respondents in each grant area before full deployment, using KoboToolbox for structured quantitative instruments to enable offline data capture, GPS-tagging of interview locations where appropriate and consented, and real-time data quality dashboards. Qualitative instruments (key informant interview guides and focus group discussion guides) will be developed for peace committee members, elders, women and youth leaders, and trauma-recovery participants, and will be translated into Somali and back-translated by an independent reviewer to check for meaning drift before fieldwork begins; where clan or locality-specific dialect variation is identified during inception scoping in Baidoa, Hudur, Jowhar, Adan Yabaal or Matabaan, local enumerators will be briefed on appropriate terminology adjustments. **Data collection plan and bias mitigation.** Cortech will devise a data collection plan sequenced to meet the RFP's compressed timeline: tool piloting and enumerator training immediately following Inception Report approval on 10 November 2026, fieldwork conducted through to the 1 December 2026 finalisation deadline, and data cleaning running in parallel rather than sequentially to protect the 9 December 2026 aide memoire deadline. To reduce selection bias, sampling frames will be drawn directly from grantee-reported beneficiary lists rather than convenience samples, with replacement rules defined in advance. To reduce non-response bias, at least three contact attempts will be made per selected respondent across different times of day before substitution, and non-response patterns will be documented and reported in the Data Integrity findings. To reduce interviewer bias, enumerators will be trained on neutral probing techniques, and a subset of interviews (a minimum proportion agreed at inception) will be back-checked by the Field Manager through call-back verification. To reduce transcription and translation error, all qualitative recordings will be transcribed in the original language before translation, with a second reviewer checking translated transcripts against source recordings for a sample of files prior to coding in NVivo. Quantitative data will be cleaned and analysed in SPSS, with syntax files retained to allow GCERF to reproduce indicator calculations, consistent with the RFP's requirement for "syntax or calculation files used to generate indicator values." **Ethical safeguards.** Informed consent will be obtained verbally or in writing, in Somali, before every interview, verification call or focus group, with consent scripts explaining voluntary participation, confidentiality, and the purpose of GCERF's evaluation and beneficiary verification exercise. Given that Grant 1 beneficiary categories explicitly include former Al-Shabaab members, IDPs and minority clans, confidentiality protocols will include anonymised data storage (unique respondent codes rather than names in analysis files), password-protected and access-restricted data systems, and a strict no-recording rule for any disclosure related to reintegration status unless the respondent explicitly consents. Referral pathways will be agreed with the grantees at inception for any disclosure of trauma, protection concerns or safety risk arising during trauma-recovery verification interviews in Hirshabelle or reintegration-sensitive interviews in Southwest State, so that field teams can refer respondents to appropriate support services rather than attempt to manage disclosures themselves.
