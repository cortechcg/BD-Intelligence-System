## Introduction, Background & Conceptual Framework

SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND

5.1 Context and Strategic Importance

Somalia enters the 2026 Deyr season facing a rapidly evolving risk environment: the 2025 Deyr rains failed, prolonged drought has depleted productive assets and reduced livestock herds, and 6.5 million people are now in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse, including more than 2 million in Emergency (IPC Phase 4) and 1.84 million children under five projected to face acute malnutrition, of whom 483,000 require treatment for severe acute malnutrition. Against this drought baseline, forecasts indicate a high probability of El Niño conditions during the October-December 2026 Deyr season, raising the likelihood of riverine flooding along the Juba and Shabelle rivers and flash floods across Somalia's most productive agricultural corridors, at a scale potentially comparable to the 2023 floods and approaching the 1997 event. The Somalia El Nino Anticipatory Action for Protecting Lives and Livelihoods (SEAL) project, contracted under Tender No. 2026/FRSOM/FRSOM/138130, LOA/RFP 007/2026, operationalizes the critical anticipatory-action window this forecast provides: acting before the shock, guided by FAO-SWALIM and FSNAU evidence and the approved anticipatory-action trigger, to protect farming, agro-pastoral, riverine and displaced communities whose capacity to absorb a second major shock is already exhausted.

5.2 Purpose and Objectives

The overall objective is to safeguard lives, livelihoods, productive assets and critical food systems from the anticipated impacts of the 2026 El Niño, preventing a further deterioration of food security and nutrition among vulnerable farming, agro-pastoral and riverine communities through the timely scale-up of coordinated, risk-informed anticipatory action and targeted emergency response. The specific outcome is that vulnerable farming, agro-pastoral and riverine households are protected through timely anticipatory and emergency actions that enable them to meet immediate food and essential needs, maintain agricultural production, and benefit from layered community infrastructure and services that reduce exposure to flooding, drought and other climate-related risks. Current planning covers 25,000 agriculture households and 5,000 fisheries households, with up to 25,000 vulnerable farming households receiving the flood-recession agriculture package and related advisory support across the twelve agriculture district lots spanning Hiraan (Beletweyne, Buulo Burto, Jalalaqsi), Middle Shabelle (Jowhar, Balcad), Lower Shabelle (Afgooye, Marka), Gedo (Luuq, Baardheere), Lower Juba (Kismayo, Afmadow) and Mudug (Hobyo).

5.3 Our Interpretation of the Assignment

We read this assignment as the construction of a delivery machine that is registered, verified and payment-ready before the approved anticipatory-action trigger fires: FAO is not buying a conventional post-shock distribution, but a Service Provider (SP) whose complete digital registration data, submitted within three weeks of LoA signature on the FAO-designated ODK, KOBO, BIMS, BIMO or FMT platform with 100 percent telephone verification, allows cash and the reviewed 2026 VRS e-voucher package to move on FAO instruction within the approved payment schedule. Two implications follow that the tender implies but does not spell out. First, because the trigger sits inside the October-December Deyr window and unverified or unresolved records shall not be paid, the entire readiness chain of inclusive Village Distribution Committees (VDCs), 3W "who does what, where" mapping with Federal and State line ministries, community consultation, registration, telephone verification and FAO voice-verification mobilization must be compressed into the first weeks after LoA signature, before hydrological conditions themselves begin restricting district access. Second, because the agriculture package targets the flood-recession planting window, the sequencing runs in two directions at once: lead-farmer GAP training and input pre-positioning must be completed before floodwaters arrive, while distribution and planting follow their recession, so the workplan must hold FAO's explicit approval gates (beneficiary list approval before any distribution, disbursement only on FAO instruction) without losing the season. Cross-component layering of Cash+ and the e-voucher entitlement must additionally be documented under the one household, one primary project participant rule so that layering never produces duplicate payments or unintended exclusion.

SECTION 2: CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK

Our analytical lens is the anticipatory-action logic that FAO itself has embedded in the SEAL design: a forecast of El Niño conditions in the October-December 2026 Deyr season, converted through the approved anticipatory-action trigger into pre-authorized assistance that reaches households before the shock erodes their remaining coping capacity. Within that logic we apply FAO's own results architecture as the organizing framework rather than importing a competing model. The four SEAL outputs form the results chain: Output 1 (Cash+/unconditional cash assistance to approved households) protects immediate food and essential needs and prevents negative coping strategies; Output 2 (the flood-recession agriculture package and advisory support to up to 25,000 farming households) protects productive capacity through the planting window; Output 3 establishes cross-cutting readiness, coordination, community engagement, targeting, protection, accountability and monitoring systems; Output 4 delivers the monitoring, verification, learning and reconciliation evidence on which FAO's payment authorization and result acceptance depend.

Two conceptual commitments structure how we operationalize this chain. The first is trigger-gated sequencing: because assistance is disbursed only on FAO instruction against the approved trigger, the framework treats readiness (VDC formation, 3W mapping, registration, telephone and voice verification) as a result in its own right, achieved and evidenced before activation, not as preparatory overhead. This mirrors the tender's indicative timeframes, which separate "registration/readiness before activation" from "payment according to the approved schedule." The second is evidence-as-delivery: every activity is designed backwards from the required means of verification named in the ToR, so that geotagged distribution evidence, synchronized BIMO transaction records, physical quality forms, PDM/PDR findings and the crop-establishment update are produced as the work happens rather than reconstructed afterwards.

Cross-cutting standards are held constant across the chain: AAP and do no harm principles at community entry, PSEA and PDAD-prevention embedded in every payment and distribution interaction, informed consent and data protection at registration, and inclusion engineered through the 40 percent women's representation target and prioritization of pregnant and lactating women, persons with disabilities, older persons, IDP households recognized by the host community, and marginalized and minority groups. The framework is deliberately layering-aware, documenting complementarity between Cash+, agriculture, livestock and fisheries components so that sequenced assistance compounds household protection without duplication, consistent with FAO-SWALIM and FSNAU evidence and the seasonal calendar in each lot.

## ii) Implementation Methodology

**Phase 1: Inception and pre-trigger readiness (Weeks 1 to 3).** The Project Manager, Yussuf Abdi Maalim, leads coordination with Federal and State line ministries including the Ministry of Agriculture and Irrigation, district authorities, clusters and operational partners to compile the village-level 3W "who does what, where" inventory, flagging overlap to FAO before any registration begins. Community Mobilizers conduct inclusive community entry with local authorities, elders, women and youth representatives, minority and marginalized groups, persons with disabilities and producer groups, establish or validate Village Distribution Committees (VDCs) with women, minority and disability representation, and explain eligibility, entitlements, complaints channels and the rule that no beneficiary pays any fee, tax, commission or contribution. Output: the Community Consultation Report with signed attendance lists, sex-disaggregated data, minutes and georeferenced site information, submitted within the LoA timeline.

**Phase 2: Targeting, registration and verification (Weeks 2 to 3, hard milestone).** Data Entry Clerks under the M&E/Data/Compliance function register beneficiaries on the FAO-designated platform (ODK, KOBO, BIMS, BIMO or FMT); Cortech's in-house KoboToolbox expertise transfers directly to form scripting, skip logic and constraint rules on whichever platform FAO designates. Registration applies one approved payee per household, 18+ age verification, informed consent on FAO forms, 100 percent telephone-number verification against mobile network operator records, and a live dashboard tracking the minimum 40 percent representation of women-headed households and women farmers. Complete registration data is submitted within three weeks of LoA signature, shown as a fixed gate in the A.3 workplan grid. Output: FAO-approved beneficiary list. No distribution or payment activity precedes this approval.

**Phase 3: Trigger-gated unconditional cash assistance.** On FAO instruction against the approved anticipatory-action trigger, and only then, the team mobilizes beneficiaries to complete FAO voice verification within the communicated timeframe, supports FAO/MMO triangulation, and applies the standing rule that no unverified or unresolved record is paid unless FAO approves an alternative procedure. Beneficiaries receive accessible orientation on SMS, PIN security and support for lost SIMs, low literacy or disability. Receipt is verified through the FAO-approved sample or call-back protocol; exceptions and grievances are logged and resolved with FAO and the mobile money operator. Output: payment reconciliation, exception log and PDM findings.

**Phase 4: Flood-recession agriculture package distribution.** The Agronomist [INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE: distinct nominee to be confirmed; role currently mapped to the Project Manager pending FAO review] leads coordination with FAO-authorized traders and submission of the detailed distribution/service plan. At every distribution point, each beneficiary is verified through BIMO or the FAO-designated platform; input quality of the 2026 VRS e-voucher package (certified maize seed, certified cowpea and Mung bean seed, assorted vegetable seed in original traceable producer packs, specification-compliant farm tools, genuine hermetic storage bags) is verified on the FAO physical quality form and endorsed by the authorized committee. Daily redemption records are reconciled across beneficiary, trader and system data; devices are synchronized and geotagged, date-stamped photographs collected per site where security permits. Output: distribution report, stock reconciliation, signed receipt evidence and geotagged photographs, all before the relevant flood-recession planting window.

**Phase 5: Extension, monitoring and close-out.** Lead-farmer training combines classroom and practical sessions on GAP, integrated production and pest management, drainage and flood preparedness, and post-harvest loss reduction, delivered before the planting window, producing the verified lead-farmer list with 100 percent functional telephone numbers. Farmer-to-farmer demonstrations run in groups of 25 to 30, adapted for women, pregnant and lactating participants and persons with disabilities. The Nutrition Focal Point, Abdifatah Aden Hussein, integrates FAO-approved nutrition and food-safety messages and trains community Nutrition Champions. The crop-establishment update is submitted approximately six weeks after input distribution, followed by harvest assessment support using FAO tools. The Reporting Officer, Salahweli Harun Abdi, produces bi-weekly or monthly progress reports on FAO templates and the final narrative and financial dossiers, with the agreed number of human-interest stories captured by the Qualified Photo/Videographer.

## iii) Cross-cutting Delivery, Data Quality Assurance and Ethics

Data quality is engineered at four checkpoints: platform-level constraint and validation rules at capture; daily supervisor review of incoming records with same-day correction of inconsistencies before submission to FAO; quantitative cleaning and analysis of PDM/PDR datasets in SPSS with outlier and duplicate detection; and qualitative coding of consultation records, FGD notes and complaints narratives in NVivo to surface targeting or protection issues early. Ethics and safeguarding are delivery functions, not annexes. The PSEA/Gender Focal Point ensures all personnel are trained on PSEA and code-of-conduct requirements with confidential reporting and referral pathways protecting complainants from retaliation. PDAD prevention includes sensitization of authorities and communities against taxation, gatekeeper demands and unauthorized deductions, with the current FAO hotline and toll-free channels displayed at every registration, distribution and training site. Do no harm and impartial access govern site selection; protection cases are referred with informed consent through confidential channels; no person below 18 is registered; personal data is access-restricted, securely stored and transferred, and any suspected loss or misuse reported to FAO immediately.

A full risk register with probability, impact, mitigation, owner and escalation trigger is provided in the prescribed format. Quantitative data from registration, e-voucher redemption, training attendance, PDM/PDR and the crop-establishment update will be exported from KOBO/BIMO, cleaned, coded and entered into SPSS, with Excel supporting data organization and daily reconciliation of beneficiary, trader and system records. Descriptive statistics will report against the LoA performance indicators: percentage of approved households paid on time and in full, redemption completeness per distribution point, hectares planted, and progress against the 40 percent minimum representation of women-headed households and women farmers, tracked live so shortfalls are corrected during registration rather than reported after it. All outputs will be disaggregated by sex, lot, district and vulnerability category, matching the sex-disaggregated reporting FAO templates require. Inferential analysis will use cross-tabulations and chi-square tests to examine whether PDM/PDR outcomes such as full-entitlement receipt, complaint incidence or awareness of the FAO hotline differ by beneficiary category or distribution point, flagging any site requiring corrective action before the next payment cycle. Where the lot includes nutrition-sensitive programming, KAP and dietary-diversity data collected with FAO-approved tools will be compared between baseline and end-line using paired comparisons.

Qualitative data from KIIs, FGDs, community consultation records and complaints logs will be transcribed from Somali, translated and coded in NVivo using a thematic framework built around the tender's cross-cutting requirements: targeting accuracy, AAP, PSEA awareness, PDAD indicators such as taxation or gatekeeper demands, and barriers facing women, persons with disabilities and minority groups.

Triangulation will be systematic rather than incidental. Each PDM/PDR finding will be tested against at least two independent sources: SPSS survey results against NVivo themes, transaction records against call-back verification, and geotagged distribution evidence against community feedback. Divergence between sources, for example high redemption rates alongside FGD reports of code-sharing pressure, triggers a documented exception review and a corrective action logged in the bi-weekly progress report, directly serving criterion 4.4 on monitoring, means of verification and corrective-action arrangements.

## The problem as the client frames it

Somalia enters the 2026 Deyr season facing a "rapidly evolving risk environment": the 2025 Deyr rains failed, drought has depleted productive assets and livestock herds, household debt has risen, and food security has deteriorated to 6.5 million people in Crisis (IPC Phase 3) or worse. Simultaneously, forecasts indicate a high probability of El Niño conditions in October–December 2026, threatening above-average rainfall, riverine flooding along the Juba and Shabelle rivers and flash floods across Somalia's most productive agricultural corridors — potentially comparable to the 2023 floods and approaching the 1997 event. Households still recovering from drought have "limited capacity to absorb another major shock." The forecast provides "a critical window for anticipatory action": FAO argues that early, trigger-based investment before the shock is effective and cost-efficient, and this tender operationalizes that window through the SEAL project's integrated anticipatory cash, agriculture inputs, and preparedness programme.
