## Our Understanding of the Assignment

Our Understanding of the Assignment

The Ministry of Education, State Department for Basic Education, is commissioning a partial end-of-program evaluation of the Kenya Primary Education Equity in Learning (KPEEL) Program, covering the Parent Program and the First Additional Financing, ahead of the scheduled programme completion on 31st December 2026. We understand this is explicitly a partial evaluation: it covers the full implementation period from programme effectiveness on 5th August 2022 through December 2026, and its findings are intended to feed directly into the design and early implementation of the Second Additional Financing, which became effective on 15th May 2026 and runs to 31st December 2029. This sequencing matters. The evaluation is not a closing exercise for a finished programme; it is a mid-course accountability and learning instrument for a Program-for-Results (PforR) operation that is still expanding, and its recommendations carry direct operational consequence for how the Ministry, the Teachers Service Commission (TSC) and the sub-implementing agencies calibrate the next financing phase. We understand the KPEEL Program as a blended financing operation, combining Program-for-Results (PforR) disbursement against verified results with an Investment Project Financing (IPF) component that funds technical assistance, institutional strengthening, and monitoring and evaluation systems, including support to the National Education Management Information System (NEMIS). Financing is drawn from the International Development Association (IDA), the Global Partnership for Education (GPE), and the LEGO Foundation, alongside the Government of Kenya, and the evaluation must therefore speak credibly to the accountability requirements of the World Bank alongside those of GPE and LEGO Foundation as co-financiers with a direct interest in evidence of results and value for money. The Program Development Objective is to reduce regional disparities in learning outcomes, improve the retention of girls in upper primary education, and strengthen systems for delivering equitable education outcomes. We understand the evaluation must assess achievement against this PDO and its indicators, the Intermediate Results Indicators, and the seven Disbursement Linked Indicators (DLIs) and associated Disbursement Linked Results (DLRs) across the three Results Areas: Results Area 1 (equalising learning opportunities through Results-Based School Grants to 5,422 target primary schools, teacher deployment against shortage, and school meals for vulnerable learners), Results Area 2 (girls' participation, principally through the Elimu Scholarship mechanism delivered with the Equity Group Foundation and the Jomo Kenyatta Foundation), and Results Area 3 (capacity for reform implementation, including the rollout of the Competency-Based Curriculum (CBC) and Competency-Based Assessment (CBA), classroom construction, and Competency-Based Teacher Education (CBTE) capacity building in Primary Teacher Training Colleges). We note the Terms of Reference give explicit, named emphasis to Results-Based School Grants (RBSG) and special needs education (SNE) interventions and reforms as a specific focus area, and we read this as a weighting signal that the evaluation design and the eventual technical proposal must visibly reflect. Geographically, we understand the programme, and therefore the evaluation, spans the ten NASMLA 2018 target counties of Mandera, Wajir, Garissa, Turkana, Samburu, Kisii, Siaya, Narok, Bungoma and Bomet, together with urban informal settlements in major urban centres and refugee camp and host-community schools. The scale of the target population, approximately 6.6 million learners, including 221,529 children in refugee-hosting counties and 117,900 children in refugee camp-based schools, 20,000 PTTC diploma teacher trainees, and 200,000 primary school teachers, means the evaluation must credibly cover both system-level performance and the lived experience of the most marginalised learners, including girls at risk of dropout, learners with disabilities, and refugee and host-community children. We recognise that the assignment extends well beyond a conventional evaluation report. The Ministry requires a Communication, Information Dissemination and Documentation Package as a substantive deliverable in its own right: a documentary film in two lengths with Kiswahili subtitling and Kenyan Sign Language inset, 10 to 15 case studies, a photo library of no fewer than 300 images, a policy brief, a non-technical summary, infographics, and a National Validation and Dissemination Workshop that the firm must organise and facilitate, with County Directors of Education, County Programme Coordinators and School Heads as lead speakers, ahead of report finalisation. We understand that all consent documentation must comply with the Data Protection Act, 2019, and World Bank data protection standards, and that all materials, footage and data vest solely in the Ministry. We understand the evaluation must apply the OECD-DAC criteria of relevance, effectiveness, efficiency, coherence, impact and sustainability, alongside value-for-money analysis, institutional capacity assessment across national, county, sub-county and school levels, and Environmental, Social, Health and Safety safeguards review, structured against the sixteen thematic areas set out in Section 5 of the Terms of Reference, and delivered against a 90-working-day, four-phase schedule with approval gates at each milestone: the Inception Report, the Draft Assessment Report, the Communication and Documentation Package, and the Final Evaluation Report, reporting to the Head of DPCAD and the National Program Coordinator.

## Proposed Technical Approach — Summary

**Proposed Technical Approach, A Summary**

Cortech Consulting Group's approach to the Partial End Term Program Evaluation of the Kenya Primary Education Equity in Learning (KPEEL) Program treats this assignment as what it is: a bounded assessment of the Parent Program and First Additional Financing (August 2022 to December 2026) within a Program-for-Results (PforR) and Investment Project Financing (IPF) design, whose findings must feed directly into the Second Additional Financing (2026-2029). The methodology combines OECD-DAC criteria (relevance, effectiveness, efficiency, coherence, impact, sustainability) with instrument-specific verification of the Program Development Objective (PDO), the three Results Areas, and the seven Disbursement Linked Indicators (DLIs) and Disbursement Linked Results (DLRs), giving particular weight to Results-Based School Grants (RBSG) and special needs education (SNE) as the Terms of Reference specifically direct. Mobilisation begins with a structured desk review of the Project Appraisal Document (PAD), the Programme Operations Manual (POM), the Project Procurement Strategy for Development (PPSD), financing agreements and any restructuring papers, requested from the Ministry of Education at contract start. This informs the Inception Report, which will set out our understanding of the evaluation criteria, data sources, a research methodology with proposed sample framework, a schedule of activities and travel, a proposal for the validation event, and validated draft data collection tools, submitted for approval by the National Program Coordination Unit before fieldwork proceeds, consistent with the approval-gated workflow the Ministry has built into the four payment phases. Fieldwork applies a mixed-methods design across the ten target counties identified through NASMLA 2018 (Mandera, Wajir, Garissa, Turkana, Samburu, Kisii, Siaya, Narok, Bungoma and Bomet), urban informal settlements, and refugee camp-based and host-community schools. Quantitative work verifies DLI achievement against school-level records, School Improvement Plans (SIPs) and TSC deployment data; qualitative work uses key informant interviews and focus group discussions with County Directors of Education, County Programme Coordinators, School Heads, teachers, PTTC trainees and beneficiary communities, including refugee and host-community respondents. This is supplemented by institutional interviews with the Teachers Service Commission, KICD, KNEC, JKF, NACONEK, KISE, KIB, KEMI and Equity Group Foundation to assess implementation arrangements, safeguards compliance and the fidelity of Competency-Based Curriculum (CBC) and Competency-Based Assessment (CBA) rollout. Value-for-money analysis re-examines the cost and benefit assumptions in the PAD's economic analysis against actual delivery, and a dedicated environmental, social, health and safety review assesses PAP compliance and universal accessibility of infrastructure investments. Analysis triangulates quantitative and qualitative streams against the theory of change, with findings tested for attribution before conclusions are drawn on each PDO and intermediate indicator. Quality assurance rests on structured field supervision, transcript review and a two-stage internal peer review of the Draft Assessment Report and Draft Evaluation Report before submission to the Ministry. The National Validation and Dissemination Workshop, held ahead of report finalisation, is organised and facilitated by Cortech with County Directors of Education, County Programme Coordinators, School Heads and beneficiary representatives as lead speakers, and its structured feedback is incorporated into the Final Evaluation Report. In parallel, the Communication, Information Dissemination and Documentation Package (documentary, case studies, photo library, policy brief, infographics) is produced against the same fieldwork sampling frame, with a specialist multimedia partner engaged to deliver the Full HD documentary, Kiswahili subtitling and Kenyan Sign Language inset to the specification required. Knowledge transfer is built into the fieldwork itself: joint data-collection planning with the National Program Coordination Unit, structured feedback sessions during the validation workshop, and hands-on orientation of MoE and implementing-agency staff in the validated tools and DLI verification approach used, so that county and national officers retain the evaluation logic beyond the assignment's close.
