## Introduction And Framework

# 5. INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND

## 5.1 Context and Strategic Importance

Save the Children International's Localization Practice Phase II builds on a widely recognized deficit in Somalia's humanitarian architecture: local and national actors (LNAs) engaged in child protection coordination remain underrepresented in decision-making spaces dominated by international actors, despite carrying the frontline burden of service delivery in Baidoa, Mogadishu, and other operational areas. This endline evaluation arrives at a moment when the Grand Bargain localization commitments and Somalia's own National Child Protection Policy are under scrutiny for implementation gaps, making the evidence generated by this assignment directly relevant to advocacy positioning with the Federal Government of Somalia, the Child Protection Sub-Cluster, and donor localization targets. Cortech's prior engagement documenting green skills outcomes for GREDO/DANIDA/Save the Children in Baidoa (2025) provided direct exposure to the operational and coordination dynamics of local partners in this same geography, informing the contextual grounding of this proposal.

## 5.2 Purpose and Objectives

The assignment's purpose is to establish the extent to which Phase II has strengthened the capacity, voice, and institutional standing of local and national child protection actors within Somalia's coordination and advocacy structures, measured against the baseline and midline evidence generated earlier in the project cycle. Objectives include assessing changes in LNA participation in coordination mechanisms, the durability of capacity-strengthening investments, shifts in advocacy influence exercised by local actors vis-à-vis government and cluster bodies, and the extent to which localization commitments translated into resourcing and decision-making authority rather than symbolic inclusion. Cortech's interpretation treats these objectives as interdependent: capacity gains are only meaningful if they convert into sustained coordination presence and advocacy leverage.

## 5.3 Our Interpretation of the Assignment

Cortech reads this assignment as a summative, theory-based evaluation rather than a compliance exercise — it requires tracing a causal pathway from capacity-strengthening inputs (training, mentorship, sub-granting, coordination seats) through to intermediate outcomes (LNA confidence, technical competence, representation) and ultimately to the higher-order outcome of localization: local actors exercising genuine influence over child protection programming and policy dialogue in Somalia. This distinguishes the assignment from a standard project completion review, since the unit of analysis is the localization *practice* itself, not solely SCI's programmatic outputs. Cortech's approach treats local and national actors as primary respondents and co-analysts of their own experience, consistent with the participatory orientation the ToR implies through its emphasis on qualitative depth.

## 5.4 Key Evaluation Questions

Cortech proposes to organize data collection and analysis around six evaluation questions, refined jointly with SCI during the inception phase: (1) To what extent have targeted LNAs strengthened their institutional and technical capacity in child protection coordination since baseline/midline? (2) How has LNA participation in coordination mechanisms (Cluster, Sub-Cluster, Area-level forums) changed in frequency, quality, and influence? (3) What evidence exists of LNAs independently leading or co-leading advocacy initiatives on child protection issues? (4) What factors — internal or external — enabled or constrained localization gains? (5) How sustainable are the capacity and coordination gains absent continued SCI support? (6) What unintended effects, positive or negative, has the localization approach produced for LNA organizational identity, funding relationships, or accountability to communities?

## 5.5 Deliverables

Cortech will produce three principal deliverables consistent with the ToR: (i) an Evaluation Methodology and Work Plan, submitted at inception, detailing sampling, tools, and analysis software; (ii) a Data Analysis and Interpretation package presenting triangulated quantitative and qualitative findings against each evaluation question; and (iii) a final Endline Evaluation Report incorporating methodology, findings, interpretation, and forward-looking recommendations for Phase III localization programming, accompanied by a concise donor/advocacy brief suitable for dissemination to the Federal Government of Somalia and cluster partners.

## 5.6 Understanding of Success

Cortech defines success for this assignment as delivery of an evidence base robust enough to withstand scrutiny from SCI's technical advisors and external localization stakeholders, while remaining actionable for program redesign. This means findings must be disaggregated by actor type (local NGO, community-based structure, government counterpart), triangulated across at least three data sources per evaluation question, and presented with explicit confidence levels rather than uniform certainty. A successful endline evaluation will give SCI's Somalia Country Office defensible, disaggregated evidence to inform both internal programming decisions and external advocacy positioning on localization financing and authority-sharing.

# 6. CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK

Cortech will anchor this evaluation in a hybrid framework combining the OECD DAC evaluation criteria with a Theory of Change (ToC) reconstruction specific to localization practice, rather than applying either instrument in isolation. The OECD DAC criteria — relevance, effectiveness, efficiency, coherence, sustainability, and impact — provide the evaluative structure required to answer summative questions about Phase II's performance and to satisfy the methodological rigor expected of an endline assessment commissioned by an international NGO operating under donor accountability requirements. Relevance will test whether the localization approach responded to actual capacity and representation gaps identified by LNAs themselves; effectiveness and efficiency will assess whether coordination and advocacy outcomes were achieved at reasonable cost and within timeframe; coherence will examine alignment with the Federal Government of Somalia's child protection policy architecture and the Grand Bargain commitments; sustainability will interrogate whether gains persist without continued external scaffolding; and impact will probe broader shifts in how child protection coordination is governed in Somalia.

Layered onto this, Cortech will reconstruct the project's implicit Theory of Change through document review and key informant interviews during the inception phase, mapping the assumed pathway from inputs (training, sub-grants, mentorship, seat allocation in coordination bodies) through outputs (trained LNA staff, functioning coordination structures) to outcomes (LNA-led advocacy, sustained coordination presence) and the underlying assumptions connecting each stage. This reconstruction serves a diagnostic function distinct from OECD DAC scoring: it identifies precisely where the causal chain held, where it broke down, and why — information that a criteria-only assessment would flag as a rating but not explain.

This combined framework fits the assignment because localization is inherently a change-in-relationships phenomenon, not merely a service-delivery output, and evaluating it credibly requires both standardized comparative criteria (for donor and cluster-level reporting) and a causal narrative (for programmatic learning). Cortech has applied comparable hybrid designs in the IGAD Land Governance Programme evaluation and the Arche Nova WASH and livelihoods endline in Somalia, both of which required reconciling institutional-level capacity change with measurable coordination outcomes under similarly constrained field-access conditions.

## Analysis Plan

## SAMPLING STRATEGY AND PARTICIPANT SELECTION

The Endline Evaluation of Localization Practice Phase II is not designed as a population-based survey and therefore does not require a probability sample or statistical power calculation. The evaluation instead relies on purposive selection of Key Informant Interview (KII) and Focus Group Discussion (FGD) participants, structured to capture the perspectives of actors directly engaged in the localization and child protection coordination architecture that Phase II sought to strengthen. Respondent categories will include: local and national civil society organizations (LNAs) that received capacity-strengthening support under the project; members of the Child Protection Sub-Cluster and Area of Responsibility (AoR) coordination mechanisms; Save the Children Somalia Country Office program and MEAL staff; representatives of relevant Federal Government of Somalia and Federal Member State line ministries engaged in child protection coordination; and community-level duty bearers and child protection committee members in project intervention districts. Selection criteria will prioritize: (i) documented engagement with Phase II activities (attendance records, capacity assessment participation, sub-grant status); (ii) role diversity across the localization value chain (funding, coordination, advocacy, service delivery); (iii) geographic spread across the districts covered under Phase II to capture contextual variation in coordination maturity; and (iv) gender balance, with deliberate oversampling of women-led LNAs given their documented underrepresentation in coordination spaces in Somalia. A respondent tracking matrix will be maintained against these criteria throughout fieldwork to demonstrate that the achieved sample reflects the diversity of the localization actor landscape, rather than convenience access, and this matrix will be annexed to the inception report for client sign-off before data collection proceeds.

## SECTION 2 — DATA ANALYSIS PLAN

Quantitative data collected through structured KoboToolbox instruments — including any closed-ended perception or capacity-scoring items administered to LNA staff and coordination members — will be exported into SPSS for cleaning, coding, and analysis. Analysis will proceed in two stages: descriptive statistics (frequencies, cross-tabulations, and mean capacity/confidence scores disaggregated by organization type, gender, and district) to establish baseline-to-endline comparison where Phase I or midline data exist; and, where sample sizes and data structure permit, inferential tests (chi-square tests of association and paired-sample comparisons) to assess whether observed changes in localization indicators are statistically distinguishable from baseline positions. All quantitative outputs will be presented against the indicator framework and theory of change underpinning Phase II.

Qualitative data from KIIs, FGDs, and document review will be transcribed, coded, and analyzed in NVivo using a thematic analysis approach structured around the OECD-DAC evaluation criteria (relevance, effectiveness, efficiency, sustainability, and where applicable, coherence), with an additional coding tree branch specific to localization dimensions — funding equity, decision-making inclusion, capacity transfer, and coordination influence. A codebook will be developed jointly with the Lead Consultant and shared with the Save the Children MEAL focal point during the inception phase to ensure alignment with Phase II's original indicator definitions.

Triangulation will occur at the analysis and reporting stage through a structured matrix that maps each key evaluation question against corresponding quantitative indicators, qualitative themes, and secondary document evidence (project reports, coordination meeting minutes, sub-grant records). Divergence between data sources — for example, where quantitative capacity scores improve but qualitative narratives indicate persistent funding exclusion — will be flagged explicitly in the findings rather than averaged out, consistent with Cortech's approach on the Arche Nova WASH and Livelihoods endline evaluation (Somalia, 2025) and the Welthungerhilfe Chukua Control civil society evaluation, both of which required reconciling quantitative outcome data against qualitative accounts of institutional and community-level change.

## Methodology

# PROPOSED APPROACH AND METHODOLOGY

## 3.1 Overall Approach

Cortech Consulting Group proposes a mixed-methods evaluation design anchored in the OECD DAC criteria of Relevance, Effectiveness, Efficiency, Impact, and Sustainability, supplemented by a sixth analytical lens on Localization/Coherence to capture the extent to which local and national actors (LNAs) have been genuinely strengthened in child protection coordination and advocacy roles. This design responds directly to the ToR requirement for a technical proposal that specifies methodologies, data analysis, interpretation, and reporting approach, which carries 30% weighting in the evaluation of this bid. The evaluation will combine quantitative household- and stakeholder-level surveys with qualitative Key Informant Interviews (KIIs), Focus Group Discussions (FGDs), and a structured desk review, allowing statistically grounded findings on coverage and outcome indicators to be cross-validated against contextual, experiential, and perception-based data from child protection actors themselves.

The rationale for this mixed-methods architecture is threefold. First, localization outcomes are inherently multidimensional — spanning funding flows, decision-making authority, capacity transfer, and coordination mechanisms — and no single data source can adequately capture this complexity. Second, triangulating quantitative indicators (e.g., proportion of Child Protection Sub-Cluster/Area of Responsibility (AoR) leadership positions held by national actors, changes in direct funding to local organizations) against qualitative narratives from local and national child protection actors reduces the risk of attribution error common in localization evaluations. Third, given Cortech's prior engagement in comparable localization and civil-society strengthening evaluations in Somalia and the Horn of Africa — including the Green Skills Documentation assignment in Baidoa (GREDO/DANIDA/Save the Children, 2025) and the Civil Society Evaluation of the Chukua Control project (Welthungerhilfe, 2025) — this approach has demonstrated capacity to produce findings that are both statistically defensible and programmatically actionable for donor and INGO stakeholders.

The evaluation will be structured around a theory-of-change reconstruction for the Localization Practice Phase II initiative, mapping the causal pathway from capacity-strengthening inputs (training, mentorship, sub-granting, coordination platform access) through intermediate outcomes (LNA participation in coordination structures, advocacy engagement) to the intended end-state of sustained local leadership in child protection coordination. Each evaluation question will be explicitly mapped against this theory of change and against the OECD DAC criteria to ensure the final report addresses both accountability and learning objectives.

## 3.2 Phase-by-Phase Breakdown

**Phase 1 — Inception (Weeks 1–2).** The inception phase will consist of a comprehensive desk review of Phase I and Phase II program documents, baseline/midline reports, Child Protection AoR coordination minutes, partnership agreements, and relevant Somalia localization policy frameworks (including the Grand Bargain commitments and Somalia NGO Consortium localization roadmap where applicable). This review will inform a refined evaluation matrix linking each ToR objective to specific indicators, data sources, and collection methods. An inception report will be submitted, including the finalized evaluation design, sampling frame, data collection tools, and detailed work plan — directly addressing the ToR's requirement for a documented work plan under the technical proposal criterion.

**Phase 2 — Field Data Collection (Weeks 3–5).** Field work will be conducted across program locations in Somalia, with data collected through KoboToolbox-based digital surveys, in-person and remote KIIs, and FGDs facilitated by bilingual (English/Somali) field enumerators. Field teams will include Somali-national data collectors with prior experience in child protection and community engagement contexts, satisfying the ToR's local field team composition requirement while ensuring cultural and linguistic appropriateness of data collection instruments.

**Phase 3 — Data Analysis (Weeks 6–7).** Quantitative data exported from KoboToolbox will be cleaned and analyzed in SPSS, generating descriptive and inferential statistics disaggregated by geographic location, organization type (local/national/international), and gender of respondents where applicable. Qualitative data from KIIs and FGDs will be transcribed, coded, and thematically analyzed in NVivo, using a coding framework derived from the theory-of-change and evaluation matrix. Where relevant, QGIS mapping will be used to visualize the geographic distribution of Child Protection Sub-Cluster/AoR activities and LNA presence across evaluation sites.

**Phase 4 — Reporting (Weeks 8–9).** Findings from quantitative and qualitative strands will be triangulated and synthesized into a draft evaluation report structured around the OECD DAC criteria, presented to Save the Children and relevant stakeholders for a validation workshop. Feedback from this workshop will be incorporated into a final report, accompanied by an executive summary, a set of actionable recommendations disaggregated by stakeholder type (Save the Children, national/local partners, donor), and a lessons-learned brief for future localization programming.

## 3.3 Method Mapped to Each Deliverable

**Desk Review.** A structured document review of Phase I and Phase II reports, monitoring data, coordination meeting minutes, and relevant national policy instruments will establish the baseline against which endline change is measured, and will inform the construction of the evaluation matrix delivered as part of the Evaluation Methodology and Work Plan output.

**Quantitative Survey.** A structured survey administered via KoboToolbox will target local and national child protection actors, Sub-Cluster/AoR coordination members, and community-level duty bearers. The survey sample will be determined using a stratified approach across program locations to ensure statistically representative coverage of participating organizations, with sample size calculated to detect meaningful change at 95% confidence and 5% margin of error, subject to the final population frame confirmed during inception. Survey data will feed directly into the Data Analysis and Interpretation deliverable via SPSS-generated cross-tabulations, trend comparisons against baseline/midline data, and disaggregated outcome indicators.

**Key Informant Interviews.** KIIs will be conducted with Save the Children program staff, Child Protection Sub-Cluster/AoR coordinators, representatives of national and local child protection organizations, and relevant government counterparts (Ministry of Women and Human Rights Development, where applicable). These interviews will probe coordination effectiveness, advocacy influence, and perceived shifts in decision-making authority — core to assessing the localization outcome pathway.

**Focus Group Discussions.** FGDs will be held with staff and members of local and national partner organizations engaged in Phase II activities, segmented by organization type and, where feasible, by gender, to surface differentiated experiences of capacity strengthening, funding access, and coordination participation. FGD data will be coded thematically in NVivo alongside KII transcripts to identify convergent and divergent narratives across stakeholder groups.

**Data Analysis and Interpretation.** All quantitative and qualitative strands will be triangulated through a structured synthesis matrix, ensuring each finding is supported by at least two independent data sources before being presented as a substantiated conclusion in the Endline Evaluation Report, consistent with the ToR requirement for a qualitative and quantitative research capability demonstrated through evidence of comparable prior contracts, including Cortech's Endline Evaluation of the Water and Livelihoods project for Arche Nova (2025) and the Assessment of Federal MOH Capacity for the World Bank (2025), both of which required comparable multi-method triangulation in the Somali operating context.

## 3.4 Data Quality Assurance

Data quality will be safeguarded through a multi-layered assurance protocol. KoboToolbox forms will incorporate skip logic, range validation, and mandatory-field constraints to minimize enumerator error at the point of collection. Daily data quality checks will be conducted by the field supervisor, including spot-checks of a random 10% sample of completed surveys through call-back verification. All KIIs and FGDs will be audio-recorded (with consent) and transcribed verbatim prior to coding in NVivo, with a second analyst independently coding a subset of transcripts to establish inter-coder reliability. Quantitative datasets will undergo a structured cleaning protocol in SPSS, including outlier detection, duplicate removal, and consistency checks against the KoboToolbox audit trail, prior to any analysis being finalized. A data quality assurance log will be maintained throughout fieldwork and submitted as an annex to the final report.

## 3.5 Ethical Considerations

The evaluation will be conducted in accordance with Cortech's Child Safeguarding Policy and PSEA compliance framework, both formally established organizational policies. Given the sensitivity of child protection programming, all field staff engaging with informants — particularly those involving child protection actors or beneficiaries — will undergo a pre-deployment briefing on safeguarding protocols, confidentiality, and referral pathways for any protection concerns identified during data collection. Informed consent will be obtained from all KII and FGD participants prior to engagement, with clear explanation of the voluntary nature of participation, the purpose of the evaluation, and data storage and confidentiality protocols. No direct engagement with children will occur without appropriate safeguarding clearance and, where applicable, guardian consent, in line with Save the Children's own child safeguarding standards. All data will be stored on password-protected, encrypted platforms accessible only to the core evaluation team, and personally identifiable information will be anonymized in the final report and any published outputs.

## Work Plan

## Work Plan and Implementation Timeline

Cortech Consulting Group proposes a ten-week implementation period from contract signing to submission of the final validated report. Given that the Terms of Reference do not specify a fixed project duration, this timeline is calibrated to the scope described — a mixed-methods endline evaluation covering child protection coordination structures, local/national actor capacity, and advocacy outcomes across Save the Children's Somalia operational areas — and remains adjustable in consultation with the Somalia Country Office during the inception phase. The structure follows five sequential-with-overlap phases, consistent with Cortech's methodology on comparable assignments, including the Arche Nova WASH and Livelihoods Endline Evaluation (2025) and the GREDO/DANIDA Green Skills Documentation in Baidoa (2025).

**Phase 1: Inception and Evaluation Design (Week 1–2).** Activities include a kick-off meeting with Save the Children's MEAL and Child Protection teams, desk review of Phase I localization documentation, refinement of the evaluation matrix against OECD DAC criteria (relevance, effectiveness, efficiency, sustainability, coherence), and finalization of sampling frame across target districts. Output: Inception Report with evaluation matrix, sampling strategy, and confirmed workplan, submitted for client approval by end of Week 2.

**Phase 2: Tool Development, Compliance Finalization, and Piloting (Week 2–3).** Data collection instruments — key informant interview guides, focus group discussion protocols, and KoboToolbox-based structured questionnaires — are drafted in English and translated into Somali, then piloted with a small sample of local child protection actors. This phase runs partially in parallel with the close of Phase 1 to avoid idle time between inception approval and field mobilization. Output: Finalized, piloted data collection tools; recruitment and briefing of the Somalia-based field data collection team.

**Phase 3: Field Data Collection (Week 3–6).** The Somalia-based field team conducts KII and FGD sessions with local/national child protection actors, community structures, and coordination mechanism representatives, supported by KoboToolbox for real-time quantitative data capture and audio-recorded qualitative sessions for subsequent NVivo coding. Daily data quality checks and weekly progress calls with the Lead Consultant run concurrently with fieldwork. Output: Complete, cleaned dataset (quantitative and qualitative) from all sampled locations.

**Phase 4: Data Analysis and Interpretation (Week 6–8).** Quantitative analysis is conducted in SPSS; qualitative transcripts are coded thematically in NVivo against the evaluation matrix. Transcription and translation of Somali-language recordings begin during the final week of fieldwork rather than after its completion, shortening the critical path. Output: Analysis brief with preliminary findings, shared with the client for a validation checkpoint before full drafting.

**Phase 5: Reporting and Validation (Week 8–10).** Draft report writing begins once preliminary findings are confirmed, overlapping with final data cleaning rather than waiting for its full completion. A validation workshop with Save the Children and localization stakeholders is held in Week 9 to test findings and recommendations before finalization. Output: Draft Endline Evaluation Report (Week 8), Validation Workshop (Week 9), Final Endline Evaluation Report incorporating stakeholder feedback (Week 10).

**Milestone Dates**
- End of Week 2: Inception Report approved
- End of Week 3: Data collection tools piloted and field team deployed
- End of Week 6: Field data collection complete; dataset cleaned
- End of Week 8: Draft report submitted
- Week 9: Validation workshop conducted
- End of Week 10: Final report delivered

**Sequential vs. Parallel Activities:** Inception and tool design are sequential, since sampling decisions determine instrument structure. Tool piloting overlaps with the tail end of inception approval to protect the fieldwork start date. Within fieldwork, data quality assurance and preliminary transcription run in parallel with ongoing collection in other districts. Analysis and report drafting are partially parallel, with narrative sections addressing methodology and context written before quantitative outputs are finalized.
