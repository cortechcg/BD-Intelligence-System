# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Christian Aid has commissioned this end-of-project evaluation to assess the extent to which the "Catalyzing Multilateral Development Banks Financing for Energy access in Africa" project — implemented over 2024-2026 with support from the Charles Stewart Mott Foundation and delivered through the Kenya Climate Change Working Group (KCCWG) in Kenya and the Civil Society Network on Climate Change (CISONECC) in Malawi — has achieved its intended outputs, outcomes and objectives. The project set out to strengthen civil society and faith actor engagement with the African Development Bank (AfDB) and other Multilateral Development Banks (MDBs) for just and accountable financing of decentralised renewable energy, and to catalyse di-investment of public finance away from fossil fuels toward locally led sustainable renewable energy systems. Three years into implementation, Christian Aid needs an independent, evidence-informed account of what this combination of policy research, multi-stakeholder dialogues, capacity building, AfDB annual meetings and CSO forum engagement, and advocacy on Country Strategy Papers and Mission 300 Energy Compacts has actually delivered — against the logframe and Theory of Change — and what should carry forward into future engagement with the AfDB and wider MDB reform, fossil fuel phase-out and just transition agendas.

Cortech's proposed approach is anchored in a single design decision: because the project's central claim is a contribution claim — that coordinated civil society and faith actor engagement shifted AfDB positions and public narratives on DRE — the evaluation cannot rely on before/after comparison alone. Cortech will build the inception report's evaluation matrix directly on the ToR's own question groupings (Effectiveness, Relevance, Coherence and Partnership, Efficiency, Impact and Contribution, Sustainability, GESI, and Theory of Change Validation), and will apply outcome harvesting to surface actual changes in AfDB/MDB policy, financing priorities and stakeholder positions before working backwards through contribution analysis to test which project workstreams — convenings, research and knowledge products, the learning platform, direct AfDB engagement — plausibly drove them, and what alternative factors (other coalitions, external events) must be discounted. This sequencing lets the evaluation explain not only what changed but why, and lets it test the Theory of Change's assumptions rather than assume they held.

The assignment will be led by a Senior Evaluation Consultant holding an advanced degree in development studies/policy analysis with over ten years' experience conducting evaluations for multi-development agencies across Africa, supported by a Kenya-based field lead and a Malawi-based research associate to ensure the stakeholder mapping matrix — disaggregated by country, stakeholder type (CSO, faith actor, AfDB official, private sector, media, government) and gender — is populated with genuinely representative respondents in both countries, not Kenya alone. ## Analysis Plan

## Sampling and Selection Logic

This assignment is an evaluation of a policy-influencing and civil society coordination project, not a population-based survey, and therefore does not require a probability sample or a calculated confidence interval. The evidence base rests on a finite and identifiable universe of stakeholders who were directly engaged in, or targeted by, the project's activities across Kenya and Malawi — namely staff and members of the Kenya Climate Change Working Group (KCCWG) and the Civil Society Network on Climate Change (CISONECC), Christian Aid project and country staff, faith actors and champions for decentralised renewable energy mobilised through the project, AfDB officials engaged through Country Strategy Papers and Mission 300 Country Compacts processes, media actors who covered decentralised renewable energy (DRE) issues, and relevant government departments. Cortech will therefore apply purposive selection, built on the stakeholder mapping matrix specified in the ToR and disaggregated by country (Kenya and Malawi), stakeholder type (CSO, faith actor, AfDB official, private sector, media and government), and gender. Respondents will be selected for their direct involvement in specific project workstreams — convenings, multi-stakeholder dialogues, AfDB annual meetings and CSO forums, the learning platform, and policy research outputs — so that each interview or focus group discussion (FGD) yields evidence directly relevant to at least one line of the evaluation matrix. Where AfDB officials, government representatives or media actors are approached, selection will prioritise individuals with documented interaction with the project (e.g., named participants in dialogues, signatories to shared CSO positions, or recipients of policy briefs) to maximise the credibility of contribution claims under outcome harvesting. Snowball referral will be used sparingly, only where the desk review and KCCWG/CISONECC staff identify additional champions or AfDB counterparts not captured in the initial stakeholder map, and any referred respondent will be validated against the project's own records before inclusion.

## Methodology

# 3. Proposed Approach and Methodology

## 3.1 Overall Approach

Cortech Consulting Group proposes a participatory, mixed-methods evaluation design that treats the project's logframe and Theory of Change as the evaluative spine of the assignment rather than a background reference document. The Energy Access Project pursued two interlinked objectives — strengthening civil society and faith actor engagement with the African Development Bank (AfDB) and other Multilateral Development Banks (MDBs), and shifting public finance away from fossil fuels toward locally led decentralised renewable energy (DRE) — delivered through two national partners, the Kenya Climate Change Working Group (KCCWG) in Kenya and the Civil Society Network on Climate Change (CISONECC) in Malawi. The evaluation design therefore treats Kenya and Malawi as two distinct implementation contexts requiring disaggregated stakeholder mapping, while allowing comparative analysis of how convenings, dialogues, CSO positioning, AfDB engagement and the learning platform reinforced one another within and across both countries.

The design is structured around the ToR's own evaluation matrix logic in Section 5.0 — Effectiveness, Relevance, Coherence and Partnership, Sustainability and Learning, Efficiency, Impact and Contribution, Sustainability, and the GESI cross-cutting lens — plus the Theory of Change validation question. Cortech will not silently reorganise this matrix into a textbook OECD-DAC sequence; the client's own groupings, including the separate treatment of "Sustainability and learning" alongside a later standalone "Sustainability" question, will be preserved and elaborated with sub-questions, indicators, data sources and methods in the inception report, as instructed in Section 6.0.

Because the ToR explicitly flags that attribution of AfDB/MDB policy shifts to a single project is inherently difficult to isolate, Cortech will apply outcome harvesting as the primary method for the Impact and Contribution and Effectiveness questions, supplemented by contribution analysis to test the critical assumptions underlying the project's Theory of Change. This combination allows the evaluation to work backward from observed changes in AfDB policy positions, Country Strategy Papers and Mission 300 Country Compacts, and forward from the project's stated theory, meeting in the middle to establish a defensible contribution story rather than a claim of sole causation.

## 3.2 Phase-by-Phase Breakdown

**Phase 1 — Contracting, Inception and Document Review (Week 1–3).** Following contracting and an initial briefing with Christian Aid's project lead and Monitoring and Evaluation lead, the Lead Evaluator will conduct a structured document review covering the project proposal, logframe, Theory of Change, annual technical and donor reports, policy research and briefs, meeting reports, partner reports from KCCWG and CISONECC, media outputs, capacity-building records, and relevant AfDB frameworks including Mission 300 Country Compacts and Country Strategy Papers. The inception report, delivered in Week 3, will present the full evaluation matrix, a stakeholder mapping matrix disaggregated by country (Kenya and Malawi), stakeholder type (CSO, faith actor, AfDB official, private sector, media, government) and gender, the outcome harvesting protocol, data collection instruments built in KoboToolbox, and the confidentiality and data-protection protocol required under Section 10.

**Phase 2 — Field Work and Stakeholder Consultations (Week 4–5).** Data collection will run concurrently in Kenya and Malawi through key informant interviews (KIIs) and, where clustering of stakeholder type permits, focus group discussions (FGDs). Responses will be logged on KoboToolbox in real time, using semi-structured interview guides tailored to each stakeholder type and cross-referenced to the evaluation matrix's sub-questions.

**Phase 3 — Analysis and Draft Report (Week 5–6).** Qualitative transcripts will be coded in NVivo against the evaluation matrix nodes (Effectiveness, Relevance, Coherence and Partnership, Efficiency, Impact and Contribution, Sustainability, GESI, Theory of Change assumptions), enabling systematic triangulation across respondent categories and countries. Quantitative participation and outcome data — including disaggregated attendance at convenings, dialogues and AfDB forums — will be analysed in SPSS to produce disaggregated tables by gender, stakeholder type and country. Outcome harvesting statements gathered in the field will be substantiated against documentary evidence and cross-checked with at least one independent corroborating source per claimed outcome, following standard outcome harvesting substantiation practice.

**Phase 4 — Validation and Finalisation (Week 7–8).** Preliminary findings will be presented to Christian Aid and selected KCCWG and CISONECC representatives in a validation session before the report is finalised, satisfying the ToR's requirement that emerging findings be checked against stakeholders' lived understanding of the project prior to sign-off. The final evaluation report, executive summary and learning brief will be delivered in Week 8.

## 3.3 Deliverable-Specific Methods

**Inception Report:** Produced from the Phase 1 document review and preliminary scoping calls with the Christian Aid project lead; contains the full evaluation matrix, KoboToolbox tool set, stakeholder mapping matrix, outcome harvesting protocol and workplan.

**Draft Evaluation Report:** Synthesises the Phase 3 NVivo-coded qualitative findings, SPSS-tabulated participation data, and outcome harvesting statements into findings organised by the ToR's own criteria groupings, with a logframe achievement scorecard as an annex.

**Validation Session:** A facilitated half-day session with Christian Aid and selected KCCWG and CISONECC partners, structured around the draft report's key findings; feedback is logged and tracked to closure in a revision matrix appended to the final report.

**Final Evaluation Report:** Incorporates validation-session feedback, presents evidence-based conclusions against each of the eight evaluation criteria in Section 5.0, and closes with practical, forward-looking recommendations for Christian Aid, KCCWG, CISONECC and wider civil society and faith actors engaging AfDB and other MDBs.

**Executive Summary and Learning Brief:** Distilled from the final report; the learning brief foregrounds advocacy lessons on champions for decentralised renewable energy, engagement modalities value-for-money, and the learning platform's prospects for continuation.

## 3.4 Data Quality Assurance

Cortech will triangulate every substantive finding across at least three sources — document review, KII/FGD testimony, and quantitative participation data — before it is stated as a conclusion. NVivo's matrix-coding query function will be used to cross-tabulate findings by country, stakeholder type and gender, exposing any finding that holds in Kenya but not Malawi, or among CSOs but not faith actors, before it is generalised. SPSS outputs will be independently checked by a second team member prior to inclusion in the draft report. Outcome harvesting statements will be substantiated with named corroborating sources, and any statement that cannot be corroborated will be flagged as unverified rather than presented as an established outcome.

## 3.5 Ethical Considerations Integration

Given that respondents include AfDB officials, government representatives and media actors, Cortech will obtain informed consent from every KII and FGD participant, explaining the evaluation's purpose and intended use of contributions before each session. The inception report will set out, per Section 10.0, how respondent confidentiality will be protected in reporting — including anonymised attribution unless explicit consent for named attribution is given — how KoboToolbox and NVivo data files will be securely stored and access-restricted to the evaluation team, and how findings will be presented in aggregate or anonymised form wherever disclosure could expose an individual respondent. The evaluation will adhere to OECD-DAC/UNEG evaluation ethics norms throughout, and the GESI lens will be applied not only to participation counts but to whether women, youth and off-grid or marginalised community representatives were positioned to influence, rather than merely attend, convenings and dialogues.

## Risk Register

Cortech has carried out evaluation fieldwork across Kenya and, through its wider East and Southern Africa network, understands the operational realities of coordinating multi-country assignments with dispersed civil society, faith actor and government respondents. The risks below reflect the specific character of this evaluation: a two-country assignment (Kenya and Malawi) working through KCCWG and CISONECC, engaging sensitive respondent categories including AfDB officials, government representatives and media actors, and operating within a compressed eight-week window with a mandatory validation gate before finalisation.

Cortech's approach to risk management on this assignment is anticipatory rather than reactive: each risk above is assigned an owner and a checkpoint within the workplan, so that emerging issues — a delayed AfDB response, a government clearance requirement, a gap in the logframe — are identified during document review or early data collection rather than discovered during analysis when correction is costlier in time. The stakeholder mapping matrix, disaggregated by country, stakeholder type and gender, doubles as a risk-tracking tool: it is reviewed at the end of each data collection week to confirm coverage across Kenya and Malawi and across CSO, faith actor, AfDB official, private sector, media and government categories, with substitution respondents drawn in where planned interviews fall through.

Ethical and reputational risk receives particular attention given the sensitivity of respondent categories named in the Terms of Reference. Cortech will apply the confidentiality, secure storage and consent protocols agreed in the inception report consistently across both countries, ensuring that no finding is presented in a way that could expose an individual AfDB official, government representative or media respondent without their explicit agreement. This discipline is treated as a precondition for stakeholder trust and for the credibility of the evaluation's contribution analysis, not as an administrative add-on.

## Work Plan

# 5. Workplan and Timelines

## 5.1 Workplan Narrative

Cortech's workplan is structured around the eight-week indicative timeframe set out in Section 8 of the Terms of Reference (September–October 2026), and is sequenced to preserve the mandatory validation gate specified in Sections 6 and 7 — findings must be checked against Christian Aid's and selected partners' lived understanding before finalisation. The workplan is organised into five phases, each producing a discrete, named deliverable, and each phase is scheduled to allow the Lead Evaluator sufficient time to reconcile Kenya-based and Malawi-based evidence streams before moving to the next stage.

**Phase 1: Contracting, Inception and Document Review (Week 1–3).** Upon signing, Cortech will hold an initial briefing with Christian Aid's project lead and Monitoring and Evaluation lead to confirm reporting lines, access to project documents, and introductions to partners. This runs concurrently with a full document review — the project proposal, logframe, Theory of Change, annual technical and donor reports, research and policy briefs, meeting reports, partner reports from KCCWG and CISONECC, media outputs, and relevant AfDB policy frameworks including Mission 300 Country Compacts and Country Strategy Papers. The document review directly informs the Inception Report, which will present the detailed evaluation matrix linking each of the seven evaluation question groupings in Section 5 — Effectiveness, Relevance, Coherence and partnership, Sustainability and learning, Efficiency, Impact and contribution, Sustainability, and the GESI cross-cutting lens — together with the Theory of Change validation question, to sub-questions, indicators, data sources and methods. The Inception Report will also set out the stakeholder mapping matrix disaggregated by country (Kenya and Malawi), stakeholder type (CSO, faith actor, AfDB official, private sector, media, government) and gender, and the confidentiality, secure storage and anonymisation protocols required under Section 10 given that respondents will include AfDB officials, government representatives and media actors.

**Phase 2: Data Collection and Stakeholder Consultations (Week 4–5).** Cortech will conduct key informant interviews and, where appropriate, focus group discussions with KCCWG in Kenya, CISONECC in Malawi, AfDB-facing champions for decentralised renewable energy, and relevant government departments and institutions named in Section 4. Interviews will run in parallel across the two countries to remain within the five-week data collection window, with the Lead Evaluator conducting priority AfDB-facing and cross-country interviews and a locally engaged researcher supporting Malawi-based fieldwork simultaneously.

**Phase 3: Data Analysis and Draft Report (Week 5–6).** Analysis overlaps by one week with the tail end of data collection to preserve the eight-week total. Outcome harvesting and contribution analysis will be applied to test the Theory of Change assumptions and to isolate the project's contribution to AfDB/MDB policy shifts where attribution is difficult to isolate. Findings will be triangulated against logframe indicators and targets rather than presented as a purely narrative account, producing the Draft Evaluation Report.

**Phase 4: Validation (Week 7).** The Draft Evaluation Report is presented to Christian Aid and selected partners in a validation session, as mandated in Section 7, to test emerging findings and strengthen the account before it is finalised. This is a hard sequencing gate: no final report drafting begins before validation feedback is received.

**Phase 5: Finalisation (Week 8).** Cortech incorporates validation feedback into the Final Evaluation Report, and produces the Executive Summary and short Learning Brief highlighting advocacy lessons and recommendations for future programming.

## 5.2 Parallel vs Sequential Activities

Contracting/briefing and document review run in parallel (Weeks 1–3); Kenya and Malawi data collection run in parallel with each other (Weeks 4–5) but are sequential to document review; analysis begins before data collection fully closes (Week 5 overlap) to protect the reporting timeline; validation (Week 7) and finalisation (Week 8) are strictly sequential, consistent with the ToR's requirement that findings be validated before the report is finalised.
