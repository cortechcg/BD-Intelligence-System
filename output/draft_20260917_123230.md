## Service Management Plan

Service Management Plan

Cortech Consulting Group's service management approach for this assignment is built to keep the Global Community Engagement and Resilience Fund (GCERF) informed and in control of decision points across a compressed ten-week delivery window, while giving the assessment team clear internal accountability for producing the Inception Report, Data Collection Tools, Aide Memoire, Draft Evaluation Report, Final Report, cleaned datasets and the two grant-specific online presentations to the dates fixed in the RFP. The plan below sets out coordination structure, meeting cadence, feedback mechanisms, communication protocols and milestone gates specific to the two-site scope of this evaluation: Grant 1 in Southwest State (Baidoa and Hudur) and Grant 2 in Hirshabelle (Jowhar, Adan Yabaal and Matabaan). Coordination structure and single point of accountability

Salahweli Harun Abdi is proposed as Evaluation Team Lead / Senior Evaluator and will hold day-to-day accountability to GCERF for the technical quality, timeliness and internal coherence of every deliverable across both grants. The Evaluation Team Lead also carries the Data Analyst / Quantitative Specialist and Qualitative Analyst / Research Specialist functions on this assignment, giving GCERF a single technical authority who can move directly between the quantitative beneficiary verification workstream and the qualitative evidence gathered on trust, social cohesion and reconciliation outcomes, rather than requiring handovers between separate analysts. Abdirahman Mohamed Aden is proposed as Data Collection Coordinator / Field Manager, with direct responsibility for enumerator recruitment, training, field supervision, and day-to-day data quality assurance in Baidoa, Hudur, Jowhar, Adan Yabaal and Matabaan, and will also act as the primary point of contact for the enumerator team engaged for the beneficiary verification exercise. This two-tier structure, an evaluation lead accountable to GCERF and a field manager accountable to the evaluation lead, keeps a short reporting line between fieldwork on the ground and the analysis and reporting that GCERF and its grantee partners will use. Communication protocols and meeting cadence with GCERF

Cortech will hold a virtual inception meeting with GCERF within the first week following Award of Contract, to confirm the review and approval process for the Inception Report, agree the format for the two grant-specific online presentations, and request early sight of the grant Theories of Change, results frameworks, targets, beneficiary lists, endline reports, final workplans and Annex 1 programme descriptions referenced in the RFP as available to the selected consultant prior to inception. From that point, the team will maintain fortnightly check-in calls with the GCERF focal point through the fieldwork and analysis phases (early to mid November through late December 2026), stepping up to weekly calls during the fieldwork window (10 November to 1 December 2026) when data quality issues are most likely to surface and require rapid GCERF sign-off on any adjustment to the sampling frame or tool design. All formal deliverables will be transmitted by email with a short cover note flagging any deviations from the agreed workplan, open risks, or clarification needed from GCERF or the grantee partners. A shared project folder will hold the evaluation matrix, sampling frame, data collection tools, field tracking sheets and version-controlled drafts of the Inception Report, Aide Memoire and Final Report, giving GCERF continuous visibility into progress rather than only seeing finished deliverables. Feedback mechanism and quality assurance gates

Each of the six deliverable milestones carries a defined review step before Cortech proceeds to the next phase of work. The Inception Report, including the evaluation matrix, sampling strategy, workplan, ethical safeguards and outline of the Final Report, will be submitted as a complete package for GCERF review and comment, with Cortech incorporating GCERF's feedback into a revised methodology before fieldwork begins, consistent with the RFP's expectation that the technical approach presented now will be refined once full grant documentation is available. The Aide Memoire, submitted within one week of completing fieldwork, is treated as a formal checkpoint at which GCERF can flag data quality concerns or request additional verification before the team commits to drafting the Final Report, reducing the risk of late-stage rework. The Draft Evaluation Report will be shared with a defined comment period before finalisation, and Cortech will maintain a tracked-changes log showing how each GCERF and grantee partner comment was addressed in the Final Report. Cross-site coordination

Because Grant 1 fieldwork in Southwest State and Grant 2 fieldwork in Hirshabelle will run in parallel to meet the 1 December 2026 data collection deadline, the Field Manager will maintain separate but synchronised field trackers for each site, reporting consolidated progress against the sampling frame to the Evaluation Team Lead at the end of each fieldwork week. Any issue likely to affect a deliverable date, data quality, or beneficiary access, including security-related access constraints in Baidoa, Hudur, Jowhar, Adan Yabaal or Matabaan, will be raised by the Field Manager to the Evaluation Team Lead within twenty-four hours of identification, and by the Evaluation Team Lead to the GCERF focal point at the next scheduled call or, where the issue is time-critical, by direct email the same day. This ensures GCERF is never informed of a risk to the timeline or to data integrity after the fact, and can exercise its own decision authority over any change to scope, sampling, or verification modality while the assignment is underway.

| Medium | High | Field movements will be coordinated through locally based enumerators with current knowledge of access conditions in each of the five named locations.
Fieldwork schedules will build in flexible sequencing between Baidoa/Hudur and Jowhar/Adan Yabaal/Matabaan so that a delay in one site does not stall the overall data collection finalisation date.
Where in-person access to a specific location is constrained on a given day, the beneficiary verification protocol will shift that stratum to phone-based verification for the affected respondent group without altering the overall 10% sampling frame.
|
| Data integrity and beneficiary counting | Grantee-reported beneficiary lists across the two grants (888 for Grant 1, 4,180 for Grant 2) may contain duplication, incomplete contact details, or inconsistent recording of how beneficiaries were counted, undermining the Data Integrity assessment.
| Medium | High | The evaluation matrix will include a data audit step at inception: cross-checking grantee beneficiary lists against reported totals per outcome area, flagging duplicate entries, missing disaggregation (sex/age, location, support type), and gaps in counting method documentation before the sampling frame for beneficiary verification is finalised.
Discrepancies and their likely cause will be recorded transparently in the Inception Report and carried into the aide memoire.
|
| Government and stakeholder engagement | Local government officials targeted under Grant 1's Outcome C (160 officials, plus 240 joint engagement participants) are spread across Baidoa and Hudur and may have limited availability for interviews within the fieldwork window, particularly given competing administrative duties.
| Medium | Medium | Stakeholder mapping and scheduling will begin during the inception phase, using existing GCERF and grantee partner contact lists to confirm official availability windows ahead of deployment.
Interviews with local government officials will be sequenced early within each site visit to avoid loss of access if officials' availability changes later in the fieldwork period.
|
| Community participation and disclosure | Vulnerable individuals (unemployed youth, women, former Al-Shabaab members, IDPs, minority clans) and trauma-recovery participants in Hirshabelle may be reluctant to disclose sex, age, location, or participation details due to stigma, security concerns, or distrust of external verification calls.
| Medium | High | Verification scripts will explain the independent, non-punitive purpose of the call before any question is asked, and enumerators will be trained to skip or soften sensitive fields (sex/age) where disclosure risks the respondent's safety, consistent with the RFP's "where safe and appropriate" instruction.
Somali-speaking enumerators familiar with the target communities will lead all contact with former combatants and IDP respondents to reduce distrust.
|
| Timeline, sequencing and approval gates | The schedule from Award of Contract (1 November 2026) to the Final Report (8 January 2027) allows under ten weeks for inception, fieldwork across two states, analysis, reporting and dataset delivery, with GCERF review of the Inception Report acting as a gate before fieldwork can proceed.

| Low | Medium | Enumerator recruitment for Southwest State and Hirshabelle will be finalised early in the inception period so that field supervision does not depend solely on the Field Manager's continuous presence in both sites.
Analysis tasks will be phased so that quantitative cleaning and qualitative coding do not compete for the same team member's time during the fieldwork-to-aide-memoire window.

Risk management for this assignment is treated as continuous rather than a one-off assessment completed at inception.
Each risk above is owned by a named function within the team (Evaluation Team Lead for data integrity, methodological and ethical risk; Field Manager for access, security and enumerator-related risk), reviewed at the coordination points set out in the Service Management Plan, and updated as conditions in Baidoa, Hudur, Jowhar, Adan Yabaal or Matabaan change.
Any risk that escalates to affect a deliverable date will be flagged to GCERF immediately rather than surfaced only at the next scheduled check-in, consistent with the tight sequence from Inception Report through to the Final Report and the two grant-specific online presentations.
The register above is deliberately weighted toward the risks most likely to materialise in this specific evaluation: access constraints across two geographically separated grant sites, the data integrity questions the RFP itself raises about beneficiary counting, and the safeguarding exposure inherent in interviewing former combatants, IDPs, minority clans and trauma-recovery participants.
Generic logistical or reputational risks that do not bear on Grant 1 or Grant 2's actual respondent groups have been excluded so that the plan remains an operational tool for this assignment rather than a boilerplate checklist.

Assumptions (optional)

These assumptions will be tested and, where necessary, revised in consultation with GCERF during the Inception Report phase, once the grant Theories of Change, results frameworks, targets, beneficiary lists, endline reports, final workplans, and Annex 1 programme descriptions referenced in the RFP are made available to the assessment team. 1. Timely access to grant documentation. It is assumed that GCERF and the two grantee partners implementing Grant 1 (Southwest State) and Grant 2 (Hirshabelle) will provide the assessment team with the grant Theories of Change, results frameworks, disaggregated beneficiary lists, prior monitoring data, and final workplans within the first five working days following Award of Contract (1 November 2026), so that the evaluation matrix and beneficiary verification sampling frame presented at Inception Report stage (due 10 November 2026) can be finalised against verified rather than provisional beneficiary totals. 2. Reliability of reported beneficiary totals as the sampling universe. The stratified beneficiary verification sample of approximately 10% of reported beneficiaries is designed against the totals stated in the RFP's grant overview (Grant 1: 888 across peace committee members, elders and local leaders, reconciliation/integration participants, youth bloggers, vulnerable individuals, local government officials, and joint engagement participants; Grant 2: 4,180 across Women and Youth Leaders, community member participants, trauma-recovery participants, media outreach audience, community dialogue participants, and livelihoods beneficiaries). It is assumed these totals, and the underlying beneficiary lists to be supplied, are sufficiently complete to serve as the sampling frame; where the assessment team's data integrity review identifies material gaps or duplication in either grantee's beneficiary database, the sampling frame will be adjusted and the change documented transparently in the Inception Report and, if it arises later, flagged in the Aide Memoire. 3. Feasibility of remote and hybrid verification modalities. It is assumed that a majority of beneficiaries in Southwest State (Baidoa, Hudur) and Hirshabelle (Jowhar, Adan Yabaal, Matabaan) can be reached by phone using contact details held in the grantees' beneficiary lists, and that in-person verification will remain feasible in accessible locations within Baidoa and Jowhar town centres. Where security conditions restrict direct access to Hudur, Adan Yabaal, or Matabaan at the time of fieldwork, the assessment team assumes it can substitute phone-based or remote verification without materially reducing the reliability of the approximately 10% beneficiary verification sample, and will document any such substitution and its effect on representativeness in the Final Report. 4. Continued availability and cooperation of grantee focal points. It is assumed that both grantee organisations implementing Grant 1 and Grant 2 will designate a focal point available to the assessment team throughout the fieldwork window (up to 1 December 2026) to facilitate beneficiary list verification, arrange access to peace committee members, elders and local leaders, and support scheduling of key informant interviews with local government officials, without requiring the assessment team to independently rebuild contact networks from the ground up. 5. No material change to the security environment. The proposed sequencing and modality mix for Grant 1 (Southwest State) and Grant 2 (Hirshabelle) fieldwork assumes no significant deterioration in the security situation in Baidoa, Hudur, Jowhar, Adan Yabaal, or Matabaan between contract award and completion of raw data collection (1 December 2026) beyond conditions typical of these areas. Availability of matched team members.
