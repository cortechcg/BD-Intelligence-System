## Introduction, Background & Conceptual Framework

5.1 Context and Strategic Importance

The Towards Ending Drought Emergencies (TWENDE) project, Ecosystem-based Adaptation in Kenya's Arid and Semi-Arid Rangelands, is a five-year Green Climate Fund (GCF) initiative implemented by the IUCN Eastern and Southern Africa Regional Office (ESARO) as GCF Accredited Entity, in partnership with the Government of Kenya and Conservation International (CI), across the Sabarwawa, Mid Tana and Chyulu Hills landscapes covering 11 counties. As the project (Reference P02886) approaches completion, the significant investments made under Component 1 (Climate Change Adapted Planning for Drought Resilience), Component 2 (Restoration of Rangeland Landscapes for Ecosystem-based Adaptation) and Component 3 (Climate-resilient Ecosystem Management Investments) in grazing committees, peace committees and other community rangeland governance mechanisms require an independent, evidence-based accounting of what they have achieved and what must be institutionalised for the gains to outlast the project. This consultancy sits at a strategic inflection point for the National Drought Management Authority (NDMA) and the TWENDE Chief of Party, who must decide, in the limited window remaining, which governance structures, county planning entry points and policy opportunities to prioritise for institutional recognition, continuity and sustainability. 5.2 Purpose and Objectives

The purpose of this consultancy, as stated in the ToR, is to provide strategic technical and analytical support to NDMA to strengthen the effectiveness, institutional sustainability and policy uptake of TWENDE investments in community rangeland governance and drought resilience. This purpose is operationalised through seven specific objectives: assessing the functionality and effectiveness of community rangeland governance institutions; identifying opportunities for their integration into county governance structures; identifying opportunities for community rangeland and restoration plan priorities to be reflected in county planning and budgeting processes, including Annual Development Plans (ADPs) and pre-County Integrated Development Plans (pre-CIDPs); assessing barriers to pastoralists' access to and use of climate information; identifying policy influencing opportunities for the next 12 months; documenting actual and expected governance impacts on resource management and conflict reduction; and producing prioritised recommendations for the remaining project period. 5.3 Our Interpretation of the Assignment

Cortech interprets this assignment as a diagnostic-to-action exercise rather than a standard institutional review: the client is not asking whether grazing committees and peace committees exist, but whether they hold real decision-making authority, resolve natural resource-related conflicts, and can be absorbed into county governance systems and county planning instruments before TWENDE closes. A constraint the ToR implies but does not state is that 45 days across 11 counties and three landscapes makes exhaustive county-by-county coverage unachievable; the assignment therefore requires a purposive, landscape-representative sampling logic, verified with NDMA at inception, rather than an attempt to visit every county with equal depth. A second implication is that because TWENDE is still operating, the assignment must document a mixture of realised and plausible expected outcomes, which places a theory of change, not a simple before-after comparison, at the centre of the effectiveness assessment and the impact story. 5.4 Key Evaluation Questions

Drawing directly from the ToR's specific objectives and scope areas 6.1 to 6.6, the assignment is organised around the following evaluation questions:

1. How functional and effective are the community rangeland governance institutions established under TWENDE, in terms of institutional arrangements, roles, decision-making processes and linkages with relevant actors, and what is their contribution to improved resource management, sustainable grazing management, reduced livestock pressure on rangelands, restoration of degraded landscapes and reduced natural resource-related conflicts? 2. What opportunities exist for integrating existing community rangeland governance structures into relevant county governance structures and systems, to strengthen their institutional recognition, continuity and sustainability beyond the project period? 3. To what extent are priorities emerging from community rangeland and restoration plans reflected in, and able to inform, county planning and budgeting processes, including ADPs, pre-CIDPs and other appropriate county planning instruments? 4. What are the key barriers to pastoralists' access to and use of climate information, and what innovative opportunities, including digital delivery mechanisms, exist for improving accessibility and uptake? 5. What strategic opportunities for policy influencing exist within the next 12 months, particularly for integrating TWENDE-generated community plans, evidence and lessons into relevant county and national policies, strategies and planning processes? 6. What is the actual and/or expected impact of governance interventions on resource management and conflict reduction, and what evidence of change or plausible expected pathways can be documented where measurable outcomes are not yet fully realised? 7. What prioritised recommendations and strategic actions can be pursued during the remaining project period to strengthen the impact, institutionalisation and sustainability of TWENDE investments? 5.5 Deliverables

SECTION 2, CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK

Cortech will apply a two-tiered analytical lens combining an institutional functionality framework with a theory of change, selected because the ToR asks simultaneously for a backward-looking assessment of what has worked and a forward-looking account of what can be institutionalised and influenced within a fixed 12-month horizon. The institutional functionality framework will score each community rangeland governance structure, grazing committees, peace committees and related mechanisms, against three dimensions drawn directly from the ToR's own language: decision-making authority (the extent to which the structure can make and enforce grazing and resource-use decisions), linkage to county structures (the degree of institutional recognition and coordination with relevant county governance systems), and conflict-resolution track record (documented instances of preventing or resolving natural resource-related conflicts). Scoring each structure consistently across the Sabarwawa, Mid Tana and Chyulu Hills landscapes allows the Community Rangeland Governance Effectiveness and Outcomes Assessment Report to compare institutional performance across landscapes rather than presenting an undifferentiated narrative. Running alongside this, a theory of change will trace the causal pathway from governance interventions (formation and strengthening of committees, development of community rangeland and restoration plans) through intermediate outcomes (improved grazing management, reduced livestock pressure, restoration uptake) to higher-level outcomes (drought resilience, reduced resource-use conflict). This framework is built into the assignment from the outset rather than reconstructed at reporting stage, so that every finding can be tagged by evidence type, that is, whether it reflects a measured outcome or a plausible expected outcome supported by documented implementation evidence. This tagging discipline is what the two-page impact story requires, since the ToR is explicit that both categories must be reported with their supporting evidence made clear. The same framework anchors the County Institutional Integration and Planning/Budgeting Analysis and the 12-Month Policy Influencing and Uptake Strategy, since institutional linkage findings from the functionality assessment feed directly into identifying which county planning instruments and which policy windows, aligned with Kenya's Vision 2030 and the Ending Drought Emergencies (EDE) strategy, offer realistic entry points within the stated 12-month horizon.

## Technical Methodology

METHODOLOGY

1. Overall Approach

This methodology is built to answer a single strategic question that runs through all seven specific objectives of the Terms of Reference: which of the community rangeland governance structures established under the Towards Ending Drought Emergencies (TWENDE) project across the Sabarwawa, Mid Tana and Chyulu Hills landscapes are functioning well enough, and are institutionally anchored well enough, to be carried by county governance systems and county planning instruments beyond the project's five-year lifespan. Cortech Consulting Group treats this as an institutionalisation assessment rather than a standard end-of-project evaluation. The distinction matters because the National Drought Management Authority (NDMA), the TWENDE Chief of Party and IUCN/TWENDE technical teams need decision-grade evidence on continuity and policy uptake, not a retrospective account of activities delivered. The approach is organised around an explicit theory of change that links three levels of intervention logic: TWENDE's institutional investment in grazing committees, peace committees and related governance mechanisms (input); the functioning of those structures in decision-making, sustainable grazing management and rangeland restoration and their record on resolving natural resource-related conflicts (outcome); and their integration into county governance systems, Annual Development Plans (ADPs), pre-County Integrated Development Plans (pre-CIDPs) and county and national policy processes (institutionalisation and impact). This theory of change is drafted during the Inception phase, tested against evidence gathered in the field, and used consistently across the Community Rangeland Governance Effectiveness and Outcomes Assessment Report, the two-page impact story and the Final Consultancy Report, so that the distinction between measured outcomes and plausible expected outcomes is applied the same way in every deliverable rather than reconstructed at the reporting stage. The methodology combines quantitative and qualitative instruments deployed in parallel rather than sequentially, because the 45-day assignment window and the four fixed deadlines (Inception Report, 9 October 2026; Community Rangeland Governance Effectiveness and Outcomes Assessment Report, 28 October 2026; County Institutional Integration and Planning/Budgeting Analysis, 4 November 2026; Final Consultancy Report, 10 November 2026) do not allow a strictly linear design. Institutional mapping of governance structures, the climate information access diagnostic, and the county planning-instrument entry-point analysis are therefore run as concurrent workstreams within a single field mission across the three landscapes, converging at defined analysis and validation points with NDMA. 2. Phase-by-Phase Breakdown

Phase 1: Inception (Days 1 to 9). Team Leader Amina Aden Maalim leads a structured desk review of TWENDE Component 1 (Climate Change Adapted Planning for Drought Resilience), Component 2 (Restoration of Rangeland Landscapes for Ecosystem-based Adaptation) and Component 3 (Climate-resilient Ecosystem Management Investments) documentation, the existing community rangeland and restoration plans, and available county planning calendars for the 11 target counties [NOT VERIFIED]. This phase produces the Inception Report due 9 October 2026, containing the finalised theory of change, the institutional functionality framework for scoring grazing committees and peace committees, the KoboToolbox data collection instruments, and a day-by-day work plan sequencing all eight deliverables against the four fixed deadlines. A kick-off validation call with the TWENDE Chief of Party and NDMA is held before finalisation to confirm the institutional functionality indicators and the county selection logic within each of the three landscapes. Phase 2: Field Work (Days 10 to 27). Data collection is conducted across a purposive sample of counties drawn from Sabarwawa, Mid Tana and Chyulu Hills, using key informant interviews (KIIs) with county governance officials, NDMA county coordinators and TWENDE field staff, focus group discussions (FGDs) with grazing committee and peace committee members, and structured household surveys with pastoralists on climate information access and use. All instruments are administered on KoboToolbox for real-time data capture and quality checks. A parallel climate information access diagnostic is conducted during the same field mission using the same enumeration teams, avoiding a second mobilisation. Phase 3: Analysis (Days 28 to 36). Quantitative survey data is cleaned and analysed in SPSS to generate functionality scores for governance structures and climate information access indicators disaggregated by landscape and county. Qualitative data from KIIs and FGDs is coded in NVivo against the institutional functionality framework, the conflict-reduction evidence categories, and the county planning entry-point typology. Spatial patterns in governance structure coverage and restoration activity across the three landscapes are mapped in QGIS to support the county institutional integration analysis. Findings are triangulated across instruments before drafting begins. Phase 4: Reporting and Validation (Days 37 to 45). Draft deliverables are shared with NDMA and the TWENDE Chief of Party for review ahead of finalisation, with a validation workshop scheduled before the County Institutional Integration and Planning/Budgeting Analysis is finalised on 4 November 2026, so that NDMA co-owns the county entry-point findings that feed the 12-Month Policy Influencing and Uptake Strategy. All reports are delivered in both editable Word or Excel format and PDF, as required. 3. Method by Deliverable

Inception Report (9 October 2026): desk review and stakeholder scoping produce the theory of change, work plan and data collection instruments; respondents at this stage are limited to the TWENDE Chief of Party and NDMA focal points for validation. Community Rangeland Governance Effectiveness and Outcomes Assessment Report (28 October 2026): institutional functionality scoring applied to grazing committees and peace committees through FGDs with committee members and KIIs with county natural resource officers, scored against decision-making authority, linkage to county structures, and conflict-resolution track record; output is a landscape-by-landscape effectiveness matrix analysed in SPSS and NVivo [NOT VERIFIED]. Two-page Impact Story, "The Impact of Governance Interventions on Resource Management and Conflict Reduction" (concurrent with the effectiveness assessment): built from the same theory of change, structured to separate measured outcomes (evidenced by KII and FGD testimony and, where available, resource-use or conflict-incidence data) from expected outcomes (evidenced by stated trajectories from committee members and county officials), with each claim tagged by evidence type rather than presented as a single narrative. Climate Information Access and Innovation Report: household-level KoboToolbox survey with pastoralists across the three landscapes on accessibility, relevance and timeliness of climate information, paired with KIIs on existing and potential digital delivery mechanisms; output addresses both diagnosis of barriers and forward-looking delivery innovations, avoiding a barriers-only treatment. County Institutional Integration and Planning/Budgeting Analysis (4 November 2026): mapping of ADPs and pre-CIDPs and county budgeting cycles for the counties visited, conducted through KIIs with county planning and finance officers, identifying the specific calendar windows in which community rangeland and restoration plan priorities can be inserted; validated with NDMA before finalisation. 12-Month Policy Influencing and Uptake Strategy: forward-dated policy-window analysis anchored to Kenya's Vision 2030 and the Ending Drought Emergencies (EDE) strategy, identifying institutional entry points and stakeholder interfaces at county and national level for the twelve months following assignment completion. Strategic Recommendations and Action Plan: consolidated recommendations mapped explicitly to each of the seven specific objectives and the six scope areas (6.1 to 6.6), prioritised for actions realistically achievable during the remaining TWENDE project period. Final Consultancy Report (10 November 2026): synthesis of all preceding deliverables into a single findings, conclusions and recommendations document for NDMA, IUCN and the TWENDE Chief of Party. 4. Data Quality Assurance

All KoboToolbox instruments are piloted with a small number of respondents in one landscape before full deployment, with skip logic and validation checks built in to prevent incomplete or inconsistent entries. Data is reviewed daily during field work for outliers and enumerator consistency, and SPSS datasets are cleaned and range-checked before analysis. NVivo coding is applied using a shared codebook derived from the institutional functionality framework, with a second-pass review by the Team Leader to check coding consistency across counties. Findings are triangulated across quantitative survey data, KII and FGD transcripts, and available secondary documentation from TWENDE Component 1, 2 and 3 records before being written into any deliverable, and draft findings are validated with NDMA and the TWENDE Chief of Party ahead of finalisation of the County Institutional Integration and Planning/Budgeting Analysis and the Final Consultancy Report. 5. Ethical Considerations

Informed consent is obtained from all respondents, including grazing committee and peace committee members, pastoralists and county governance officials, before any KII, FGD or household survey, with consent recorded digitally through KoboToolbox. Given that natural resource-related conflicts and resource-use conflicts are part of the assessment scope, FGD facilitation is structured to avoid reopening active disputes and to protect the confidentiality of individuals who disclose conflict-related information. Data is stored on password-protected devices and anonymised at the point of analysis, with disaggregation limited to landscape and county level in reported outputs. ## Sampling Strategy & Data Analysis Plan

**Sampling and Selection Logic**

This assignment is an institutional and policy assessment, not a household-level impact survey, and the sample frame is therefore purposive rather than probabilistic. Participants for key informant interviews (KIIs) and focus group discussions (FGDs) will be selected deliberately to capture the full range of community rangeland governance structures, functionality levels and institutional positions across the Sabarwawa, Mid Tana and Chyulu Hills landscapes, rather than drawn at random to achieve statistical representativeness. Selection criteria will include: landscape and county coverage (ensuring at least two counties per landscape are represented within the 45-day window), governance structure type (grazing committees, peace committees and other TWENDE-supported mechanisms), functionality status (high-, medium- and low-performing structures, identified in consultation with NDMA and IUCN/TWENDE technical teams during the inception phase), and institutional vantage point (community leadership, county planning and budgeting officers, NDMA county coordinators, and, where relevant, climate information intermediaries). This purposive approach is appropriate because the seven specific objectives ask for depth of understanding of institutional functionality, policy entry points and causal pathways, not population-level prevalence estimates, and because the assignment duration does not permit a probability-based household survey across 11 counties. Where numeric scoring is applied (for example, institutional functionality indices), the resulting dataset will be a purposively constructed institutional census of sampled structures, not a random sample of the pastoralist population, and will be analysed and reported as such. **SECTION 2. DATA ANALYSIS PLAN**

Quantitative data will consist principally of the institutional functionality scores generated during the rapid institutional mapping of grazing committees, peace committees and related mechanisms, scored against decision-making authority, linkage to county structures and conflict-resolution track record, together with any structured climate information access indicators collected during pastoralist consultations. These datasets will be cleaned, coded and entered into SPSS, with descriptive statistics (frequencies, means, cross-tabulations by landscape, county and governance structure type) used to characterise functionality patterns across Sabarwawa, Mid Tana and Chyulu Hills. Where sample sizes and distributional assumptions permit, inferential tests (chi-square tests of association, and comparison of means across landscapes or structure types) will be used to test whether functionality or climate information access differs systematically by landscape, county or governance structure type. Excel will support data organisation and the production of summary tables for the Community Rangeland Governance Effectiveness and Outcomes Assessment Report and the Climate Information Access and Innovation Report. Qualitative data from KIIs, FGDs and validation workshops with NDMA and the TWENDE Chief of Party will be transcribed, coded and analysed in NVivo using a thematic framework built directly from the seven specific objectives and scope areas 6.1 to 6.6, with a priori nodes for institutional functionality, county integration pathways, ADP and pre-CIDP entry points, climate information barriers and delivery innovations, policy windows, and governance impact narratives. Thematic analysis will distinguish measured outcomes from plausible expected outcomes, tagging each coded excerpt with an evidence type (observed change, stakeholder-reported change, or anticipated change) to support the two-page impact story's evidentiary structure. Triangulation will occur at two points: first, functionality scores from SPSS will be cross-referenced against NVivo-coded qualitative explanations for why specific structures score as they do, so quantitative patterns are explained rather than merely reported; second, findings from both strands will be jointly tested against county-level planning-instrument evidence (ADPs, pre-CIDPs) and validated with NDMA at the scheduled milestone workshop before the County Institutional Integration and Planning/Budgeting Analysis is finalised.

SECTION 2, ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS AND SAFEGUARDING

Cortech Consulting Group's ethical approach for this assignment operates under the firm's ISO-certified quality management system, its child safeguarding policy and its PSEA (prevention of sexual exploitation and abuse) policy compliance, applied to the Team Leader, any technical specialists and all enumerators engaged across the Sabarwawa, Mid Tana and Chyulu Hills landscapes. The ToR does not prescribe a specific ethical clearance procedure or safeguarding protocol for this consultancy. In the absence of a stated requirement, Cortech applies its standard research ethics protocol to all engagement with pastoral communities, grazing committees, peace committees and county governance officials across the 11 counties. This includes voluntary participation, informed consent obtained (written or verbal, depending on literacy and local context) before every interview, focus group discussion or validation workshop, and a clear prior explanation of the study's purpose, expected duration, confidentiality arrangements and the respondent's right to decline or withdraw at any stage without consequence. Because community rangeland governance structures include grazing committees and peace committees whose membership spans women, elders and youth directly involved in managing natural resource-related conflicts, particular care is taken in consultations touching on resource-use disputes and conflict history. Such interviews will be conducted in private settings, and no individually identifying account of a conflict incident will be attributed in the Community Rangeland Governance Effectiveness and Outcomes Assessment Report or the two-page impact story without the respondent's explicit consent, to avoid exposing participants to reprisal or social risk. Data protection follows Cortech's standard protocol: household- and institution-level data collected through KoboToolbox is stored on password-protected, access-controlled systems, anonymised at the point of analysis, and retained only for the period necessary to complete the Final Consultancy Report. Do-no-harm principles inform fieldwork sequencing: consultations with pastoral communities and county officials across the 11 counties are timed around grazing calendars and county budgeting cycles rather than imposed on them, and findings shared with NDMA and the TWENDE Chief of Party are presented in a manner that does not compromise the institutional standing of individual grazing or peace committees whose functionality is under assessment.
