### understanding — from Technical Proposal Cortech to DRC.docx

The ABLI-G II Project has been designed as a market-driven livelihoods intervention responding to the increasingly complex interaction between prolonged displacement, chronic unemployment, limited access to finance, weak market systems, and systemic barriers affecting refugees and host communities in Dadaab, Garissa County. The Dadaab refugee complex continues to host one of the world's largest and most protracted refugee populations, with significant humanitarian, development, and peacebuilding implications. According to the World Bank, refugees in Kenya remain economically vulnerable, with the majority relying on humanitarian assistance as their primary livelihood coping mechanism. In Dadaab, only a third of respondents reported having access to an income, and 70 percent of households stated that reliance on humanitarian aid was their primary livelihood coping mechanism (REACH & NRC 2018). Host communities are also economically vulnerable, with Garissa County poverty standing at 73 percent and the unemployment rate at 28.4 percent. The county has the highest number of people who have never attended school (62.2 percent). The project adopts a Market Systems Development (MSD) approach recognising that sustainable improvements in livelihoods and self-reliance cannot be achieved through isolated, direct-service interventions alone. Instead, resilience and self-reliance require simultaneous investments in skills development, business development, agricultural development, market linkages, financial inclusion, and systemic change in the enabling environment.
