### analysis — from TECHNICAL PROPOSAL HI Kenya.docx

The study will adopt a purposive and stratified sampling strategy to ensure representation of the diverse stakeholders within Kenya’s agritech ecosystem. Given the study’s focus on youth-led agritech innovation, purposive sampling will be applied to identify and engage actors who play a pivotal role in the ecosystem, including startup founders, accelerator managers, government officials, academic representatives, investors, and donor agencies. This approach is necessary because such respondents hold specialized knowledge that is central to understanding the ecosystem dynamics. To complement this, stratification will be applied across different categories of actors namely implementers, funders, academic institutions, startups, and government agencies, so that no critical group is overlooked. For startups and youth-led agribusinesses, we will use a snowball sampling technique, beginning with well-documented and established agritech innovators from AYuTe networks and other known directories, then expanding outward to capture emerging or lesser-known actors through referrals. This ensures that both highly visible and grassroots-level innovators are represented. The sampling frame will therefore include: (i) youth-led agritech startups at varying stages of maturity, (ii) innovation hubs, accelerators, and incubators, (iii) donor and venture funding entities, (iv) relevant ministries and regulatory agencies, and (v) universities and technical training institutions engaged in agritech.
