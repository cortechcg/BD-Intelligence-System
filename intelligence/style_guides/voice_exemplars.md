A mixed sampling approach will be used and will combine stratified random sampling (quantitative data collection) and purposive sampling (qualitative data collection). This approach will ensure statistical validity for household surveys, contextual richness for qualitative tools such as FGDs and KIIs, inclusion across key strata (such as gender, age, vulnerability status, geographic location (rural/remote)) and participation in different project components (cash transfers, school feeding, WASH). For the quantitative component, random sampling will be used within the strata to ensure statistical representativeness. The sample size for household surveys will be calculated based on the total number of direct beneficiaries in Kenya and Ethiopia, using a 95% confidence interval and a 5% margin of error, subject to feasibility and logistical constraints. The final sample size will be agreed upon in consultation with PACIDA and Caritas Germany during the inception phase. Kilifi and Tana River Counties lie in Kenya’s arid and semi-arid lands, where erratic bimodal rains (300–700 mm annually) alternate with prolonged droughts and flash floods. Land degradation—from soil erosion to deforestation—threatens biodiversity in riverine forests and mangroves. Over 70 percent of households live below the poverty line, depending on seasonal crop cultivation and pastoralism; livestock assets and off-farm incomes are critical buffers but remain highly vulnerable to climate shocks. Safe water coverage is under 40 percent, forcing women and girls to walk kilometers daily and exposing them to safety risks. Local water user associations exist but function weakly, and no formal committees manage rehabilitated systems. Nutrition suffers: nearly one-third of under-five children are stunted, and fewer than 10 percent of women of reproductive age meet minimum dietary diversity. Yet, abundant indigenous foods—wild fruits, baobab, traditional greens—offer opportunities to enrich diets if integrated into community outreach and value chains.
